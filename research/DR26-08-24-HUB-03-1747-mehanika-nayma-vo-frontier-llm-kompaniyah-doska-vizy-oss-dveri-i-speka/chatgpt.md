# Block 1 — Does the public job board matter?

## What is actually measured

FACT — Ashby’s cross-company dataset found that nearly 94% of applications were inbound, but inbound applicants had only a 3% interview rate, versus approximately 40% for referrals and internal applicants. Referral application-to-offer conversion reached 7.3% in Q4 2024 versus 0.2% for inbound; internal candidates reached 17.9%. These are application-level conversions, not the percentage of DevRel hires at frontier labs. ([Ashby, 16 May 2025](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals))

## Best defensible conclusion

ESTIMATE — For these labs, the public board probably accounts for most hires as a recorded application source, but it does not follow that the board created the opportunity. A sourced or referred candidate can still be recorded in the ATS and complete the same official process.

ESTIMATE — A realistic planning assumption is:

- Expect roughly two months from opening to fill based on the Greenhouse cross-industry median proxy.
- Apply in the first 24–72 hours where possible.
- Pair the application with one artifact-aware internal contact.
- Do not interpret a role’s continuing presence as proof that no preferred candidate exists.
- Do not skip a good role because “they only hire through referrals”; the measured data does not support that folklore.

## US routes

### O-1A

FACT — O-1A requires either a major internationally recognized award or evidence satisfying at least three of eight regulatory categories; USCIS then evaluates the quality and totality of the evidence, so merely checking three boxes does not establish eligibility. ([USCIS decision explaining the standard, observed 2026](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/err/D8%20-%20Aliens%20of%20Extraordinary%20Ability%20or%20Achievement%20%28O-1%2C%20O-2%2C%20and%20O-3%29/Decisions_Issued_in_2019/DEC182019_01D8101.pdf); [USCIS STEM policy update, 8 January 2025](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/policy-manual-updates/20250108-ExtraordinaryAbility.pdf))

FACT — An O-1 petition must be filed by a US employer or agent; the beneficiary cannot simply self-petition. ([USCIS employment guide](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/USCIS/About%20Us/Electronic%20Reading%20Room/Applicant%20Service%20Reference%20Guide/Nonimmigrant_Empl.pdf))

ESTIMATE — This is the fastest plausible US path for the supplied profile. The useful evidence is not the PhD alone but a coherent package of scholarly authorship, judging others’ work, major/significant original contribution, essential roles, press or independent recognition, and high remuneration. An h-index of 7 and roughly 137 citations helps but is not independently decisive.

FACT — Premium processing requires USCIS to take adjudicative action within 15 business days; this can be an approval, denial, request for evidence or other action, not necessarily final approval. ([USCIS premium-processing rule](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/policy-manual-updates/20200902-FeeRule.pdf))

FACT — From 1 March 2026, the inflation-adjusted premium-processing fee is $2,965. The regular I-129 O fee is $1,055 for a standard employer or $530 for an eligible small employer/nonprofit, with a separate Asylum Program Fee depending on employer status; the petition-based visa application fee is $205. ([2026 Federal Register fee adjustment](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-00321.pdf); [USCIS G-1055 fee schedule, observed 24 August 2026](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/g-1055.pdf); [State Department fees](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html))

ESTIMATE — Allow four to eight weeks to prepare a serious petition, 15 business days for premium USCIS action, and roughly one to eight additional weeks for appointment/visa issuance. End-to-end: approximately two to four months when the evidence is ready and no RFE or appointment backlog intervenes.

ESTIMATE — Attorney pricing is commonly about $5,500–$15,000, with higher figures for complex or boutique cases; this is market pricing, not a government fee. ([Headman Law, 8 June 2026](https://headmanlaw.com/insights/immigration-lawyer-cost-2026/); [TalVisa, 28 July 2026](https://www.talvisa.com/blog/choosing-best-immigration-lawyer))

([State Department employment immigration guide, observed 24 August 2026](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/employment-based-immigrant-visas.html); [USCIS Policy Manual](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-a-chapter-8))

FACT — EB-1A similarly uses a major-award or at-least-three-criteria threshold followed by a final-merits assessment. ([USCIS EB-1 decision explaining the framework](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/err/B5%20-%20Members%20of%20the%20Professions%20holding%20Advanced%20Degrees%20or%20Aliens%20of%20Exceptional%20Ability/Decisions_Issued_in_2023/JUL032023_02B5203.pdf))

ESTIMATE — EB-1A is the most robust route because it yields permanent residence and is not tied to one employer, but the evidentiary bar is higher than O-1A. The current record sounds potentially buildable, not safely approval-ready: citations are modest for a standalone scientific-contribution claim, and the missing evidence is independent proof that the OSS/governance work materially affected the field.

FACT — The I-140 filing fee is $715, plus the applicable Asylum Program Fee; an individual self-petitioner generally pays the reduced $300 Asylum Program Fee. Premium processing is an additional $2,965 from 1 March 2026, and the immigrant-visa fee is $345. ([USCIS fee schedule](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/g-1055_2.pdf); [2026 premium-fee adjustment](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-00321.pdf); [State Department fees](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html))

ESTIMATE — Budget approximately $7,500–$15,000 in legal fees and roughly 8–18 months end-to-end, although premium processing can accelerate the I-140 stage. Consular/adjustment timing and visa availability remain separate variables. ([Headman Law market survey, 8 June 2026](https://headmanlaw.com/insights/immigration-lawyer-cost-2026/); [State Department Visa Bulletin, observed 24 August 2026](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin.html))

### H-1B

FACT — The normal cap is 65,000 plus 20,000 for people with a qualifying advanced degree from a US institution. A foreign PhD does not enter the US advanced-degree exemption. Higher-education institutions, affiliated nonprofits, nonprofit research organizations and government research organizations can be cap-exempt. ([USCIS employment guide, observed 2026](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/foia/Nonimmigrant_Empl.pdf))

FACT — The job must qualify as a specialty occupation with a directly related specific degree requirement. ([USCIS H-1B decision](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/err/D2%20-%20Temporary%20Worker%20in%20a%20Specialty%20Occupation%20or%20Fashion%20Model%20%28H-1B%29/Decisions_Issued_in_2020/MAR042020_09D2101.pdf))

ESTIMATE — H-1B is inferior to a viable O-1A for this goal: lottery risk, annual timing, and a possible specialty-occupation argument for broadly titled community/DevRel jobs. Applied AI Architect or Solutions Architect is easier to map to a specialty occupation than Community Lead.

### L-1

FACT — L-1 normally requires one continuous year of qualifying employment abroad during the preceding three years for a related foreign entity, followed by transfer to an affiliated US entity as a manager/executive or specialized-knowledge employee. ([USCIS L-1 guidance, updated 2025](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-l-chapter-8))

ESTIMATE — Not presently available unless the candidate first joins a lab’s non-US entity, works there for the qualifying period, and later transfers. This can become strategically relevant after joining Mistral Paris, Anthropic London/Paris or another multinational lab.

### Cap-exempt route

ESTIMATE — A joint university/lab or nonprofit-research appointment can eliminate the lottery, but ordinary commercial employment at the listed labs is not cap-exempt merely because the work is research-related. It is a niche backup, not the primary plan.

## Europe, UK, Switzerland and Singapore

FACT — If “EU-eligible” means an actual Portuguese or other EU citizenship, the candidate generally needs no work permit for France or the Netherlands. If it means only Portuguese residence, this freedom does not automatically apply. ([EU Your Europe, updated 19 August 2026](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/work/work-abroad/work-permits/indexamp_en.htm); [Netherlands IND, updated 10 July 2026](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/eu-eea-or-swiss-citizens/staying-in-the-netherlands-as-an-eu-eea-or-swiss-citizen))

FACT — Portugal is covered by Switzerland’s EU/EFTA free-movement framework; work lasting more than three months generally requires the relevant residence permit, while short work uses a notification process. ([Swiss government guide, observed August 2026](https://www.ch.ch/en/work/working-in-switzerland-as-a-foreign-national/); [Swiss SEM, 13 January 2026](https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/fza_schweiz-eu-efta/eu-efta_buerger_schweiz.html))

FACT — EU citizenship no longer removes UK immigration requirements. A Skilled Worker applicant normally needs an approved sponsor, an eligible occupation and the higher of £41,700 or the occupation’s going rate. ([GOV.UK, observed August 2026](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-job))

FACT — Singapore’s Employment Pass is employer-filed and requires both the age-adjusted qualifying salary and, unless exempt, at least 40 COMPASS points. The 2026 minimum starts at S$5,600 monthly and rises with age; online applications are processed or given an update within ten business days. ([Singapore MOM eligibility, 28 April 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/eligibility); [processing time, observed August 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/faq/work-pass-general/what-are-the-changes-in-the-processing-time-for-work-passes))

## Where relocation materially improves odds

ESTIMATE — Location priority:

1. San Francisco: greatest concentration of the relevant jobs, but highest immigration friction.
2. London: exceptional current concentration—Cursor, Anthropic, Together, Cohere, Google/DeepMind and other AI infrastructure firms—but requires UK sponsorship unless the candidate already has UK status.
3. Paris: best risk-adjusted move for an EU citizen because Mistral concentrates product, GTM and Applied AI work there and explicitly provides relocation support. 4. New York: substantial Cursor, OpenAI, Anthropic, Fireworks and Perplexity density.
5. Zurich: immigration is relatively straightforward for an EU citizen, but the matching role volume is far smaller.
6. Singapore: meaningful Cursor, Together, ElevenLabs and DeepMind presence, but an employer-filed Employment Pass is still required.

UNKNOWN — I found no named, verifiable public case explicitly identifying a frontier lab as having sponsored an O-1 for a DevRel or community employee. Visa records generally do not disclose role narratives cleanly, and employee accounts rarely name the classification. Do not treat the absence of a published case as evidence that companies will not do it.

# Block 4 — The OSS door: documented sequences

## Successful cases

### Peter Steinberger → OpenAI, 2026

FACT — Peter Steinberger created OpenClaw, which became a rapidly visible public agent project in late 2025/early 2026; OpenAI hired him in February 2026 to work on personal agents, while OpenClaw moved to an independent foundation. ([TechCrunch, 15 February 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/15/openclaw-creator-peter-steinberger-joins-openai/); [OpenClaw Foundation account, 16 February 2026](https://openclaw.academy/blog/openclaw-creator-joins-openai-foundation/))

FACT — Steinberger said he had not planned to return to company employment and was waiting for a US visa while hoping to relocate to San Francisco. ([Sources, 26 March 2026](https://sources.news/p/clawfather-on-joining-openai))

UNKNOWN — The precise first message and whether OpenAI or Steinberger initiated it have not been publicly documented.

Sequence: build a conspicuous, product-adjacent agent → rapid developer adoption → labs and investors become aware → founder joins OpenAI without a normal public-application narrative.

### Lewis Tunstall and Leandro von Werra → Hugging Face

FACT — In 2019, Tunstall and von Werra decided to write a practical Transformers book and cold-emailed Hugging Face co-founder Thomas Wolf. Wolf responded, collaborated with them, and roughly 1.5 years later the book existed. Tunstall said that collaboration “set the seeds” for both authors eventually joining Hugging Face; by April 2022, Tunstall had been there about nine months as a machine-learning engineer. ([Tunstall first-person interview, 13 April 2022](https://huggingface.co/blog/lewis-tunstall-interview))

Sequence: identify an education gap → cold outreach with a concrete artifact commitment → 1.5 years of collaboration → trusted ecosystem relationship → employment.

This is the cleanest documented analogue for Developer Education.

### Xuan Son Nguyen / ngxson → Hugging Face, 2024

FACT — Nguyen wrote that he started contributing in early 2024 to projects including llama.cpp, wllama and huggingface.js, then spoke “by coincidence” with Hugging Face CTO Julien Chaumond; the team was open to bringing him aboard, and he joined as a software engineer in 2024. ([Nguyen first-person account, 5 August 2024](https://blog.ngxson.com/i-joined-hugging-face))

UNKNOWN — He did not fully disclose who initiated the decisive conversation or whether a conventional application followed.

Sequence: multiple ecosystem contributions → visible browser/local-model expertise → CTO conversation → direct hiring discussion.

### Joshua Lochner / Xenova → Hugging Face, 2023

FACT — Lochner created Transformers.js, bringing transformer inference to the browser, and joined Hugging Face in 2023. W3C meeting minutes explicitly identify him as the creator and note that he had recently joined. ([W3C minutes, 8 June 2023](https://www.w3.org/2023/06/08-webmachinelearning-minutes.html); [Transformers.js talk](https://gitnation.com/contents/transformersjs-state-of-the-art-machine-learning-for-the-web))

UNKNOWN — The first contact, exact contribution duration before hiring and application mechanics are not public.

Sequence: independently create a strategically important missing implementation → implementation effectively becomes part of the company’s product surface → hire the creator.

### Gradio team → Hugging Face, 2021

FACT — Gradio began in early 2019 and built an OSS interface for ML demos; after repeated collaboration with Hugging Face, Hugging Face acquired Gradio in December 2021 and the team joined. ([HF announcement, 21 December 2021](https://huggingface.co/blog/gradio-joins-hf); [Abubakar Abid first-person retrospective, 17 January 2024](https://huggingface.co/posts/abidlabs/668103230842988))

Sequence: build independent developer infrastructure → product-level integration and repeated collaboration → acquisition/team hire.

### XetHub founders and team → Hugging Face, 2024

FACT — XetHub, founded by Yucheng Low, Ajit Banerjee and Rajat Arya, built Git-based storage technology for very large repositories; Hugging Face acquired the company in August 2024 and the whole team joined. ([Hugging Face, 8 August 2024](https://huggingface.co/blog/xethub-joins-hf))

UNKNOWN — The first contact was not disclosed.

Sequence: solve a scaling problem that is core to the target company → demonstrate a functioning company/product → strategic acquisition and team hire.

### Yih-Dar Shieh → Hugging Face

FACT — A Hugging Face employee publicly described Shieh as a “textbook” OSS-contributor hire and a “no-brainer,” saying the company asked the contributor to join the open-source team for reliability and testing work. ([Nathan Raw public account, 2022](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nathan-raw_opensource-huggingface-hiring-activity-6904085520519958529-0yno))

UNKNOWN — The public post does not provide a complete contribution timeline or identify the first message.

## Cases where OSS did not produce a hire

### Robin Grell / enigo → Anthropic rejection, 2025

FACT — Grell maintained `enigo`, a Rust input-simulation library with approximately 300,000 downloads and more than 1,200 GitHub stars at the time of his account. He discovered that Claude Desktop used it on macOS and Windows. Through a friend-of-a-friend, he found a related opening, applied, waited several weeks, and was told that the team lacked capacity to review more applications; he received no interview. ([Robin Grell, 3 July 2025](https://grell.dev/blog/ai_rejection))

Why it failed: UNKNOWN — Anthropic did not provide a substantive decision. The documented lesson is that even direct product dependency, niche expertise and a warm informational path do not guarantee review when the requisition is saturated or effectively closing.

### Contributor programs that are not employment

FACT — Hugging Face’s Fellowship is explicitly a community-contribution arrangement, not an employee role; fellows can receive resources and recognition but are not automatically employees. ([Hugging Face Fellowship, 17 May 2022](https://huggingface.co/blog/fellowship))

FACT — A Harvard case study reported that Hugging Face often hired contributors but discouraged people from treating contribution as an audition; most contributors remained volunteers. ([Harvard/Hugging Face case, published 2023](https://ap.itc.ntnu.edu.tw/NtnuFileShare/file.do?action=dl&fno=39762307536b46af80cfcbc53be4fdf5&gno=94a865ce2c7740539980e925b6be42ce))

## What the successful sequences have in common

ESTIMATE — The artifact is not merely “a PR.” It is one of:

- a missing product layer;
- a serious educational asset produced with the company;
- infrastructure the company already needs;
- an adopted project that creates strategic pressure;
- repeated contribution that builds trust with a named internal sponsor.

The Robin Grell case shows the limiting condition: even excellent, directly used OSS can remain an unpaid dependency unless a person inside the company converts it into an active recruiting process.

# Block 6 — Anti-patterns

## AI-assisted rather than hand-coded work

FACT — Anthropic encourages AI for refining an applicant’s own first draft and for interview preparation, but forbids invented experiences, AI-written assessments unless expressly allowed, and AI assistance during live interviews unless permitted. It says candidates must be transparent and show their own thinking. ([Anthropic candidate guidance, updated 10 July 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance))

FACT — OpenAI says AI/tool rules vary by assessment: some explicitly allow tools and others test independent problem solving. Engineering assessment still evaluates solution quality, code, performance and tests. ([OpenAI interview guide, observed 24 August 2026](https://openai.com/interview-guide/))
