## 3. 1023-EZ vs full 1023 for *this* case

### Eligibility facts (IRS Form 1023-EZ instructions / worksheet)

You may use EZ **only if** you answer **No** to all eligibility questions, including:

- Projected annual gross receipts **> $50,000 in any of next 3 years** → must use full 1023  
- Assets **> $250,000** → full 1023  
- Formed under foreign law → full 1023  
- Certain org types (e.g., **schools**, churches, hospitals, etc.) barred from EZ  

Your stated year-1 **&lt;$50K** keeps the *revenue door* open for EZ **if** you honestly project years 2–3 the same and you are **not** a formal “school” under IRS definitions (degree-granting / formal school attributes). freeCodeCamp-class **public educational programming** is often *not* a “school” — but if you describe accredited curriculum, enrolled student body, faculty, etc., you can walk into a disqualifier. **Honesty on the worksheet is not optional** — IRS warns material misstatements undermine reliance on any favorable letter.

| Camp | Argument | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Use EZ** | You qualify on dollars; 80% in ~22 days; get letter + Google stack fast; freeCodeCamp-scale orgs grew with lean starts | Strong on speed/cost [established for IRS times] |
| **Use full 1023** | Related for-profit in same domain = private benefit optics; full form forces **narrative, related orgs, compensation, conflict policies** into the IRS file; foundations and sophisticated sponsors prefer a “real” file; EZ is attestation theater | Strong for *this fact pattern* [emerging consensus among careful counsel; Charity Lawyer Blog stresses EZ risks of omission] |

No high-quality public dataset found that Google Ad Grants / TechSoup / foundations systematically reject **valid** 1023-EZ determination letters. Google eligibility is **valid 501(c)(3)** (plus program rules), not “which form you used.”

**Claim “EZ gets more grant rejections”:** [speculative / thin evidence] — treat as risk of *optics*, not a hard Google rule. [no source found for systematic EZ discrimination by Google]

### Recommendation for *this* case

**Prefer full Form 1023** despite slower IRS clock, **because the dominant risk is private benefit / inurement with a same-domain for-profit**, not the $325 fee difference.  
**Exception:** if year-1 truly stays &lt;$50K **and** you will (i) separate brands completely, (ii) put **≥3 unrelated directors** in control, (iii) zero lead-funnel from nonprofit → commercial products, and (iv) accept later upgrade complexity if you outgrow $50K — then EZ is *legally available* and fastest to perks.

## 4. Pitfalls (ranked by severity)

### S1 — Private benefit / inurement with sibling for-profit **[severity: existential]**

- Nonprofit must be organized/operated for **public**, not founder’s commercial brand.  
- Traps: name that echoes the VC brand; website CTAs to paid consulting; “free course → sales call”; founder-only board; exclusive IP licenses one-way to the for-profit; “reasonable compensation” without comparables/board minutes.  
- Design: distinct name/brand; arm’s-length MOUs if any shared services; **written COI policy + annual disclosures**; compensation set by **disinterested directors**; no traffic arbitrage from Ad Grants to commercial products (Ad Grants is for the mission site).  
- 501c3.help’s public site correctly flags this as “the risk that ends organizations” — the *idea* is right; the *execution* must be lawyer-grade. [established doctrine; specific enforcement examples case-by-case]

### S2 — Wrong form / false EZ eligibility **[high]**

- File EZ while projecting foundation grants that push receipts &gt;$50K in year 2–3 → ineligible.  
- Services that push EZ because it’s productized: classic cheap-service trap. IRS: if not eligible, application can be **rejected** (fee pain + delay).

### S3 — California $800 franchise tax window **[high $ risk]**

- FTB: **all corporations are taxable until FTB grants exemption** (FTB 3500A instructions).  
- After federal determination letter → file **FTB 3500A** + copy of letter. Retroactivity can track federal effective date if done correctly.  
- Leave this hanging and you flirt with **minimum franchise tax exposure** ($800 class risk for nontax-exempt CA corps — FTB Pub 927 language). File 3500A **immediately** after the letter. [established]

### S4 — CA AG Registry (CT-1 / RRF-1) **[high operational]**

- Initial **CT-1**, fee **$50** (OAG forms update).  
- Annual **RRF-1**; fee **$25** if total revenue &lt;$50K (schedule on RRF-1).  
- Operating/soliciting without registration → delinquency / cannot legally solicit in CA.

### S5 — Board composition **[high governance / grant optics]**

- CA public benefit corps and funders expect a **real board**, not a founder solo show.  
- Common practical standard: **≥3 directors**, majority **unrelated by family/business** to founder (not always a hard IRS statute for every micro-charity, but **grantmakers and Google validation** care).  
- Foreign-resident directors: generally allowed; banking/KYC and notarization harder.  
- Spouse on board: common but weakens independence if board is only founder+spouse.

### S6 — Non-resident founder banking/mail **[medium-high]**

- You have **SSN + US mailing address** — big advantage for EIN online and many fintech KYC flows.  
- **Mercury / fintechs in 2026:** mixed, stricter than earlier years for non-US residents; denials common for thin presence / address issues [emerging, low-authority blogs + founder forums].  
- Practical ladder: **local CA community bank / credit union** with US director present OR fintech that accepts US entity + SSN founder + US address; keep **registered agent** separate from “principal office” if required.  
- Notarization from Portugal: plan apostille/remote online notarization early for bank resolutions.

### S7 — Multistate fundraising registration **[medium]**

- If you solicit nationally (online donate button), many states require charity registration. Harbor/Labyrinth-class vendors exist for this; DIY is death by forms.

### S8 — Cheap-service traps **[medium]**

- Template bylaws missing dissolution / purpose language.  
- No POA → service cannot talk to IRS (seen in InstantNonprofit Reddit dispute).  
- Upsells (compliance subscriptions, “autopilot 990”).  
- Ghosting after payment.  
- **Mitigation:** written SOW, deliverables list, who files what, RFI handling, refund triggers, named specialist.

### S9 — Perk-stack traps **[medium, after letter]**

- **Google for Nonprofits / Ad Grants:** need valid 501(c)(3); strong site; ongoing policy compliance.  
- Fiscally sponsored projects **without own 501(c)(3)** are **not independently eligible** for Google for Nonprofits (Google Support).  
- Ad Grants: accounts under-spend or suspend when **CTR / quality / conversion** rules fail (industry guidance repeatedly cites **~5% CTR** thresholds and management needs — treat operational details as [emerging] vendor/practitioner consensus; confirm current Google policy at application time).  
- **TechSoup:** validation delays if determination letter / Guidestar / website incomplete — plan 2–6 weeks buffer [low-authority timing; no single official SLA found in this pass].
