Good brief — tightly scoped, and the platform-comparison framing is exactly the right question given you already know the DIY path. Let me give you my direct read on the verdict-shaping items before the numbers get verified, since a few of them are fairly stable:

**1023-EZ vs full 1023** — for your specific case, most serious practitioners would say full 1023, even with year-1 revenue under $50K. Three reasons: (1) the determination letter looks identical, but the EZ filing is visible in the IRS's public database, and some foundation grant officers treat EZ orgs as un-vetted (the IRS itself found high error rates in EZ approvals in its own audits, which is why grantmakers discount it); (2) the for-profit sibling brand is precisely the situation where you *want* the full narrative — Part IV lets you proactively describe the relationship, the conflict-of-interest policy, and the firewall, so it's on record rather than discovered later; (3) perk programs themselves (Google, TechSoup) don't distinguish, so the EZ speed advantage only buys you weeks on a multi-month critical path anyway. The counterargument is real — EZ historically clears in weeks vs months — but you'd be trading credibility you explicitly said you want for time you can bridge via HCB.

**CA vs Delaware** — with operations and contacts in Sacramento/Bay Area, Delaware incorporation is almost certainly the wrong call. You'd still need CA foreign registration, still fall under the CA AG's Registry of Charitable Trusts, still need FTB exemption, and now you're paying registered agents and annual filings in two states. The $800 franchise tax fear is also mostly a phantom for nonprofits: a CA nonprofit public benefit corporation that files the FTB 3500A promptly after getting the federal determination letter is exempt, and the exemption is retroactive to formation in the normal case. Delaware/Wyoming nonprofit formation is mostly sold by formation mills whose templates are for-profit-shaped.

**Fiscal sponsorship bridge** — yes, "operate under HCB now, register in parallel, migrate later" is a well-established and recommended pattern, not a conflict. The one trap you flagged correctly: Google for Nonprofits (and thus Ad Grants) requires your own determination letter — fiscally sponsored projects generally can't get their own G4NP account, so the perk stack clock only starts at your own determination letter regardless of the HCB bridge. Budget donor-communication and fund-transfer mechanics into the migration.

**Platform landscape, rough prior** — LegalZoom and the ZenBusiness/Bizee tier are overpriced generic filers for this use case; the specialist tier (Harbor Compliance, InstantNonprofit, Nonprofit Elite, BryteBridge) is where the real comparison lives, and their reputations, current pricing, and whether they genuinely handle the CA-specific stack (3500A, CT-1) vs Delaware boilerplate is exactly what needs current verification — as does 501c3.help, which I'd want evaluated on documented reviews and deliverables like any other vendor, not on the acquaintance relationship. Current IRS processing times, CA SoS turnaround, banking options for a founder abroad (Mercury's 2024–26 policy changes matter here), and the Ad Grants suspension patterns all drift too fast for me to state numbers confidently without checking.
