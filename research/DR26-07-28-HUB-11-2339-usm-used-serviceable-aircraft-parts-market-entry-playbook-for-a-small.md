# Insight (DR DR26-07-28-HUB-11-2339): USM (used serviceable aircraft parts) market entry playbook for a small independent broker in 2026

> Deep-research playbook for an experienced B2B/e-commerce entrepreneur entering the global used-serviceable-material aircraft parts trade legally, outside the sanctioned Russian market.

## Ключевые выводы
- Global USM market is roughly $6-9B/year (emerging confidence, not auditable) vs. the much larger commercial MRO market at $139.6B (2026) growing to $193.1B (2036); USM is a minority but important slice relieving OEM cost/availability pressure.
- Most liquid demand in 2026 remains legacy narrowbody fleets (A320 family, 737 family) because airlines have delayed retirement of 737NG/A320ceo, constraining USM supply; CFM56 is the largest in-service engine family (~20,000 units); some MRO shops use USM for up to 30% of part demand.
- Post-2024 dynamic is split: broad legacy narrowbody USM is supply-constrained by delayed retirements, while distress-driven teardown of near-new A320neo aircraft (e.g. Spirit's 2026 shutdown) is releasing engines/APUs/landing gear because they're worth more than the airframe.
- Value chain: teardown specialists/part-out investors, stockists/distributors (AAR, AJW, GA Telesis, Kellstrom, Setna iO), brokers, MROs (issue release paperwork), lessors/airlines (hidden major suppliers of surplus). Feedstock acquisition is increasingly financialized/capital-blended, squeezing out small players from teardown.
- For $50k-200k starting capital, best model sequence is: pure back-to-back brokerage first (5-12% gross margin, near-zero working capital if prepayment secured) → consignment for MROs/operators second (10-25% commission, 60-180 day cycle) → niche stocking third (15-35% margin, longer cash cycle); avoid principal-risk teardown investing and broad inventory ownership as a beginner.
- A broker without a repair station is fully legal (FAA does not certificate distributors) as long as it doesn't misrepresent airworthiness/eligibility/traceability (14 CFR 3.5); it cannot self-issue FAA 8130-3 or EASA Form 1 — only approved maintenance orgs (Part 145) can. 'Back to birth' is not a regulatory requirement (EASA/FAA) but is often a commercial/market expectation for life-limited parts. ASA-100/AC 00-56B accreditation is voluntary and not needed to start.
- Core marketplaces: ILS (1B+ parts listed, $5B+ annual RFQs), PartsBase (API-driven RFQ/quote/inventory), Locatory (25,000+ members, custom pricing), StockMarket.aero (free listing — cheapest entry). AI-native players (SkySelect, ePlane AI) focus on procurement automation for airline/MRO buyers, leaving a gap for a nimble broker combining marketplace flow, repair quotes, sanctions screening, and document extraction into one workflow.
- Compliance is existential: EAR covers most civil parts (EAR99 is not a free pass), sanctions screening must cover OFAC, US Consolidated Screening List, EU list, and the new UK Sanctions List (replaced OFSI list Jan 2026); EU Article 12g 'no re-export to Russia' clause applies to aircraft/engine/parts exporters. Multiple 2022-2026 US enforcement cases (Latvian broker, Ohio parts supplier, Indian national, Belarusian woman, US-Russian dual national) show real prison-time risk for Russia-diversion schemes even via third countries (India/UAE/Turkey/Central Asia).
- Realistic timeline to $10k/month gross profit is 90-180 days, achieved via ~6 small brokerage deals/month ($1.5-2k gross each) or ~3 rotable/component deals ($3-4k gross each) — figures are speculative operating assumptions, not published benchmarks. Common failure modes: too-broad focus, unverified supplier paperwork, early credit extension, slow/manual RFQ response.
- Best legal demand geographies ranked by fleet growth + MRO capacity gap: India (7.1% fleet CAGR, 8.4% MRO CAGR through 2036, +3,290 aircraft over 20 years per Boeing), Middle East (high growth but 2026 security volatility), Latin America (~4% growth, needs practical support over sophisticated procurement), Africa (strong MRO growth but supply-chain fragmentation), Southeast Asia (growing but needs country-by-country execution).

## Рекомендации / решения
- Enter as a documentation-first, compliance-hard, software-accelerated broker in legacy narrowbody USM (737NG/A320ceo focus, 2-3 ATA niches), then expand into consignment, and only later into physical stock.
- 90-day plan: Days 1-30 — pick platform focus, form legal entity, build compliance/KYC procedures and supplier approval files, subscribe to at least one core marketplace (ILS/PartsBase/Locatory/StockMarket.aero), implement sanctions screening (OFAC/CSL/EU/UK), build quote CRM + inbox automation. Days 30-60 — recruit 10-20 approved suppliers, approach regional MROs for consignment, turn on RFQ automation, start outreach to India/SE Asia/Middle East/LatAm buyers. Days 60-90 — review win/loss, formalize records checklist, attend one trade event, decide on a narrow inventory micro-bet only if demand is proven.
- Build an internal QMS (receiving checklist, records control, quarantine procedure, tag verification, trace matrix, sanctions screening log, supplier approval file) as if audited, before pursuing voluntary ASA-100 accreditation later.
- Never ship to a counterparty you don't fully understand; never accept deferred end-user disclosure; never rely on a single sanctions list; never let a freight forwarder be your only KYC layer; never upgrade AR condition to SV via vague language; avoid any Russia/Belarus diversion chain, even indirect.
- Rank top-10 risks in this order: sanctions/export-control breach (existential) > false condition/paperwork misrepresentation > supplier non-performance > customer credit/default > counterfeit/SUP exposure > over-broad market focus > repair-cycle margin surprises > platform dependency > cash-cycle mismatch > premature inventory buying.
- First contacts (20): marketplaces ILS/PartsBase/Locatory/StockMarket.aero; suppliers/partners/benchmarks AAR/AJW Group/GA Telesis/Kellstrom Aerospace/Setna iO/AerFin/APOC Aviation/Aventure Aviation; automation intel SkySelect/ePlane AI; teardown nodes Ascent Aviation Services/TARMAC Aerosave/ecube; communities ASA/AFRA/ISTAT/MRO Europe/MRO Asia-Pacific.
