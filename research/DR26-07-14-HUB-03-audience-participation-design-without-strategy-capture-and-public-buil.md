# Insight (DR DR26-07-14-HUB-03): Audience participation design without strategy capture, and public-build path to LLM DevRel hiring

> Researches how a founder can give a public audience real, bounded influence over a build-in-public show without losing strategic control, and what specific public artifacts and framing get people hired into DevRel-type roles at OpenAI/Anthropic-tier companies.

## Ключевые выводы
- Best participation model is a scoped 'consult/involve' system, not audience-decides-everything: audience shapes bounded, reversible decisions and sees receipts, while founder keeps fiduciary/strategic judgment (IAP2 spectrum: inform/consult/involve/collaborate/empower).
- Unclear or symbolic voting backfires because people infer an implicit contract once asked to vote — canonical failures Boaty McBoatface and Mountain Dew's 'Dub the Dew' happened because an under-scoped decision was opened to the public and virality outran the institution's real willingness to comply.
- For sub-10k audiences, specificity/trust/repeated interaction beats scale; small communities offer expertise, trust, easier newcomer integration and fewer trolls, and are easier to keep 'seen and heard.'
- Recognition-heavy participation (visible acknowledgment even for non-winners) sustains continued engagement better than one-off wins; short-term engagement spikes alone show null loyalty effects in panel-data research.
- At scale, free-form crowd voting gets noisier and more gameable — 'wisdom of crowds' weakens when voters are socially networked rather than independent, so large audiences need pre-filtered options and staged authority, not open write-ins.
- Minimum viable interactivity for a founder diary: one weekly consult poll on a real bounded fork + one monthly binding vote from a pre-screened, reversible-decision menu + an ongoing forecast board + a founder-constrained challenge lane.
- OpenAI/Anthropic-tier DevRel hiring in 2025-2026 screens for credible technical artifacts (demos, tutorials, repos, workshops) plus proof of moving builders from curiosity to adoption — not content/post volume; Anthropic explicitly ranks blog posts/OSS/independent research at the top of the resume.
- DevRel is fragmenting into ~4 lanes at frontier labs: technical content/demos, education systems, developer marketing/ecosystem activation, and adoption/customer engineering (Cursor's role design makes this split explicit).
- Documented public-build-to-hire cases (Steinberger→OpenAI via OpenClaw, Marsh/Astral→OpenAI Codex via Ruff/uv/ty, Rodriguez→Camunda via OSS, Proser→Pinecone via public learning, Gee→JetBrains via public reputation) show the noticed artifact was consistently a repo/tool/talk circuit/educational body of work, not an opinion feed.
- Web3 ecosystem-growth experience (hackathons, builder cohorts, protocol education, governance feedback) maps cleanly onto DevRel functions but must be reframed in adoption vocabulary ('activated builders', 'time-to-first-working-agent', 'adoption outcomes') instead of community/hype language to avoid reading as a weak generalist.

## Рекомендации / решения
- Default all audience input to consult/involve levels; reserve 'empower' (binding votes) only for narrow, reversible, low-risk decisions from a founder-pre-screened menu, never open write-ins on brand-critical items (e.g., naming).
- Publish an explicit public vote schema before every vote: decision class, in/out of scope, binding vs advisory, implementation window, tie-breaker, founder veto conditions, and a post-vote 'how your input changed the outcome' receipt.
- Keep business model, pricing, pivots, fundraising, hiring/firing, security/legal, and declared strategic direction strictly founder-only, never put to audience vote.
- Build the minimum viable interactivity stack (weekly consult poll, monthly binding reversible vote, forecast board, challenge lane) rather than constant ad hoc voting.
- Track repeat participation, comment quality, repo visits, signups and downstream action — not raw vote counts — as the real loyalty/engagement signal.
- For DevRel hiring positioning: ship 2-3 real public AI demos with strong READMEs, publish one flagship tutorial/workshop, and build a one-page portfolio linking repos, talks, decks, metrics, and testimonials.
- Translate Web3/ecosystem-growth background into adoption-outcome language (activated builders, POC-to-production conversion, feedback loops into product) instead of community/growth/hype terminology when packaging for LLM DevRel roles.
- Lead resume/portfolio metrics with adoption evidence (builders who shipped, hackathon-to-retention conversion, issues surfaced that changed product) rather than impressions or event-attendance counts.
- Target authentic referrals only from people who have directly seen the work; avoid generic networking asks, per OpenAI's explicit recruiting guidance.
- Frame the show's public target-company narrative as a coherent, work-backed conviction ('I build in public because it improves my work'), and set explicit client-service boundaries to manage the (currently unevidenced) risk of unsettling existing consulting clients.

## Открытые вопросы
- Exact Anthropic interview loops for DevRel-adjacent roles (Technical Education, Enterprise Community Lead, Head of Content & Curriculum) are not fully documented publicly.
- Causal attribution in several public-build-to-hire stories (e.g., Scarlett, Cone) is self-reported rather than independently verified.
- No direct empirical evidence found on how existing consulting clients react to a public declaration of a target employer — mitigation is inferred, not evidence-based.
- Whether forecasting tournaments (audience forecast boards) actually improve creator/audience conversion is unsettled — evidence supports forecasting judgment accuracy generally, not this specific conversion effect.
