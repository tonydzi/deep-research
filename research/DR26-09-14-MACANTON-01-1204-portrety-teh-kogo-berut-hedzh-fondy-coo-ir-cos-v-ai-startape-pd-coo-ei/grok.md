B. ROLE FAMILY 1 — HEDGE FUNDS AND QUANT / CRYPTO FUNDS

B1. What the 2024–2026 hiring market actually looks like [established]

Three parallel markets, not one:

Market A — AI/ML research inside multi-strats and prop shops. Citadel, Two Sigma, Jane Street, Balyasny, Bridgewater AIA, Point72, Millennium, Jump, D.E. Shaw are all building dedicated AI labs or applied-AI desks. Recruiter Andy Legg (ex Citadel/Point72/AQR/Two Sigma, now Riviera Partners) said hiring has been “PhD-centric” since ~2009, and that CS/Stats/ML/AI PhDs under a renowned professor with papers at leading conferences can command seven-figure total comp young. https://www.ai-street.co/p/inside-ai-hiring-on-wall-street 2026-04-28 [established]

Market B — IR / capital formation. These seats hire career IR and fundraising people from alts (Apollo, Perella Weinberg, Meta IR for AI-lab IR), not physicists and not founders. Galaxy’s 14 Sep 2026 Head of IR hire is the cleanest recent example. https://cryptobriefing.com/galaxy-hires-taylor-reinhardt-investor-relations/ 2026-09-14 [established]

Market C — digital-assets PMs / crypto strategy at large HFs. These seats hire traders with a book, not operators. Brevan Howard’s BH Digital hire of David Kalk (ex Thiel family office, co-founder/CIO Reflexive Capital, Goldman rates) is the pattern. https://www.hedgeweek.com/brevan-howard-hires-ex-thiel-trader-as-part-of-crypto-push/ 2025-07-29 [established]

The PhD-physicist pipeline is real, and it is also narrow. Citadel Securities’ 2025 PhD QR posting asks for a PhD in math/stats/physics/CS, probability and statistics, coding in Python/R/C++, and independent research — not a finance résumé. B2. Hiring archetypes (composite portraits)

Archetype 1 — Fresh STEM PhD into QR / ML research. Prior: PhD in physics/math/CS/stats, often from a top program, coding in Python/C++, papers or a thesis with empirical ML. Years: 0–2 post-PhD. Credentials: PhD is the credential; CFA is irrelevant. Signature: “no prior finance required” on the JD. Frequency: the dominant junior research hire at Citadel Securities, Point72, Schonfeld-class shops. [established]

Archetype 2 — Big-tech / bank AI lead into fund AI lab. Prior: Google Brain / FAIR / Goldman Applied AI / MS Distinguished Engineer. Years: 8–15. Signature: named papers, named platforms, or a rare internal title (0.3% distinguished engineer). Frequency: the senior AI hire. Examples below. [established]

Archetype 3 — Career IR / capital formation. Prior: 8–15 years IR or fundraising at an alt manager, bulge-bracket, or public-company IR. Years: 10+. Credentials: CFA is nice-to-have, LP Rolodex is must-have. Signature: “led IR through an IPO / fundraise.” Frequency: almost every Head of IR / Head of Capital Formation seat. [established]

Archetype 4 — Trader / PM with a book, moving into digital assets. Prior: Goldman desk → family office / crypto HF → multi-strat crypto book. Years: 8–15. Track record is the product. Frequency: the only way into PM seats at BH Digital-class groups. [established]

Archetype 5 — Emerging-manager COO / CoS / business manager. Prior: HF COO, consulting-to-markets, or a PM’s right hand. Years: 10–15. JDs ask for “exceptional academic record and preferably additional industry qualifications” and names traders, McKinsey/BCG FS, or sitting HF COOs as the pool. Frequency: real but small; filled by recruiters (Mondrian Alpha), not public postings. [emerging] [single-source on the Mondrian Alpha CoS JD, seen in SERP]

Match: physics PhD is the classic QR feeder on paper. Crypto framework + APAC ecosystem is a real differentiator for a tiny set of crypto funds’ research/BD seats. O-1 + Palo Alto/Lisbon is operationally convenient.

Mismatch: you are not a fresh PhD who codes interviews all day; you are not a Google Brain / Goldman Applied AI lead; you are not a 10-year IR professional; you do not have a tradable book. h-index 7 with 50 papers is academic-modest, not the Legg “renowned professor” packet. Non-engineer operator who ships agents is impressive for ops, invisible for QR.

C. ROLE FAMILY 2 — COO / IR / CHIEF OF STAFF AT AI STARTUPS (SEED–B)

C1. What actually gets hired [established, with a disagreement]

The job that exists in volume in 2025–2026 is Chief of Staff / BizOps / Strategy & Ops, not “first COO.”

Evan Lee’s 2 Jul 2025 roundup listed 24 CoS/BizOps openings in one week, seed through Series F, including Fastino, Savant Bio, Formant, Extend, Warp, EliseAI, Decagon, Aidoc, OpenAI. https://aioperators.substack.com/p/vol-005-ai-operators-24-chief-of 2025-07-02 [low-authority newsletter, but it is a primary scrape of live JDs] [emerging]

Bain Capital Ventures (Natalie Coletta, 18 Jun 2025) says CoS open roles have never been more common, and warns: if you are pre-PMF and technical, do not hire a CoS; if 80% of the JD is one function, hire that function. Four archetypes, in stage order: Early Team Generalist (seed/early A, 2–3 years, banking/consulting/PE analyst); Conductor (A/B, 4–6 years, PM/implementation); Functional Lead Stand-in (B/C, 6–8 years, consulting/IB/VC plus startup); CEO Brain Extension (C+, 10+ years, GM/strategic COO). Ideal Early Generalist: “banking analyst, management consultant, PE analyst/associate.” Beware of F500 operators and of people who want to be CEO. https://baincapitalventures.com/insight/the-four-chief-of-staff-archetypes-and-which-one-your-startup-actually-needs 2025-06-18 [established as VC-operator testimony]

String AI’s CEO-written CoS JD (seed, 7-figure ARR, <10 people, NYC) is the cleanest primary: 2–5 years high-growth startup / consulting / IB / VC; former founder is a nice-to-have, not the target; “stepping stone to Head of Operations”; $130k–$180k + equity; already using AI daily. https://www.chiefofstaffjob.com/jobs/chief-of-staff-string-ai [established as a live JD]

Disagreement on COO:
- Jack Altman (Lattice) argues more post-PMF startups should hire a COO as an “executive gap-filler.” He hired J Zac Stein, former VP Ops at Zenefits, at ~20 people (Nov 2017 — old case, republished 13 Nov 2025). Stein ran sales through a leadership gap, CX, legal/finance/HR via contractors, then marketing. Altman: hire great people and give them scope; anxiety about loss of control is the blocker. https://jackealtman.com/how-to-work-with-a-coo-at-a-startup/ 2025-11-13 [emerging; one founder’s successful case, 2017 facts]
- The 2025–2026 AI-startup job market I could scrape does not match Altman’s advice. Seed–B AI companies post CoS, Head of Ops, StratOps — not COO. When a crypto/AI-infra company does hire a COO, it is a 30-year AWS-scale operator (Alchemy / Bill Platt, below), which is Series-scale, not seed–B. [emerging]

IR at AI companies is a late-stage public-markets job, not a seed–B job. Anthropic hired Meta’s IR head, then posted a $425k–$600k Director, IR to “explain a research-driven company to a financial audience” ahead of IPO. https://www.businessinsider.com/anthropic-ipo-hiring-investor-relations-director-2026-7 2026-07-17 [established] That seat is not your seat.

F. DECISION OPTIONS (NAMED, WITH TRADE-OFFS) AND ONE RECOMMENDATION

Option 1 — Run Family 3 as the primary campaign for 90 days.
Do: map 30 live programs (AI accelerators, university incubators, corporate accelerators, protocol builder/grants, Catalyze-class safety incubators, Lisbon + Palo Alto nodes). Rewrite one CV as Program Director / Head of Platform. Warm intros to program GPs, not job boards first. Offer to guest-run a cohort module (agents, fundraising ops, APAC) as a forcing function.
Trade-off: cash is lower ($75k–$180k at nonprofit/early programs; partner economics only if you get carry). Status is high in the builder world, low on Wall Street.
Risk: you look like you are “stepping back from founder” rather than stepping into a leverage seat. Mitigate by attaching the AI lab as a continuing affiliation.

Option 2 — Parallel Family 2 CoS, only via named founders, not applications.
Do: 15 warm shots to seed–A AI CEOs who need an operator who already runs agents. CV title CoS / Head of Ops, not COO. Explicitly write “I will not be a second CEO.”
Trade-off: you will be paid like a 5–8 year operator, not like a co-founder. You must be in SF or NYC five days.
Risk: “too senior” rejection. If three founders say that, stop; do not spray.

Option 3 — Family 1 only as a side door into emerging crypto managers.
Do: not Citadel QR. Do: two or three GPs at sub-$1bn crypto funds where the stablecoin/APAC work is on-topic, aiming at COO / capital intro / research-advisor. Use the Moallemi pattern only if you can put a technical note on the table, not a bio.
Trade-off: long cycle, relationship-driven, high chance of zero.
Risk: wasting the 90 days that Option 1 needs.

Option 4 — Stay founder, productize the lab, use programs as distribution (EIR-without-the-title).
Do: keep the lab, take a 1–2 day/week EIR or venture-partner affiliation.
Trade-off: no salary; best long-term identity fit.
Risk: affiliation theater if it does not come with a cohort or capital.

Recommendation: Option 1 as the main line, Option 2 as a 15-shot overlay, Option 3 only if a GP asks. Confidence: [emerging] — the named-hire sample in Family 2 COO and Family 3 non-YC PD is thinner than Family 1 QR, so the rank order could move if a flood of seed COO hires appears. Main assumption: a candidate who has already operated an incubator is hired to operate incubators, not retrained as a junior quant or as Meta-IR. Secondary assumption: you will build a public professional surface (personal site + GitHub is the minimum; no-LinkedIn is a self-inflicted filter in recruiter-driven markets).

If that assumption about LinkedIn is wrong — if your network is strong enough that every process starts with a forwarded PDF — Family 3 still wins, just faster.
