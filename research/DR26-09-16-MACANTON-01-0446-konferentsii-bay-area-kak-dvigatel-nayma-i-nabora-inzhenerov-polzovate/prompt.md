# Deep Research request — Conferences as a job-search and user-acquisition engine (SF Bay Area, 2026)

## Who is asking
Anton, 45+, Russian-speaking, based in Palo Alto (Bay Area), running a tiny "AI research lab" (agent memory, second brain, multi-machine Claude fleet, CRM automation). Non-coder who ships via LLM agents; ~15 open-source repos / PRs into Anthropic cookbook, Claude Code, agent-memory projects (GitHub: tonydzi). Strong network builder, weak at follow-up discipline (his own words: "conferences are pointless if you can't follow up leads well"). He attends many free AI meetups and conferences in SF / Palo Alto / Mountain View every week (lu.ma, Meetup, hackathons, demo nights, company-hosted events).

## Two missions the research must serve
1. **Get hired by a top-10 LLM company** (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI, Mistral, Meta, Cohere, etc.) — realistic target roles: Developer Relations / DevRel engineer, Developer Advocate, Solutions / Forward-deployed engineer, Community / Ecosystem, Agent-tooling PM. He is on a visa-sensitive path (needs sponsorship or O-1 evidence).
2. **Recruit engineers who will USE his open-source tools and help improve them** (contributors, testers, co-maintainers, design partners) — not customers who pay, but engineers who adopt and give feedback.

## The main question
**How should a person with this profile completely redesign the way he attends the dense stream of AI conferences and meetups in the Bay Area so that every event produces measurable progress toward (1) a job offer at a frontier-LLM company and (2) new engineers adopting and contributing to his tools — instead of just "seeing what's out there"?**

## Sub-questions (answer each with evidence, not platitudes)
1. **Event selection.** Which event TYPES in the Bay Area in 2025–2026 actually convert into hiring conversations and OSS adoption (company-hosted dev days, hackathons, small dinners, meetups with talks, large conferences like AI Engineer World's Fair, TechCrunch Disrupt, etc.)? Cite any data or credible first-hand accounts on hiring outcomes per event type. What is the realistic "hit rate" per event?
2. **Pre-event preparation.** Concrete 30–60 minute prep routine: attendee-list mining (Luma, LinkedIn, X), picking 3–5 target people, preparing a 15-second self-intro and one "artifact to show" (repo, demo, one-pager). Templates that work.
3. **The pitch.** How should a 45+ non-coder who builds with agents describe himself so that hiring managers and engineers take him seriously? Give 3 versions of a 15-second intro: for a recruiter/hiring manager, for a senior engineer, for a founder. What to say about his goals explicitly ("I'm looking for a DevRel role at X") — does stating the goal help or hurt?
4. **Finding job opportunities on the floor.** Who at an event actually influences hiring (hiring managers, team leads, DevRel leads, recruiters at booths, internal referrers)? How to get a referral or a warm intro from a 5-minute chat. How to talk about visa/O-1 status without killing the conversation.
5. **Finding engineer-users for OSS tools.** Best-practice playbooks from OSS maintainers / DevRel people who recruit contributors at events: live demo on a laptop, "try it now" QR, asking for a specific 10-minute task, converting to a Discord/GitHub issue within 24 hours. What actually converts a conversation into a first PR or issue?
6. **Follow-up system.** The 24h / 72h / 2-week follow-up cadence that people who actually get hired or get contributors use. Message templates (LinkedIn, email, X DM, Telegram). How to log every contact so nothing is lost (CRM fields that matter). Which follow-up mistakes kill the lead.
7. **Measuring whether an event was worth it.** A scorecard: inputs (hours, $), outputs (qualified contacts, referrals, demo-tries, PRs/issues, interviews scheduled within 30 days). Thresholds for "stop attending this kind of event".
8. **Speaking / hosting as leverage.** Evidence on whether giving a 5-minute lightning talk, running a demo table, or hosting one's own small meetup outperforms attending as a guest — for hiring visibility and for OSS adoption. How to get lightning-talk slots at Bay Area AI meetups.
9. **Counter-evidence.** Where do conferences NOT help (e.g., frontier-lab hiring runs through referrals and GitHub reputation, not badges)? When should he stop going to events and do something else instead? Be honest.
10. **A 30-day plan.** Given 3–5 events per week available, propose a concrete weekly rhythm: how many to attend, which to skip, what to do before/during/after, and the metrics to review each Sunday.

## Output format
- Lead with a one-page executive answer to the main question.
- Then answer sub-questions 1–10 in order, each with sources (URLs, dates) and explicit confidence.
- Mark clearly what is evidence-based vs. your opinion.
- Include ready-to-use templates: 3 intros, 4 follow-up messages, 1 scorecard table, 1 weekly plan.
- Language: English is fine. Be direct; he is used to blunt feedback.
