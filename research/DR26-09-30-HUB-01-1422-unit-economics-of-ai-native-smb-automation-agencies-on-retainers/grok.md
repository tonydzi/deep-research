How the three compare, for planning:

| Proxy | Typical logo loss | Implied LTV (rough) | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| MSP (embedded IT) | ~8–12%/year | ~8–12 years | Outcome + switching cost |
| Vertical SaaS | ~9–14%/year GRR loss | ~7–11 years | Workflow lock-in |

| Cheap AI product (<$50/mo) | 77% GRR loss / year (ChartMogul 23% GRR) | months | Experiment |

An AI-automation retainer that looks like a marketing campaign (monthly “we’ll build 1–3 workflows,” no SLA, no embedding) will churn like a marketing agency. One that looks like an MSP (monitoring, ticket budget, QBR, named owner of a production system) can approach MSP rates. ChartMogul’s $250/mo cliff is the product analogue: below a seriousness threshold, buyers treat the spend as a trial. [emerging]

At what churn the retainer stops compounding.

Arithmetic, not a source. Monthly logo churn c implies mean lifetime 1/c months if constant hazard.

- 5%/month (SMB SaaS midpoint) → LTV 20 months. A $2,000/mo book is worth $40,000 lifetime revenue before delivery cost.
- 3%/month → 33 months.
- 8%/year (~0.69%/month, good MSP) → ~12 years.
- 40% first-year exit (vcita-style new-book hazard) → you rebuild ~40% of the book every year; compounding does not start until vintage-2 retention is measured.

Treadmill rule: the book stops compounding when new logos per month ≤ churned logos per month. At 5% monthly churn, a 20-client book requires one replacement close every month just to stand still. At $2,000/mo, that 20-client treadmill is $480k ARR of running to stay in place. At 1.5% monthly (≈16% annual, still worse than a good MSP, better than SMB SaaS), the treadmill starts at ~67 clients. That is the difference between a cash engine and a sales job. [established as math; speculative as the actual AI-agency c]

Legal/compliance for US clients + Russian-based contractors.

Direction of the OFAC rule: E.O. 14071 determinations prohibit US persons from providing certain services to persons located in Russia. Categories now include accounting/trust/management consulting (from Jun 2022), architecture/engineering (from Jun 2023), and from 12 Sep 2024: IT consultancy and design services, plus IT support and cloud services for enterprise-management and design/manufacturing software. https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1128 (updated 12 Jun 2024); definitions in https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1187 (12 Jun 2024); determination PDF https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/932951/download.

That rule bites the US company when it sends prohibited IT services into Russia (including to its own Russia-based contractor). FAQ 1195 (24 Sep 2024), opened this run: a US company may not provide those prohibited IT/software services to an employee or contractor located in Russia who works directly for the US company; the “US-owned Russian entity” exclusion does not cover a Russia-based contractor of a US-located company. https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1195

Hiring a non-SDN Russian person to deliver n8n work to a US SMB is a different legal question from “exporting IT consultancy to Russia.” I did not find an OFAC FAQ that categorically bans paying a Russia-resident software contractor to work on a US client’s Zapier account. What I did find is operational blockage: Wise disabled USD–RUB (SafeBridge, 13 May 2026: https://safebridgeinsurance.com/en/blog/transfer-money-russia-to-usa-2026-post-sanctions [low-authority]); US banks and correspondents de-risk any Russia nexus; OFAC SDN screening is mandatory; “person located in Russia” is a location test, not a passport test (Fesenko, 18 Sep 2026: https://fesenkolaw.com/blog/person-located-in-russia-how-ofac-decides-this-status/ [low-authority law-firm blog]).

On 18 Sep 2026 the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 was signed. It codifies and hardens Russia financial sanctions, requires sanctions on foreign financial institutions that do significant business with covered Russian banks (Sberbank, VTB, Gazprombank and other state-linked banks), and adds tariff authorities. Implementation window 30 days (to 18 Oct 2026). https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/president-signs-bipartisan-russia-and-iran-sanctions-bill-into-law-key-provisions-of-the-lindsey-o-graham-sanctioning-russia-and-iran-act-of-2026 (29 Sep 2026). This makes third-country banks that still touch Russian rails a worse idea in October 2026 than they were in 2025.

Workarounds that exist in the wild, with friction:

Armenia / Georgia / Kazakhstan bank accounts and entities are the documented retail corridor for moving money around Russia (SafeBridge lists Ameriabank/Inecobank, TBC/Bank of Georgia, Halyk/Kaspi). Correspondent banks still freeze Armenia-origin wires on Russia-nexus alerts (Vardanyan & Partners, 23 Sep 2026: https://armenian-lawyer.com/business-immigration/international-payment-held-or-returned-armenia/). Armenia is simultaneously a US-blessed AI-infrastructure site (Firebird/Nvidia licenses) and a documented transshipment concern (Armenian Bar Association report, Sep 2026). Using an Armenian or Georgian company to employ people who have actually relocated there is a standard contractor structure. Using it as a thin pass-through for people still sitting in Moscow is the pattern Treasury has been designating third-country facilitators for (Treasury 15 Jan 2025 Kyrgyz/Keremet action: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2785; NYT 24 Sep 2026 on A7: https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/24/business/russia-sanctions-evasion-a7.html).

Practical recommendation from the sources, not from legal advice: do not contract Russia-resident individuals onto a US entity’s books; do not send them US SaaS admin access as “IT support” if FAQ 1187’s definitions can be read onto the work; prefer implementers who are tax-resident in the EU, Georgia, Armenia, Kazakhstan, or LatAm, paid to a local company, SDN-screened, with IP assignment in writing (Armenia contractor IP does not transfer on payment alone: https://armenian-lawyer.com/zh-CN/business-immigration/armenia-software-ip-ownership-audit/, 23 Sep 2026). This is a compliance and banking problem more than an hourly-rate problem. [emerging]

E. THE BPO TRAP

Sequoia’s live thesis is not a warning against services. Julien Bek, 5 Mar 2026: “The next $1T company will be a software company masquerading as a services firm.” Copilot sells the tool; autopilot sells the work. Start where the work is already outsourced. Software $1 / services $6. https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/

I did not find Sequoia using “BPO with a GPU bill.” The trap language in 2025–2026 comes from people reacting to that thesis.

The case that AI services become a worse BPO:

Foundation Capital (3 Jul 2025): every AI product now looks the same; differentiation is implementation; the pre-sales/post-sales line is gone; POCs require data ingestion, prompt tuning, live validation; “when a pilot fails, the vendor forfeits not only anticipated revenue but also the weeks of implementation work… and the non-trivial token expenses.” That is the BPO trap: human hours plus a GPU bill, with SaaS-like CAC and services-like COGS. https://foundationcapital.com/ideas/the-4-6t-services-as-software-opportunity-lessons-from-the-first-year [established as a serious VC field report]

Fortune / Bek (21 Apr 2026): GTM for services is unsolved; you cannot scale enterprise service sales the way you scale software sales; autopilot GM ~70% not 90%. Same Fortune URL as above.

a16z (4 Jun 2025): ServiceNow IPO GM 63.2%, Workday 54.1%; they later reached 79% and 75% after the system of record stuck. “Trading margin for moat” via forward-deployed engineers. https://a16z.com/services-led-growth/ The honest reading: early AI-service GM looks like a services firm on purpose.

ChartMogul (10 Dec 2025): AI-native GRR 40% unless price >$250/mo. Experimental ARR is not ARR. Professional services from FDEs get booked as if they were software. https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/

Gartner (25 Jun 2025): >40% of agentic AI projects canceled by end-2027, causes = escalating costs, unclear business value, inadequate risk controls — not model quality. Poll n=3,412 (Jan 2025). https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027 [established as a Gartner forecast]

Patrick Salyer (X, 8 Jul 2026): “Margins are not moats… Point a model at a labor-heavy workflow and the cost line drops. But if that’s all that happens, your advantage lasts exactly as long as it takes the next founder to apply the same models.” Crowded: AI implementation services, SDR-as-a-service, recruiting-as-a-service, back-office BPO. https://x.com/patricksalyer/status/2074939064361550057 [single-source]

The case that productized AI services escape the trap:

YC Spring 2026 RFS (documented 3 Feb 2026 by ProductMarketFit.tech and 21 Apr 2026 by Forbes; the live YC RFS page has since rotated to Fall 2026): agencies that use AI internally and sell finished outcomes can run at software-like margins. Forbes quotes YC partner Aaron Epstein: “instead of selling software to customers to help them do the work, you can charge way more by using the software yourself and selling them the finished product at 100x the price.” https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/04/21/ai-native-agencies-sell-outcomes-not-software-and-investors-are-paying-attention/ and https://www.productmarketfit.tech/p/just-dropped-yc-spring-2026-request (3 Feb 2026). Live YC page today: https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs/ (Fall 2026; AI-native agencies is not on the current page).

Funded autopilot precedents 2026 (these are product companies selling a service, not SMB n8n shops): Crosby $60M Series B Mar 2026, ~$400/document NDA/MSA; WithCoverage $42M Series B Jan 2026; Auctor $20M Sequoia-led 15 Apr 2026 (enterprise software implementation); Distyl $175M at $1.8B Sep 2025. Same Forbes piece.

Promethean: agencies that narrowed services hit 30% net vs 13% average. Specialization is the agency version of productization. https://prometheanresearch.com/2026-state-of-digital-services-digital-agency-industry-research/

Move at Pace (1 May 2026): one advised agency, £1.5M revenue, 38% GM and 65 founder hours/week → 52% GM and 38 founder hours after productizing, in six months. https://moveatpace.com/insights/productised-services-for-agencies/ [single-source] [low-authority]

Concrete markers that separate the two (synthesized from Foundation Capital, Promethean, Move at Pace, Rex, Alpenglow, ChartMogul):

Productized (>60% gross, low founder hours)
- One workflow, one vertical, price on the website
- Client-owned infra, shadow-mode acceptance, retainer is monitoring not “hours in a bucket”
- Delivery time falls with repetition (10th build is 30–50% of the first)
- Founder is not in Slack for every edge case
- Scope exclusions are written; new features are new milestones
- Evidence of this in 2026: Rex’s named $65–$2,000 retainers with change-control; Alpenglow’s $250/system; Promethean narrow-mix 30% net

Custom shop / BPO-with-GPU
- Every SOW is a unique integration
- Hours creep past the retainer (Crunch’s own warning: “under-utilised hours; ask for usage reporting” is the client-side view of the same problem)
- Founder still does discovery, QA, and angry-client calls
- Price is “it depends,” sales cycle is 8–16 weeks
- Gross margin quoted at 60% and actuals land at Promethean 13% net
- Token/API bill is blamed; the real COGS is humans (Automaton Agency’s own decomposition of a $5,000 retainer: $150–$600 model spend vs $1,500–$2,500 human review — https://automatonagency.com/insights/ai-automation-agency-economics, 8 Jun 2026 [low-authority], directionally consistent with Foundation Capital)

- Google at $524/lead needs ~5% close to match a $10k CAC, which is already 5 months of a $2,000 retainer.

G. SCALE CEILING AND EXIT

What $1M and $5M ARR look like, constructed from the retainer bands above:

$1M ARR ≈ $83k MRR.
- At $2,000 average retainer: ~42 clients.
- At $1,000: ~83 clients.
- At $5,000: ~17 clients.

A 42-client book at 5% monthly churn needs ~2 replacement closes a month. Delivery at 6 hours/client = 252 implementer hours/month ≈ 1.6 FTE offshore, plus a part-time US account lead. This is a small firm, not a product company. Promethean says this size (studio) is where net margins are highest (19% at <10 FTE in the 2025 cut).

$5M ARR ≈ $417k MRR.
- At $2,000: ~208 clients. That is an MSP-scale operations problem: CS, QA, on-call, QBRs. Promethean: 50+ FTE agencies average 8% net. The AI-automation blogs that promise $500k–$1M revenue per employee (demg.ai, MeasureU) were not backed by a named P&L in this run. [speculative]

Sellability, 2025–2026:

Agencies are sold on EBITDA/SDE, not ARR. FE International (20 May 2026): digital marketing agencies 3x–12x adjusted EBITDA by size; under $500k EBITDA typically 2.5x–4x SDE; $1–2.5M EBITDA 4x–6.5x; $5M+ EBITDA 7x–12x; recurring mix and vertical specialization move the multiple. Most digital agencies 3x–7x, strategic outliers 8x–12x. Revenue multiples 0.4x–1.0x, used as a floor. https://www.feinternational.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-valuation [established as a broker’s 2026 band]

Quiet Light live-adjacent example: $1.84M revenue, $1.11M SDE, 3.31x, 80% recurring, 12.4-year tenure. That is a cash-flow business a search-fund or SBA buyer will underwrite. It is not a venture outcome. https://quietlight.com/listings/16527071/

Marketplace asking prices (not closed): BigIdeasDB, 615 acquire.com listings, updated 26 Jun 2026: agency category median 1.10x revenue / 2.20x profit; SaaS 2.30x / 3.60x; AI 3.05x / 5.10x. Caveat on the page: asking prices, not closed. https://bigideasdb.com/state-of-saas-acquisitions-2026 [emerging]

SaaS M&A: Windsor Drake briefing cites private SaaS median ~4.1x TTM revenue in 2025, public SaaS ~3.4x by Mar 2026; Aventis via GigRadar: disclosed SaaS M&A 3.1x as of Mar 2026. https://gigradar.io/blog/saas-valuation-calculator (16 Aug 2026). Professional services/consulting 3x–6x EBITDA (Greenspoon Marder, 28 Sep 2026: https://www.gmlaw.com/news/difference-between-u-s-and-european-valuations/).

So: a productized automation agency with 80%+ retainer mix and low founder dependence can sell like a good digital agency (roughly 4x–6x EBITDA at $1M+ EBITDA, 2.5x–4x SDE below that). It will not sell like SaaS (3–5x revenue) unless it has actually become software. Marketplace agencies at 1.1x revenue are the dump bin for non-transferable books. [established as the multiple gap; [emerging] for where an AI-automation shop would land]

When an agency morphs into a fundable product company.

Precedents are autopilots in already-outsourced, intelligence-heavy verticals with a standardized work product: Crosby (NDAs at ~$400/document), WithCoverage (buy the insurance, don’t sell brokerage software), Auctor (implementation of enterprise software), Distyl (Fortune 500 outcomes). Forbes 21 Apr 2026, same URL as above. None of these is “we retain SMBs at $2k/mo to keep their n8n instance green.”

The morph happens when (Foundation Capital + Sequoia):
- the 10th delivery is a configuration of a product, not a project
- FDE work is harvested into reusable adapters
- pricing moves from hours to workflow- or outcome-based
- GRR looks like vertical SaaS (ChartMogul’s >$250/mo / 70% GRR band), not like a marketing agency

Until then this is a cash-flow business. That is not an insult. Quiet Light’s 3.31x on $1.1M SDE is $3.7M of liquidity with no dilution. It is also not a seed-stage story that helps the founder’s other raise. [emerging]

H. VERDICT

Given: founder time is the scarce resource (he is raising a seed for an agent-security product); an offshore Russian-speaking delivery partner exists; Valley presence and network exist; paid social is allowed.

Is this worth 5–10 hours/week as a cash engine?

Yes, on a narrow design. No, on the design most “AI automation agency” Twitter threads sell.

The design that can work in 5–10 founder hours/week [emerging, contingent]:

- One productized workflow in one vertical the Valley network already touches (accounting firms’ client-onboarding, or HVAC/plumbing missed-call / lead-follow-up). Not “AI-native your whole business.”
- Price on a page: $750–$1,500 setup + $250–$1,000/mo, client-owned tools, 30-day pause. That is where Rex and Alpenglow actually collect, not $5k.
- Founder time = partner conversations, close, QBR. Zero delivery Slack.
- Delivery = productized playbooks run by the offshore partner, from an EU/Georgia/Armenia/LatAm entity, not Russia-resident contractors on a US LLC. SDN screen. Written IP assignment.
- Acquisition = accountants and MSPs the founder already can reach in the Valley, not Facebook. Paid ads as a later test, not the engine.
- Kill the book if vintage-1 retention is vcita-like (half the churners gone by month 12) rather than MSP-like.

The design that burns the seed raise:

- Horizontal “we automate anything”
- $5k/mo SMB retainers sold on paid social
- Founder in delivery
- Russia-resident implementers billed from a US company into a post–18 Sep 2026 sanctions environment
- Counting 60–80% “net margins” from a blog as if they were Promethean

Unit economics that have to be true, or the 5–10 hours are a hobby:

- CAC (fully loaded, including founder time at a real hourly) < 2 months of retainer
- Delivery hours capped so gross stays ≥60% after offshore fully-loaded cost
- Monthly logo churn ≤2% (≈21% annual) or the 20-client treadmill starts before the book is interesting
- Founder hours stay in sales/partners, not in n8n debug

At 2% monthly churn, $2,000/mo, 15 clients = $360k ARR, ~$15k/mo gross after ~$3k delivery, against 20 founder-hours/month. That is a real cash engine. At 5% monthly churn the same book is a sales job. The first 10 clients will tell you which world you are in. [speculative as a forecast; the arithmetic is [established]]

Minimal viable test (2–4 weeks, <$2,000)

Week 0 design (already implied by prior DR): one workflow, one vertical, one page, one price. Example that matches published SMB invoices: missed-call SMS + CRM log for home-services, or lead-intake dedup + draft reply for a bookkeeping firm. Setup $1,000, retainer $500/mo, 14-day shadow mode, client-owned Make/n8n.

Week 1–2, $0–$400: founder personally books 12 conversations with people who already sell to the vertical (CPAs, bookkeepers, MSPs, one HVAC owner, one dental-office manager). Offer is a paid $500, 10-day audit that credits into setup. No ads yet. Network is the point of the Valley partner.

Week 2–4, remainder of budget: run up to 4 paid audits. Convert at least 1 to a retainer. Log delivery hours for the first 14 days of any live system. Do not hire. Do not spend the $2,000 on Facebook until 12 partner conversations have produced fewer than 3 audits.

Pass (economics look real):
- ≥3 paid audits in 4 weeks from partner intros
- ≥1 retainer signed
- First 14 days of delivery ≤6 implementer hours and 0 founder delivery hours
- Client can point to a number (recovered leads, hours saved) without a slide deck

Fail (walk away, cheaply):
- 12 partner intros, 0 paid audits: the offer is not a vendor-swap, it is a cold education sale. That will not run on 5–10 hours/week.
- Audits close, retainers do not: buyers will pay to learn, not to stay. That is vcita churn in miniature.
- Retainer signs, delivery hours >15 in week 1: you have a custom shop. Productize or stop.
- Payment or contracting with the offshore partner requires a Russia-resident individual on a US invoice: compliance fail. Reroute the entity before the second client.

Do not spend the $2,000 on ads first. Metadata will happily sell $145 Facebook leads. First Page Sage’s $1,047 Meta CAC is the number that matters, and it is a B2B average, not this offer. The 4-week test is a close-rate and hours-creep test. Those two numbers decide the venture. Ads cannot.
