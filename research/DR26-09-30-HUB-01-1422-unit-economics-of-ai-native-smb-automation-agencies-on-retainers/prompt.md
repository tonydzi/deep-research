# DR26-09-30-HUB-01-1422 — Unit economics of AI-native SMB automation agencies on retainers (churn, delivery cost, BPO trap)

CONTEXT (what we already know — do NOT re-research this baseline, build on it):
We already ran prior deep research (Aug 2026) that established: (a) the US SMB AI-automation consulting market exists; working offer ladder = fixed 14-day pilot $12-18k, then $3-5k/mo operations retainer; best verticals = accounting/bookkeeping firms, then HVAC/plumbing/electrical, then dental admin; (b) reality check: median US SMB spends ~$30/mo on AI services (JPMC Institute 2025), so "every pizzeria pays $10k/mo" is hype; (c) horizontal "AI-native your whole business" pitches fail, single-workflow offers win.

NEW QUESTION (the delta we need now):
We are evaluating a partnership: a Silicon Valley-based founder (brand, leads, vision) + a partner supplying inexpensive, high-quality Russian-speaking offshore engineers/implementers. The offer: AI-native automation for SMB (not enterprise) sold as monthly retainers. Advertising via Facebook/Google is possible (not crypto). We need the ECONOMICS of this agency model validated with real 2025-2026 data:

1. RETAINER REALITY. What monthly retainers do AI-automation agencies ACTUALLY collect from SMB clients (closed-deal evidence, not asking prices)? Distribution: $500/mo vs $2k/mo vs $5k/mo — which tier survives? Which SMB verticals sustain retainers longest?

2. CHURN. What is realistic monthly/annual churn for SMB automation retainers? How does it compare to (a) marketing agencies serving SMB, (b) MSPs (managed service providers), (c) vertical SaaS? At what churn does the retainer model stop compounding? What retention mechanics work (usage reporting, quarterly ROI reviews, embedding into workflows)?

3. DELIVERY COST & OFFSHORE MODEL. Fully-loaded cost of delivery per client per month using offshore (Eastern-European / LatAm / Asian) implementers vs US-based. Real hourly/monthly rates 2025-2026 for automation engineers (n8n/Make/Zapier/LLM-integration skill set) in these regions. What gross margin do AI-automation agencies actually run (evidence: interviews, podcasts, acquisition data, agency P&Ls)? Any legal/compliance friction for US clients with Russian-based contractors (sanctions, payments) — and standard workarounds (entities in Armenia/Georgia/Kazakhstan/EU)?

4. THE BPO TRAP. Sequoia and others warn AI-service companies become "BPO with a GPU bill": margins collapse as human hours creep in. What separates agencies that productize (>60% gross margin, low founder hours) from those that degrade into custom dev shops? Concrete markers and case studies 2025-2026.

5. ACQUISITION CHANNEL ECONOMICS. Does paid Facebook/Google acquisition actually work for selling SMB automation retainers? Real CAC figures, payback periods. Compare vs partner channels (accountants, MSPs) and content/community. Evidence from agency operators, not ad-platform marketing.

6. SCALE CEILING & EXIT. What do these agencies look like at $1M/$5M ARR? Are they sellable (multiples for agencies vs productized services vs SaaS, 2025-2026 M&A evidence)? Or is this strictly a cash-flow business? At what point (if any) does an agency morph into a fundable product company (precedents)?

7. VERDICT. Given: founder time is the scarcest resource (he is simultaneously raising a seed for an agent-security product), offshore delivery partner available, Valley presence and network available. Is this venture worth 5-10 hours/week of founder time as a cash engine? What is the minimal viable test (2-4 weeks, <$2k budget) that would prove/disprove the economics before committing?
