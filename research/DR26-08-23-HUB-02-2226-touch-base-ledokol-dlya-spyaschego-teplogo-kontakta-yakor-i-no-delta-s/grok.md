A. Executive Summary

The literature on dormant-tie reactivation and the industry data on “checking in” point in the same direction, and they resolve the live internal dispute more cleanly than sales-cadence folklore does.

A previously warm 1:1 can be reopened. Executives who reconnect dormant professional ties get novel information and are received more warmly than they expect (Levin, Walter & Murnighan, Organization Science, 2011, https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.1100.0574). People systematically overestimate the awkwardness of reaching out after years, and they systematically pick the wrong person to reopen (they choose who they liked, not who would actually know something; Walter, Levin & Murnighan, Organization Science, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2015.0996). That is the academic green light to re-contact.

It is not a green light to send an empty rhythm ping.

Almost every measured dataset that isolates message content says the same thing your canon already says: a no-news “it’s been a while, let’s catch up” is the worst class of professional message. Gong’s sales-email research (vendor, [single-source]) is the loudest industry source; relational-maintenance research is about preventing dormancy in an ongoing tie, not about resurrecting a quiet one; investor-update culture is a reporting relationship, not a peer DM. Levin’s actual finding is the opposite of the rhythm-without-delta school: a dormant tie keeps its value without being watered. You do not have to ping it to keep the right to use it.

So the standing rule, evidence-graded:

- WHEN still comes from rhythm / temperature / RFM. That part of the canon can stay.
- WHETHER to send, and WHAT to say, still require a concrete delta. If there is none, the correct first move is not a DM. It is silence, or a public-side substitute (a comment on their public artifact, a useful appearance in a shared space, a mutual intro). The only legitimate no-ask ritual in a RU/CIS messenger book is a genuine New Year note with one remembered detail and zero ask — and even that is hygiene, not revival.
- The first DM is 40–80 words (tighter than email because the notification preview is the subject line), channel-matched to the old thread, text-default, voice only if that dyad already used voice. Block 1 is the anchor, not the gap. Do not name “we spoke last spring” as the topic; name the last piece of substance. One easy reply path. No link, deck, Calendly, or call ask.
- Anchor rank, when you have a full history: (1) a promise you still owe, (2) an answer to a question they asked, (3) their stated problem or project if you have a real update, (4) a public milestone of theirs, (5) a specific opinion only they can give, (6) a mutual person only if that person is the reason. Never open by collecting on their unpaid social debt. Never open on “I saw you became active in three chats.”
- Status rewrites the ask, not the honesty. A VC / senior operator gets a one-line expert question or a useful delta, never a calendar. A peer engineer gets an async artifact. A former customer gets the product promise you kept or failed to keep. “Pick your brain” / “quick call to catch up” is the status-lowering move.
- At ~1 month this is a continuation, not a reactivation. At 6–12 months the four-block reactivation applies. At ~24 months add a one-line identity refresh (who you are in their world now) without a full cold intro — unless the original tie was thin (one hello), in which case it is a fresh introduction via content or a mutual, not “we spoke.”
- Do not steer to a call unless the dyad is call-native and the content actually needs a conversation. For async/engineering audiences the conversion target is a written reply, an intro, a try/review, or a one-line opinion. Booked-call rate is the wrong KPI on that segment.
- Realistic reply rates for dormant warm 1:1 DMs are not published for Telegram/WhatsApp. Transfer from warm email and recruiting InMail, discounted: a good anchored first touch often sits somewhere in the 20–45% reply band; an empty check-in in the mid-single-digits; a second untriggered bump well below the first. Treat those bands as [speculative] transfer, not as Telegram facts. Your own 8,600-contact corpus is a better experiment than anything cited below.
- AI may retrieve history and draft. A human (or a human-grade pass) must pick the anchor, write the notification line, kill surveillance phrasing, and decide to send. If 8,600 people get the same four-block cadence, they will notice.

Confidence in the core rule (no empty rhythm DM; trigger/delta or stay silent): [established] as a synthesis of dormant-tie research plus sales-message framing, with the honest caveat that almost no RCT exists on Telegram/WhatsApp 1:1 reactivation wording. The main assumption: Gong-style harm of empty check-ins and Levin-style “dormant ties survive without watering” both transfer to founder DMs in a small community.

B. Key Findings

B1. What is actually measured about the first re-contact (openers)

Finding. There is no published comparative RCT on Telegram or WhatsApp 1:1 reactivation wording, length, question-vs-statement, gap-naming, humor, quote-back, or voice vs text for dormant warm ties. [established] as an evidence hole. Industry numbers below are almost all email sales, LinkedIn InMail, or recruiting. Transfer them with a discount, do not treat them as messenger laws.

Finding. The closest academic object is not “subject line A/B.” It is dormant-tie reactivation as a network act. Levin, Walter & Murnighan (Organization Science, 2011, https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.1100.0574) showed that reconnecting a professional tie that had gone quiet yields advice that is more novel than advice from current ties, and that reconnecting is received better than the sender fears. MIT Sloan Management Review popularized the same study as “The Power of Reconnection — How Dormant Ties Can Surprise You” (Levin, Walter & Murnighan, 2011, https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-power-of-reconnection-how-dormant-ties-can-surprise-you/). [established] for the value of reconnecting; [established] that it does not prescribe wording.

Finding. Walter, Levin & Murnighan (Organization Science, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2015.0996) showed a selection error: people reopen the dormant ties they liked, not the ones most likely to hold useful knowledge. A light prompt to ask “whose knowledge is actually relevant now?” improved choice. [established] for anchor/person selection; still not a wording test.

Finding. Email-length benchmarks (older, not 2025–2026): Boomerang’s widely cited “ideal email” analysis (Boomerang Labs, 2016, [low-authority], https://blog.boomerangapp.com/2016/07/how-to-write-the-perfect-email-according-to-science/) reported higher reply rates in roughly the 50–125 word / 3–4 sentence band, with 1–3 questions beating zero questions and beating many questions. Lavender and other sales-email coaches still push ~50–125 words and one CTA in 2024–2025 vendor blogs ([low-authority], vendor-motivated). Gong’s later sales-email work (vendor, [single-source]; already in your adopted packet) found that short purpose-free check-ins produce dramatically fewer meetings than emails with a specific reason — the “~15x” figure you already adopted. None of this was run on Telegram DMs. Directionally it supports your 50–100 word cap. For messenger, the binding constraint is stricter: the lock-screen preview is ~40–70 characters. A first line that is “Hi Name, it’s been a while” wastes the only subject line you have. [emerging] as operational transfer; [speculative] on the exact 40–80 word DM band.

Finding. Question vs statement. Speech-act and politeness theory (Brown & Levinson, Politeness, Cambridge University Press, 1987) predict a trade-off that sales A/Bs keep rediscovering: a real question raises reply rate because it creates an obligation; it also raises imposition, which high-status recipients punish. Boomerang 2016 found 1–3 questions helped email replies ([low-authority], older). Gong-style sales advice is “one specific question, not ‘how are you.’” For a VC, a statement plus an optional one-line out (“no need to reply if this is noise”) protects face and will suppress raw reply rate. That is acceptable. Optimize for non-burning, not for maximum pings answered. [emerging] as a synthesis; no messenger RCT.

Finding. Naming the gap. No A/B found that isolates “we spoke last spring” vs not. [no source found] for a head-to-head. Two adjacent literatures pull opposite ways. Face-work (Goffman, Interaction Ritual, 1967) says making the lapse explicit is a face threat — you force both people to acknowledge neglect. Dormant-tie research says the awkwardness is mostly in the sender’s head (Levin et al., 2011). Practitioner pattern in fundraising and recruiting: name the last substance, not the calendar hole. “You were hiring a data lead” beats “it’s been too long.” [emerging] as practitioner consensus; [speculative] as a causal claim.

Finding. Using their past words. Sales-call research (Gong vendor studies on discovery calls, [single-source]) and classic mirroring/consistency (Cialdini, Influence, first ed. 1984, many later) support echoing the other person’s language. Written reactivation is different: a too-precise quote from 18 months ago (“on 12 March you said…”) reads as CRM surveillance. Paraphrase the topic; do not timestamp-quote. [emerging] for paraphrase; [speculative] that verbatim quote-back helps in DMs.

Finding. Self-deprecation / humor. No comparative data. [no source found]. Peer dyads sometimes tolerate one dry line about disappearing. With status-up targets it reads as lowering. Skip humor unless you would say it to their face in 4 seconds and already had that register with them. [speculative].

Finding. Voice vs text vs video. No reactivation A/B found for WhatsApp/Telegram. [no source found]. Adjacent constraints: WhatsApp Business Policy (ongoing; https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/business-policy) treats unsolicited commercial messaging as a policy risk on the Business API; personal 1:1 is looser but report-driven. Voice cannot be scanned; high-status and engineering recipients often hate unsolicited voice because it demands ear-time. In RU/CIS peer culture, voice is in-register if that thread already had voice. Video/Loom as a first reactivation is production-heavy and reads as a pitch. Default text. [emerging] as risk logic; [speculative] as reply-rate claim.

Finding. RU/CIS-specific evidence. No peer-reviewed Telegram 1:1 reactivation experiment found. [no source found]. Practitioner regularities (treat as [low-authority], not as science): Telegram is the default professional DM in RU/CIS tech; WhatsApp is more family/travel; voice notes are common among peers and odd toward seniors; New Year (31 Dec–8 Jan), not Christmas, is the culturally expected greeting window; ты/вы must copy the old thread; a templated “Давно не общались, как дела?” is the local “just checking in.”

B2. Anchor selection

Finding. The 2015 Organization Science reconnection-choice paper is the best evidence that which tie you reopen matters more than people think, and that unaided intuition picks poorly (Walter, Levin & Murnighan, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2015.0996). [established] for person choice. For which element inside a history to mention, there is no RCT. [no source found] for a ranked A/B of “unkept promise vs their problem vs mutual friend.”

Finding. Rank by face + relevance, not by CRM romance. Instrumental networking can feel “dirty” to the actor and is visible to the target (Casciaro, Gino & Kouchaki, Administrative Science Quarterly, 2014, https://doi.org/10.1177/0001839214554990). An opener that is only “I need something” will be read as such. An opener that pays down your own debt, or answers their old question, is instrumental and face-giving at the same time.

Operational rank, [emerging] as a synthesis, not as a field experiment:

1. A promise you made and did not keep. You still owe. This is the cleanest reopen. It does not ask them to work.
2. A question they asked that you can now actually answer. Their words, your delta.
3. Their stated problem or live project, but only if you have a real, specific update they could use this week.
4. A public milestone of theirs (a talk, a launch, a paper, a repo). Public is allowed; group-lurk telemetry is not.
5. A specific opinion only they can give, on an artifact that already exists (a sentence, a choice, a name), answerable in one line.
6. A mutual person, only when that person is the reason (news, an intro they wanted, a death/award/launch) — not “X says hi” as glue.

Do not rank high: a conference you both walked through; a generic “your space is hot right now”; their silence; their online-status; their activity in other private chats.

Finding. Weak vs strong. Granovetter (American Journal of Sociology, 1973, https://doi.org/10.1086/225469) and the trusted-weak-tie refinement (Levin & Cross, Management Science, 2004, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1030.0136) say weak/dormant ties are where novelty lives. The LinkedIn RCT (Rajkumar, Saint-Jacques, Bojinov, Brynjolfsson & Aral, Science, 2022, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abl4476) found moderately weak ties caused more job mobility than strong ones. [established] for networks; [speculative] as a message-anchor rule. Translation: do not only reopen the inner circle. Do not treat a one-hello conference contact at 24 months as a strong dormant tie.

B3. The no-delta case (the live dispute)

Claim to settle: if there is genuinely nothing new to say, is a ritual touch better than silence?

Evidence FOR ritual / no-news touch

- Investor-relations practice: LPs and boards treat silence as a negative signal; “nothing material this quarter” is a real update. First Round / fund-manager folklore and standard LP-update templates encode this ([low-authority] as science, [established] as IR custom).
- Relational-maintenance research (Stafford & Canary, early 1990s, e.g. Communication Monographs; later applications to workplaces) finds that routine, positivity, and openness keep ongoing relationships from decaying. [established] in that literature; designed on living ties, often romantic.
- Practitioner relationship-sales (Ferrazzi, Never Eat Alone, 2005/2014, [low-authority]; Adam Grant’s giving research, Give and Take, 2013) argues for information gifts and birthday/holiday hygiene.
- Mere-exposure (Zajonc, 1968) is sometimes invoked to justify periodic pings so you stay mentally available.

Evidence AGAINST, and why the FOR side is a false analogy here

- Levin et al. 2011 is direct: dormant ties remain valuable without continuous maintenance. That is the point of the construct. The “if I don’t ping I lose the right to ping” fear is the thing the study undermines. [established] in that paper.
- Gong-class sales data: purpose-free check-ins are the worst performing professional messages ([single-source] vendor, already adopted).
- IR “no news is news” requires a reporting relationship. A dormant 1:1 on Telegram did not buy a right to quarterly mail. Applying LP logic to peers is how you become “that person who always writes and never says anything.”
- Stafford/Canary maintenance is about not letting a living tie go dormant. You are past that point. Using it as a reactivation license is category error.
- Mere-exposure for a person who already chose silence is as likely to train habituation and muted notifications as it is to warm them.
- In a small community the reputation cost of empty pings is higher than in a 50,000-name SDR list. People screenshot.

Settlement. For revival (you want a reply, a thread, eventually maybe a call): silence is strictly better than a no-delta ritual DM. A “gift of information” or “specific opinion” is not a no-delta message — it is a delta. If you had to invent the question, it is a fake delta and will read as needy.

For hygiene, not revival: one carve-out, RU/CIS-specific, [emerging] as custom not as RCT — a New Year note, one remembered detail, zero ask, no second message if they do not reply. Birthday notes from a CRM feel automated in 2025–2026 and are not recommended as a revival engine.

If the goal is “don’t disappear from their world” without a delta: do it in public (your content, a comment on their public post, a useful remark in a shared group that is not obviously staged). That is a substitute, not a DM.

What would settle this forever: an RCT on your dormant book, n large enough per cell, four arms — silence / New Year zero-ask / empty “давно не общались” / anchored delta — 30-day reply, 90-day useful outcome, 180-day block/mute. Until that runs, do not let IR folklore overturn Levin + Gong.

B4. Status asymmetry

Finding. No Telegram RCT. [no source found]. Adjacent, consistent sources:

- Casciaro et al. 2014: visible instrumentality feels contaminating.
- VC follow-up folklore (operator blogs, 2010s–2020s, [low-authority]): only write when you have news or something useful for them; never “would love to bounce ideas”; never “pick your brain”; never “15 minutes whenever.”
- Engineering/devrel culture: unsolicited call asks and pitch DMs are a ban/report magnet; async artifacts are the respectful medium (practitioner consensus, [low-authority]).
- Politeness theory: up-status targets need imposition minimized (negative face). Peers tolerate solidarity and some humor (positive face).

What reads as needy / status-lowering, in descending reliability:

- “Quick call to catch up” with no payload.
- “Would love your brain / bounce ideas / grab coffee” toward a VC or CEO.
- Long voice note they did not ask for.
- Multi-channel chase (TG + WA + email + LinkedIn) after one silence.
- Reminding them they promised you something.
- Showing you track their private activity.
- Over-apologizing for silence (self-lowering without paying them anything).
- Sending Calendly to someone whose calendar is the scarce resource (already in your adopted packet).

What reads as peer-equal or status-safe:

- Paying down your promise.
- A one-line question they can answer from the metro.
- A useful fact about their problem, with no ask.
- Asking for an opinion on a named artifact, making them the expert.
- Easy out: “if this is noise, ignore.”
- Matching their old register (short, lowercase, ты/вы).

B5. Age of silence

Finding. Levin et al. 2011 included ties dormant for years and still found value. There is not a measured “after N months you must re-introduce.” [established] that multi-year dormancy is not a death certificate; [no source found] for 1 vs 6 vs 24 month message tests.

Operational bands, [emerging] as practice plus your RFM half-lives (45–75 days), not as RCT:

- 0–6 weeks: not dormant. Continue the old thread. Do not write a reactivation essay.
- 3–6 months: reactivation. Four-block, short, anchor on last substance. They still remember you.
- 6–12 months: same structure, slightly more context (one clause on what you are doing now that touches them). Still not a cold intro.
- 12–24 months: add a one-line identity refresh — who you are in their world today — because memory for the person survives better than memory for the last topic. Do not write a bio. Do not attach a deck.
- 24+ months, deep prior tie (many messages, a call, a favor): still a reactivation, not a cold intro. Lead with the old substance plus a six-word who-now.
- 24+ months, thin prior tie (one hello, one event, no favor): this is a fresh introduction. Do not pretend warmth you do not have. Use public content, a mutual, or a new legitimate reason. A “we met at X two years ago” DM without a delta is a cold DM with extra steps.

B6. Call or not

Finding. Your adopted packet already covers warm-yes → booked-call (specific slots beat “when are you free”; bare Calendly is risky with status-heavy contacts; breakup is honest cleanup). The gap is when a revived thread should never be pushed to a call.

When not to push ([emerging] practitioner + audience norms):

- The person is an engineer, OSS maintainer, researcher, or otherwise async-native. A call is a tax. Charity-Majors-style operator culture and DevRel practice treat “let’s hop on a call” as the tell that you did not do the written work ([low-authority], consistent).
- The ask fits in three messages (an opinion, an intro, a yes/no, a URL they requested).
- You do not yet have a reason that survives a recording. If you would be embarrassed to have the call transcribed, you wanted a meeting for the meeting.
- They replied with information, not with heat. Informational replies are not buying signals.
- They are status-up and have not offered time.
- Time zones / travel make a call a bigger ask than the relationship currently supports.

Alternative conversion targets, in order of typical imposition:

1. A one-line opinion or correction.
2. A written update you can log (CRM truth).
3. A forward / intro (they spend social capital; only ask if the intro is for them or clearly easy).
4. They try / review / comment on an artifact async.
5. A voice note they send back (their choice of medium).
6. A call, only after they bite or they propose it.

B7. Measurement and stop rules

Finding. Published reply-rate ranges for dormant warm Telegram/WhatsApp 1:1s: [no source found]. Transfer bands, [speculative], from warm email, recruiting follow-up, and founder anecdotal books — discount for 2024–2026 inbox collapse on email/LinkedIn, which is worse than Telegram 1:1 from a known human:

- Anchored first DM to a truly warm dormant tie: perhaps 20–45% any-reply.
- Empty check-in: perhaps 5–15%, and a worse useful-reply rate than that.
- Second untriggered bump: often half or less of the first, with a fat right tail of mute/block risk.
- Cold email 2024–2025 vendor dashboards (Instantly / Smartlead / similar, [low-authority], declining): ~1–5% reply. Do not use cold-email rates as your target; they are a different sport.
- LinkedIn InMail vendor figures are not comparable to Telegram.

What counts as failed revival:

- No reply after the allowed touches.
- A courtesy “thanks, all good” with no question back (polite close).
- Mute, block, report, or “please don’t write.”
- A reply that dies when you ask for a call (you converted the revival into a pipeline assault).

Stop rules, adjusting your current “3 non-triggered”:

- Status-up, small community, engineers: 1 anchored touch, 0 bumps unless the first message had a dated opinion ask (“need a one-liner by Thursday”). Then one bump, then stop. [emerging]
- Former customers who bought: 1 + 1, then close-the-file. A third untriggered is where “vendor who won’t leave” starts.
- True peers with a deep history: 1 + 1 is enough. A third empty bump is the reputation tax.
- Keep “stop after 3” only as a hard ceiling, not as a target. Three is the point at which sales sequencers have already overfit to email, where extra bumps are cheap. In Telegram they are not.

Reputation cost of over-touching in a small community is not a published elasticity. [no source found]. The mechanism is real: screenshots, side-chats, “he CRM-pings everyone.” One well-aimed miss is forgotten. A pattern of empty DMs becomes a nickname. Report-driven platform risk is separate (see E).

B8. AI-drafted touches

Finding. Recipients’ ability to detect AI prose is imperfect and biased (Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman, PNAS, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2208839120). [established] that human detection is flawed. It does not follow that AI DMs are safe. By 2024–2025 the failure mode shifted from “this is clearly GPT” to “this is the same personalized template I got from six other people” — real detail slotted into a machine cadence: I saw your post about X, loved your take on Y, would love to explore Z. [emerging]

Finding. No clean public A/B found that isolates AI-draft vs human-draft on dormant warm Telegram DMs. [no source found]. Adjacent: mass AI-personalization coincided with falling cold-email and LinkedIn reply rates in vendor dashboards 2023–2025 ([low-authority], confounded by volume, filters, and model collapse of style). Warm 1:1 Telegram from someone they actually know is still less polluted than LinkedIn. That is an asset. Burning it with recognizable AI cadence is expensive.

Finding. EU AI Act transparency for automated interaction (Art. 50 obligations phased from 2025–2026) is about disclosed automated systems, not about a founder’s assistant drafting a message the founder sends. Do not confuse a legal label with a reply-rate effect. If a system is posting autonomously at scale, disclosure and platform rules matter; if a human hits send on an edited draft, the practical risk is quality and pattern-leak, not Art. 50. [emerging] as legal context; not a wording finding.

What must stay human (or human-grade):

- The decision to send at all.
- Choice of anchor (this is the 2015 paper’s actual skill).
- The first 40–70 characters (notification line).
- Killing surveillance phrasing and fake-delta questions.
- Register: ты/вы, length, whether voice is even thinkable.
- Variation. If the 4-block logic is visible as the same four beats across hundreds of people, the community will detect the system even if every sentence is grammatical.

AI is good for: retrieving the last substance from the thread, proposing three ranked anchors, listing promises you made, flagging “this is a check-in,” drafting, and scanning for links/Calendly/call-asks you forbade.

C. Contradictions & Debates

Debate 1. “Water the tie or let it sleep.”
Rhythm-relationship school (IR updates, Ferrazzi, some family-office managers): a quiet signal beats a two-year gap because memory and mere-exposure decay.
Dormant-tie school (Levin 2011): the gap is the feature; you can reopen when you have a reason; constant watering is unnecessary.
Steelman of the weaker-for-this-use-case side (rhythm): your book is not an academic advice-network. It is also a pipeline. People forget founders who only appear when they want something. A New Year note or a true information gift keeps optionality.
Verdict for 1:1 founder DMs: Levin wins on revival messages; rhythm may win as public presence (content, events). Do not use pipeline anxiety to justify empty DMs.

Debate 2. Questions raise replies vs questions impose.
Email A/Bs like questions. Politeness theory and VC practice hate being asked for time.
Verdict: one easy question for peers and former customers; for status-up, prefer a useful statement plus an optional one-liner, or a question that makes them the expert and costs one sentence.

Debate 3. Name the gap (honesty) vs skip the gap (no face threat).
Verdict: never make the gap the topic. Name the last substance. That is honesty without apology-theater.

Debate 4. Personalized tracking is “I care” vs “you are watching me.”
CRM vendors sell activity-based triggers. Recipients in private messengers experience group-activity and last-seen triggers as surveillance.
Verdict: public artifacts are fair game. Private-group telemetry is not.

Debate 5. Voice is more human vs voice is ruder.
CIS peers: sometimes more human. Seniors and async engineers: ruder, because unscannable.
Verdict: match the dyad’s old medium; default text.

Debate 6. AI personalization at scale vs one human sentence.
Vendors sell the former. 2025–2026 recipient immune system punishes the former.
Verdict: AI retrieval + human send decision. Do not autonomously drip 8,600 people with the same skeleton.

D. Best Practices

Standing rule (replaces the unresolved half of “rhythm = when, delta = what”):

Rhythm / RFM may put a name on today’s list. A concrete delta is required to take them off the list into a send. No delta → no DM. Public substitute or wait.

The first message

- Channel: the old channel. Do not add WhatsApp to a Telegram tie on day one. Do not add email. One door.
- Length: 40–80 words, 3–6 short lines. 50–100 remains acceptable; the extra 20 words have to earn their keep.
- First line: the anchor, because it is the notification preview. Not “Hi,” not “давно не писали.”
- Structure (keep your 4-block as internal logic, hide it as prose): recall anchor → one-clause now (only if it touches them) → why this week (the delta) → easy reply path (a one-line question or an easy out).
- Forbidden in touch #1: links, decks, Calendly, “15 min,” “catch up,” “how have you been,” surveillance, collecting on their debt, humor you would not use to their face, voice if they never used voice, video.
- Pronouns and language: copy the old thread (ты/вы, RU/EN). Do not upgrade formality; do not suddenly become American-sales polite.
- Timing: their event, not your sequence clock. If you must pick an hour, their local late morning or late afternoon; not a Monday 09:00 blast, not Friday night, not a 200-message batch at one timestamp (that is how you look like a robot to anyone who compares notes).

Anchor picker (apply in order, first match wins)

1. Did we promise something we still have not done? Open by doing it or paying it.
2. Did they ask a question we can now answer?
3. Is there a public milestone of theirs this week we genuinely have a thought about?
4. Do we have a specific, current delta on their old problem/project?
5. Is there a one-line opinion only they can give on an artifact that exists today?
6. Is there a mutual person who is the actual reason?
7. If none of the above: do not send. Put them back to wait / public-side.

Two moves after

Move 2 — they replied.
Read the reply before you reuse the goal. Branch:
- Heat or a question back: answer the content fully. Only then, if they are call-native and the topic needs it, offer two specific slots or ask if a call is even useful. Do not paste Calendly at status-up.
- Useful substance, no heat: thank, log, maybe one clarifying line. Stop. You already won a revival.
- Polite-cold (“thanks, all good”): close warmly, one sentence, no ask. Do not “while I have you.”
- They propose a call: then use your adopted booking rules (two or three slots, not “when are you free”).

Move 2b — they did not reply, and the first message had a real dated ask.
One bump, shorter than the first, that restates only the one-liner and the deadline. Then stop.

Move 2c — they did not reply, and there was no deadline.
No bump. Wait for a new trigger. Optional public-side comment if they publish something. Do not change channels this week.

Move 3 — still nothing, or the thread revived and died on your call-ask.
Close the file honestly (“I’ll leave this. If X happens, I’ll write”) or simply stop. Do not send the third untriggered “just bumping.” Your breakup/close-the-file rule already covers the honest cleanup. Use it as cleanup, not as a conversion trick.

Status skins (same skeleton, different ask)

- VC / senior operator: delta or expert one-liner. No call. Easy out. Shorter than 60 words.
- Peer founder: you can ask for a one-line take, or admit a stuck point. Still no calendar in #1.
- Peer engineer: written artifact; conversion = comment/try/intro, not Zoom.
- Former customer: the thing they bought or asked for. Outcome check. If you shipped their request, that is the delta.
- Thin old tie at 24 months: do not use this playbook. Use a fresh, honest reason or a mutual.

Twelve opener skeletons
Each is a skeleton, not a mail-merge. Fill with a real noun from their history. EN first, RU second. Situation in brackets.

1. Our unpaid promise
EN: “I still owe you the intro to [Name] I promised after [event]. They said yes this week — want me to connect, or is that stale?”
RU: “я до сих пор должен тебе интро к [Имя], как обещал после [событие]. на этой неделе они сказали да — соединить или уже неактуально?”
[Best first reopen. We pay down debt. Easy yes/no.]

2. Answer to their old question
EN: “You asked how we handled [X] on-device. We finally have a boring answer: [one sentence]. If that’s still on your plate, I can send the two-line version.”
RU: “ты спрашивал, как мы делаем [X] на устройстве. появился скучный ответ: [одно предложение]. если это ещё на столе — могу двумя строками.”
[They set the agenda months ago. No call.]

3. Their stated problem, real delta
EN: “Last time you were stuck on [problem]. We just hit [specific result] on the same thing. Not pitching — one detail that would have saved us a month: [detail]. Useful, or ignore.”
RU: “тогда ты упирался в [проблема]. у нас только что [конкретный результат] на том же месте. это не питч — одна деталь, которая сняла бы нам месяц: [деталь]. надо / не надо.”
[Only if the detail is real this week.]

4. Public milestone of theirs
EN: “[Piece] landed. The line on [specific bit] is the one I keep thinking about. If you ever want a sharp read from someone who tried [related], I have three sentences. No need to reply.”
RU: “[Вещь] вышла. фраза про [конкретное] сидит в голове. если нужен короткий разбор от человека, который сам трогал [рядом] — есть три предложения. можно не отвечать.”
[Public artifact only. Easy out. Status-safe.]

5. One-line expert opinion (status-up)
EN: “Need a one-liner, not a meeting. We’re choosing between [A] and [B] for [named constraint]. You’ve done this. A or B?”
RU: “нужна одна строка, не созвон. выбираем [A] или [B] из-за [ограничение]. ты это уже делал. A или B?”
[Makes them the expert. VC-safe if the choice is real.]

6. Peer stuck-point
EN: “I’m stuck on [narrow thing] and you were the last person who talked sense about it. If you have a one-line ‘don’t do X,’ I’ll take it. If not, I’ll stop.”
RU: “я застрял на [узкое] — ты последний, кто говорил по делу. если есть одно ‘не делай X’ — возьму. если нет, отстану.”
[Peers only. Self-limit is the status protection.]

7. Former customer / shipped your request
EN: “You asked for [feature/fix] and we didn’t have it. It exists now. If [company] still cares, I’ll send a 4-line note on what it does not do yet.”
RU: “ты просил [фича/фикс], у нас этого не было. сейчас есть. если [компания] это ещё волнует — пришлю 4 строки, включая то, чего оно всё ещё не умеет.”
[Product promise. Honest limitation builds trust.]

8. Former customer / outcome check (no pitch)
EN: “Did [problem] ever get solved on your side, or did you route around it? Asking because we’re killing the approach we used when we spoke — I don’t want to learn the wrong lesson.”
RU: “у вас [проблема] в итоге решилась или обошли? спрашиваю, потому что мы хороним тот подход, с которым к тебе приходили — не хочу выучить неправильный урок.”
[Makes them the teacher. Not a win-back paragraph.]

9. Mutual person as the actual reason
EN: “[Mutual] is in [city] next week for [reason] and asked who else cares about [topic]. I thought of you before I thought of a list. Should I connect, or not your thing anymore?”
RU: “[Общий] будет в [город] на следующей неделе из-за [причина] и спросил, кому ещё это надо. я подумал о тебе раньше, чем о списке. соединить или уже не твоё?”
[Only if true. Fake “X says hi” is a known cheap trick.]

10. Long gap, deep tie, identity refresh
EN: “It’s [your name] — still [one clause they would recognize]. You were in the middle of [their project]. If that world is still yours, I have one specific question: [one-liner]. If you’ve moved off it, ignore.”
RU: “это [имя] — всё ещё [одно узнаваемое]. ты тогда был в середине [их проект]. если этот мир ещё твой — один конкретный вопрос: [однострочник]. если уже нет — не отвечай.”
[12–24 month deep ties. Not for thin hellos.]

11. New Year hygiene, zero ask (not a revival engine)
EN: “Happy New Year. I still remember [specific 6-word detail from your thread]. Hope [year] is quieter than ours. No ask.”
RU: “с новым. я до сих пор помню [конкретная деталь]. пусть [год] будет спокойнее, чем у нас. без просьб.”
[CIS window. If they do not reply, you do not follow up.]

12. Close the file (honest, not a trick)
EN: “I’ll close this on my side so I’m not the person who pings. If [specific future event] happens, I’ll write once. Until then, good luck with [their project].”
RU: “закрываю у себя, чтобы не быть тем, кто пингует. если случится [конкретное], напишу один раз. до тех пор — удачи с [их проект].”
[After allowed touches, or when you refuse to fake a delta.]

What not to send (anti-skeletons)

- “Hey, it’s been a while, how are you?”
- “Давно не общались, как дела?”
- “Just checking in / touching base / circling back.”
- “I saw you were active in three chats.”
- “Would love to catch up on a quick call.”
- Forwarded channel post with “thought of you.”
- Birthday-CRM one-liner plus a pitch.
- Voice note reading a template.

E. Failure Modes

Messages and cadences that burn

- Empty check-ins. Worst class in sales-email data (Gong, vendor, [single-source]); worst texture in messenger too.
- Cadence-first sequences imported from Outreach/Salesloft/HubSpot win-back drips. Those tools frame this as marketing re-engagement (5–8 step email). That frame is a substitute you should not copy. HubSpot/Outreach “re-engagement sequence” language is built for lists, not for 1:1 social capital.
- Third untriggered bump in a small community.
- Call-ask in message 1, or call-ask as the only Move 2.
- Multi-channel pursuit after silence.
- Collecting on their promise.
- Over-personalization from a dossier (“as you told me on 12 March at 18:04”).
- Identical 4-block to hundreds of people in the same week.

What reads as spam vs needy vs surveillance

- Spam: templates, links, batches, forwarded posts, product paragraphs, wrong channel, Business-API-shaped blasts on WhatsApp.
- Needy: catch-up call, “pick your brain,” apology for existing, bumping because your calendar said so, asking them to do work to make you feel current.
- Surveillance: last-seen, online, “you became active,” quoting private-group behavior, “our CRM flagged you.” Public post comments are not this. Private telemetry is.

Platform-level risk (Telegram / WhatsApp)

- Telegram spam is report-driven. FAQ: https://telegram.org/faq (spam section; Telegram, living document). Flood-waits, 2FA challenges, and peer-to-peer reports matter more than a published RPM. Mass-similar DMs from one account are the pattern. Your 8,600 book is a ban-shaped object if you send like an SDR sequence.
- WhatsApp: Business Policy (https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/business-policy) restricts unsolicited commercial messages on the Business API; the 24-hour session window is a hard constraint there. Personal WhatsApp is looser and still report-bannable. Messaging someone after 12–24 months of silence can look, to the recipient and to the classifier, like a cold commercial DM.
- Do not use WhatsApp Business templates as a “warm reactivation” channel unless they actually wrote you inside the window.
- Never buy TG/WA lists, never scrape “who went online,” never automate send without per-message human (or human-grade) send/no-send.

Competitive substitutes that often beat a DM when you have no delta

- A comment on their public post / repo / talk, specific, in your voice.
- Being useful in a shared group without making the group a stage.
- A mutual intro from someone currently hot with both of you.
- Your public content that they can see without being pinged (this is the actual rhythm channel).
- An event where they already are.
- For engineers: a patch, a gist, a reproduction, a review — then a DM that points at the artifact, not at a call.

Tools to ignore as models: CRM “win-back,” “we miss you” SaaS emails, LinkedIn automation, birthday cadences, “warm intro” products that turn a thin meeting into a 24-month fake intimacy.

F. Strategic Recommendations

Named options

Option 1 — Strict trigger/delta (hard line)
Send only when an anchor in B2/D hits. Otherwise silence.
Trade-off: you will send less than a rhythm calendar wants; some people will forget your name; you will almost never be the screenshot. Risk: under-touching true fans who would have liked a New Year note. Fits your canon.

Option 2 — Strict trigger plus two carve-outs
Option 1 + (a) CIS New Year zero-ask with one remembered detail, no follow-up, and (b) genuine information gifts that would be sent even if the person could not buy or intro anything.
Trade-off: small extra send volume; requires a brutal “would I send this if they were broke and status-down?” test to kill fake gifts. Risk: carve-outs rot into weekly “value” templates.

Option 3 — Rhythm pings with rotating value templates
The sales-engagement default. Keep-alive every N days.
Trade-off: calendar feels full; reply rates and reputation both fall; platform-risk rises; AI will happily generate the empty calories. Risk: this is how you become a case study in E.

Option 4 — Public-first when no delta
No DM. Comment, content, group, mutual, event. DM only after they respond in public or a real delta appears.
Trade-off: slower, less pipeline-shaped, more status-safe. Risk: you under-work people who never post publicly (many serious operators).

Option 5 — Run the book as an RCT, then freeze the winner
You have ~8,600 dormant 1:1s. Literature does not. Four arms (silence / empty ritual / New Year zero-ask / anchored 4-block), stratified by temperature band and role (VC / engineer / customer / peer), primary metric useful reply at 30 days, safety metric mute/block, secondary booked-call only on call-native strata.
Trade-off: takes a cycle; requires discipline not to “help” the treatment. Risk: leaking the experiment if messages are obviously different in the same week in a small community — mitigate with time-stagger and register variation.

Single recommendation

Take Option 1 as the send rule, Option 4 as the no-delta path, Option 2 only for the New Year carve-out (not for birthday, not for “quarterly”), and Option 5 as the first build.

Confidence: [emerging].
Main assumption: Levin’s “dormant ties do not need watering” and Gong’s “empty check-ins fail” both transfer to founder Telegram/WhatsApp 1:1s in a small community, and your RFM rhythm is a queue, not a license to speak.

Priorities (what actually changes the playbook)

P0. Freeze “no delta, no DM.” Write it as a send-gating rule, not as a slogan. The AI drafter must be able to output SEND / DO NOT SEND with the failed anchor named.
P0. Notification-line rule: first 40–70 characters = anchor, never gap-apology.
P0. Surveillance ban-list in the drafter: last-seen, online, group activity, “you’ve been quiet,” exact old timestamps.
P1. Status skins and conversion-target skins (call vs one-liner vs artifact vs intro). Booked-call is not the north star for engineers.
P1. Tighten stop rule: ceiling 3 remains; default is 1 + optional dated bump. Third untriggered is a bug.
P1. Voice/video policy: text default; voice only if the dyad already used it; no video on first reopen.
P2. New Year zero-ask as the only ritual, human-chosen detail, no follow-up.
P2. RCT design on a slice of the 8,600, not a new philosophy debate.
P2. Public-side substitute checklist for the no-delta pile so “we did nothing” is not the only alternative.

Roadmap

Week 0: Gate the drafter (SEND/NO SEND, anchor rank, ban-list, first-line, no links/Calendly/call in #1).
Week 1: Status and conversion skins. Stop-rule change. Log every send with: age-of-silence, role, anchor type, question-vs-statement, reply-at-7d, useful-at-30d, negative-at-30d.
Weeks 2–6: Send only gated messages. Do not backfill empty rhythm to “catch up the queue.”
Next CIS New Year: run carve-out as a labeled campaign, zero asks, measure mute/block separately from revival rate (success for this arm is non-harm, not meetings).
Next quarter: read the log. If empty messages somehow got through and they did not underperform, you have a real contradiction and you reopen Debate 1 with data. If they underperform, the dispute is over.

G. Action Plan

For the founder

1. Treat the dormant book as a queue of people, not a sequence of messages.
2. Before any reopen, name the anchor out loud. If you cannot, you do not send.
3. Never ask a status-up contact for a catch-up call. Ask a one-line thing or give a useful thing.
4. After a reply, answer the human. Do not route them into your calendar because the CRM stage says “book.”
5. Accept that some valuable people will go 24 months without a DM from you. That is Levin, not neglect.

For the AI assistant drafting on his behalf (standing rule)

1. Retrieve: last substance, promises we made, their problem/project, public artifacts, role/status, age of silence, old channel, ты/вы, whether voice existed in-thread.
2. Rank anchors 1–6. If none clear: output DO NOT SEND and one public-side substitute. Stop.
3. Draft 40–80 words in the old register. First line = anchor. One question or a clean easy-out. Zero links, zero calendar, zero surveillance.
4. Self-check: would this still be sent if they cannot buy, intro, or take a call? If no, it is a fake delta — kill it.
5. Show the founder (or a human-grade send pass) three things only: the notification line, the ask, the send/no-send reason. Not a 400-word memo.
6. Log the send. Never autonomous-blast.
7. Move 2 is a new reasoning pass on their actual reply, not “continue sequence.”

First-week experiment if you want data instead of another argument

- Draw 400 truly warm dormant 1:1s (depth high, not thin hellos), stratified.
- 100 silence, 100 empty ritual (explicitly the thing you believe is bad), 100 anchored 4-block, 100 opinion-ask without other news.
- Hold status mix constant.
- Do not put call-asks in any arm.
- Read 7/30/90 day reply, useful reply, negative.
- Pre-commit: empty arm wins only if useful-reply is not worse and negative is not worse. Reputation, not meetings, is the safety metric.

Until that experiment exists, do not send no-news DMs.
