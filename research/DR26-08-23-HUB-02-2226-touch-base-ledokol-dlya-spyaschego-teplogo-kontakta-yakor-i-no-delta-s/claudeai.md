# "Touch Base" — Reactivating a Dormant Warm 1:1 Contact: Evidence-Graded Operational Playbook

*Research conducted 2026‑08‑26. Scope: solo founder, relationship-based B2B, Telegram/WhatsApp DM, thousands of contacts dormant 3–24 months.*

---

## A. Executive Summary

The academic literature on dormant ties is unusually good news for your use case: reconnecting with a lapsed contact is not a lesser substitute for a live relationship — multiple controlled studies show dormant ties often outperform active ties in the value of advice/information they provide, precisely because they've spent the gap acquiring different knowledge and networks ([Levin, Walter & Murnighan, 2011](https://business.gwu.edu/sites/g/files/zaxdzs5326/files/15_FP.SP_Walter.J_15levin_2011a.pdf); [established]). The single most important, most directly actionable finding for your anchor-selection question is a documented **mismatch**: people systematically pick which dormant tie to revive based on *comfort* (how much time they spent together before), while the ties that actually deliver value are the ones offering *novelty* — limited past interaction and/or higher status — plus basic trustworthiness ([Levin et al., "Reconnection Choices," *Organization Science*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2015.0996); [established]). Applied to your status-asymmetry question, this reframes the VC/senior-operator dormant tie from "scary, skip it" to "systematically higher-value, worth the discomfort."

On openers, there is no controlled experiment on *dormant-tie DM reactivation specifically* — that exact cell (Telegram/WhatsApp, previously-warm 1:1, 3–24 months silent) has **[no source found]** as a studied unit anywhere in the literature or industry benchmarking I could locate. Everything below is triangulated from three adjacent, well-measured bodies of evidence: (1) B2B cold/warm email and LinkedIn outbound data (large N, real reply-rate data, but different channel and different — usually never-warm — relationship), (2) social psychology of reconnection and liking (small-N lab studies, but directly on-topic for *why* reconnection works or fails), and (3) practitioner playbooks from recruiting, fundraising/LP relations, and nonprofit donor reactivation (real operational experience, weak methodological transparency). Where these three bodies agree, confidence is highest; where they visibly disagree (e.g., ideal message length), I present both sides rather than average them away.

On the no-delta ritual-touch dispute your team is having internally: the evidence leans toward **"a ritual touch can work, but only if it does real interpersonal work" — plain "long time no see, how are you" is close to worthless, but a touch built around asking a specific opinion, marking a real anniversary, or sending something of genuine information value is not the same category and has real (if thin, cross-domain) support.** This is not a clean settlement of your dispute; it's a reframe: the fight isn't "rhythm vs. delta," it's "does this specific ritual touch smuggle in a delta or not."

On AI-drafted touches: a large 2026 peer-reviewed study is directly on point and genuinely useful — recipients essentially cannot detect AI-authored personal messages unaided, but the moment they are *told* a message was AI-written, they rate the sender as lazy and insincere ([Molnar & Zhu, 2026, *Computers in Human Behavior*](https://theconversation.com/most-people-do-not-realize-when-a-personal-message-they-receive-was-written-by-ai-study-finds-278874); [established]). This has direct implications for your voice-as-Mycroft disclosure canon (§3.3) discussed below.

## B. Key Findings

### 1. Openers — what's measured, what isn't

**What genuinely has evidence behind it:**

- **Length is contested, not settled.** Gong's own outbound-email data claims long emails (30–150 words) are "15x more effective" at booking meetings than short ones ([Gong, cold email guide](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-email-examples); [single-source][low methodological transparency] — I fetched the underlying page and could not find sample size, exact length bands, or methodology behind that 15x figure). This directly **contradicts** Woodpecker's dataset of 20M+ emails, where sub-80–100-word messages show ~50% higher response engagement, and the wider 2025–2026 cold-email benchmarking consensus that shorter, single-CTA messages outperform ([Woodpecker, 2026](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/); [emerging], cross-corroborated by multiple aggregators). See Section C for how to hold both.
- **Personalization depth reliably beats generic templates, at large effect sizes, across every channel measured.** Cold email: ~7–9% generic vs. 17–18% with advanced personalization ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/); [emerging]). LinkedIn: a personalized connection note nearly doubles reply rate (9.36% vs. 5.44%) ([Belkins 2025 study, via belkins.io](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study); [emerging][single vendor study]). AI-signal-based prospecting: 5–25% vs. ~3% baseline ([Autobound, 2026 state-of-AI-prospecting report](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/state-of-ai-sales-prospecting-2026); [emerging][vendor-produced]).
- **Naming the gap explicitly, using shared history, is directly supported by the dormant-tie reconnection literature** — the "Remembering" element (reminiscing about shared experience) is one of three necessary ingredients Levin's team found for a reconnection to feel authentic rather than superficial ([Rondi, Levin & De Massis, 2023/24, *Organization Science* 35(2):573-600](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2023.1685); [established]). This is strong independent support for your existing 4-block "recall anchor" opener structure.
- **Asking a question vs. making a statement:** no controlled comparison exists for reconnection messages specifically [no source found]. The closest proxy — Gong's finding that *asking for interest* rather than *asking for a specific time/date* roughly doubles meeting-booking odds in cold outreach ([Gong cold-email guide](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-email-examples); [emerging]) — argues for ending on an open, low-friction question rather than a scheduling ask, which matches your existing "easy reply path" block.
- **Using the person's own past words back at them** has no direct study, but is strongly supported by two adjacent bodies: the reconnection literature's "Remembering" requirement, and the self-disclosure/liking literature, where mirrored, reciprocal disclosure measurably increases liking and closeness in strangers ([Sprecher, Treger & Wondra, 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0265407512459033); [established] for the underlying mechanism, [speculative] as applied to reconnection specifically).
- **Self-deprecation/humor about the gap ("this is objectively a long time — no excuse, just glad you're doing X"):** no study found [no source found]. Practitioner and RU-language advice sources converge informally on honesty about the awkwardness being disarming rather than damaging — "будьте честными и открыто заявляйте о своем желании наладить общение" ("be honest and openly state your wish to reconnect") ([fb.ru advice piece](https://fb.ru/post/friendship/2022/3/7/350790); [low-authority]) — but this is advice-column material, not measured.
- **Voice note vs. text vs. video:** this is the weakest-evidenced sub-question in the whole brief. No controlled study compares voice notes to text for *reconnection* messages [no source found]. The nearest real data is from a different category entirely — personalized *video* messages in cold sales outreach show 10–16% reply, up to 30% with deep personalization, vs. 1–3% for cold text email ([Vidyard/Loom/BombBomb vendor data, cross-corroborated by 3 vendors](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/top-10-video-outreach-platforms-for-sales-2026); [emerging][vendor-sourced]) — but that's stranger-cold, not warm-dormant, and video is a much heavier ask for a first re-contact than a voice note. Voice notes are a strong cultural norm in Telegram/CIS messaging generally, but I found no measurement of their effect on dormant-tie reply rates specifically — treat this as a channel-fit hypothesis, not evidence.

**8–12 opener skeletons, evidence-graded by which finding each one leans on:**

1. **Unkept-promise anchor** — "You asked me about X back when we talked in [month]. Finally have a real answer: [one sentence]." *Leans on:* Reconnection Choices' engagement/trustworthiness factor + your existing delta rule. Best for: any prior conversation where you owed something.
2. **Their-words-back anchor** — "You said [near-verbatim phrase] last time we talked. That stuck with me — turned out to be right, because [update]." *Leans on:* self-disclosure/mirroring liking research + "Remembering" element. Best for: contacts where you have a specific memorable quote (works well when drafting from real chat history).
3. **Shared-person anchor** — "[Mutual contact] mentioned you two are still in touch — reminded me I owe you an update on [thing you discussed]." *Leans on:* Reconnection Choices (mutual ties add engagement signal); mirrors LP re-engagement practice, where warm intros consistently outperform ([Angel Investors Network LP outreach guide](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/how-to-build-lp-outreach-strategy); [low-authority]). Best for: peer-tier contacts in a small, interconnected community — high fit for your Telegram-heavy CIS network.
4. **Concrete-delta / "why now" anchor** — "We talked about [problem] back in [time]. Just shipped/learned something directly relevant: [one line]." *Leans on:* your existing canon + Gartner's finding that 73% of B2B contacts actively avoid irrelevant outreach ([Gartner, survey Aug–Sep 2024, n=632](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience); [established]). Best for: any contact where you genuinely have news.
5. **Ask-their-opinion anchor** — "You know [domain] better than almost anyone I talk to — got 30 seconds for a gut check on [specific narrow question]?" *Leans on:* Ben Franklin effect (asking for a favor/opinion increases *their* liking of you, works best when the ask is social/advice-type rather than transactional) ([Jecker & Landy, 1969, via Wikipedia/Effectiviology summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin_effect); [established, classic study] + [Reconnection Choices "engagement" factor]). Best for: senior/status-asymmetric contacts — this is your best-evidenced *no-delta-feeling* opener, because the "delta" is actually the specific question, not you.
6. **Milestone/anniversary anchor** — "Just realized it's been almost exactly a year since [specific event you both attended]." *Leans on:* thin — general relationship-ritual literature shows regular meaningful rituals correlate with relationship health in *romantic* pairs ([Forbes/psychologist summary, Dec 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/traversmark/2025/12/20/3-relationship-rituals-worth-carrying-into-2026-by-a-psychologist/); [low-authority, wrong population — romantic couples, not professional ties]) — extrapolation to professional dormant ties is [speculative]. Only strong when the anniversary is *genuinely specific* (a real shared event date), not generic.
7. **Direct/no-pretense anchor (RU-market specific)** — "Привет, [Имя]! Это [Имя] (познакомились на [событие]/работали над X). Пишу сразу по делу: [one line]." *Leans on:* RU-language networking-advice convergence on brevity + immediate self-identification + stating purpose plainly ([kinzhal.media messenger etiquette guide](https://kinzhal.media/howto-messenger/); [low-authority]). Best for: CIS-market peers, especially where the relationship was brief/professional rather than deep.
8. **Congratulate-on-something-specific anchor** — "Saw [specific, publicly-shared thing] — congrats, that's a big deal." *Leans on:* "lead with value not ask" networking-with-seniors consensus ([multiple career-advice sources, cross-corroborated](https://markghaida.medium.com/how-to-approach-without-seeming-too-needy-36ca65516f6d); [low-authority but broad consensus]). **Caution:** this is the mode most at risk of reading as surveillance if the "specific thing" is too granular or too recently observed — see Failure Modes.
9. **Portfolio/state-of-the-world update, unprompted give** — "Not asking anything — just sharing what I learned building [X] since we last talked, in case it's useful to you." *Leans on:* nonprofit donor-reactivation sequencing, where leading with an impact update (not an ask) before any request measurably improves reactivation ([DonorPerfect/Data Axle nonprofit reactivation guides](https://www.donorperfect.com/nonprofit-technology-blog/featured/win-back-lapsed-donors/); [low-authority][cross-domain extrapolation]). Best for: contacts where the delta is about *you*, not them — good for periodic "annual update" style touches.
10. **Async-native technical anchor (for engineers/OSS contacts)** — "Fixed the thing you flagged in [repo/thread] a while back — wanted you specifically to know since you're the one who raised it." *Leans on:* DevRel/OSS contributor-relations norm that recognition + closing the loop on a specific past contribution is the primary re-engagement lever, not sales framing ([GitHub ReadME Project / OSSF DevRel practice](https://github.com/readme/featured/contributor-relations); [moderate-authority, practitioner-sourced]).
11. **Breakup-adjacent honest close, used as an *opener* for very old ties** — "It's been [long time] — no hard feelings if this doesn't land anymore, but wanted to close the loop / say hi." *Leans on:* breakup-email psychology (directness + explicit permission to not respond lowers the bar to reply) — vendor claims of very high response rates for closing-the-loop language exist but are **[single-source][low-authority]**, essentially anecdotal vendor copy, not a controlled study ([HubSpot/Sandler breakup-email pages](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/the-power-of-breakup-emails-templates-to-close-the-loop); treat the specific "76% response rate" figure as unverified marketing copy, not evidence).
12. **Peer-status humor/self-aware opener** — "I'm bad at staying in touch and you deserve better than a 14-month silence, but here I am." *Leans on:* the "liking gap" — people systematically underestimate how glad the other person actually is to hear from them ([Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark, 2018, *Psychological Science*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797618783714); [established]) — this finding is really an argument *against* over-hedging in the opener (the anxiety about reaching out is disproportionate to the actual risk), more than direct evidence that self-deprecating language itself performs better.

### 2. Anchor selection — this is where the evidence is strongest

The single cleanest, most directly transferable academic finding in this whole research is the **Reconnection Choices mismatch** ([Levin, Kurtzberg, Phillips & Naquin — full author list needs verification against the primary paper; cite as Levin et al., *Organization Science*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2015.0996); [established]):

> People systematically *prefer* to reconnect with dormant ties they spent a lot of time with in the past — because reconnection triggers anxiety, and familiarity reduces that anxiety. But the reconnections that actually *deliver value* are the ones offering **novelty** (limited past time together, and/or higher status than the chooser) plus basic **engagement** (trustworthiness, willingness to help).

Practical translation for anchor selection when you have a full conversation history to choose from:
- **Rank anchors by information/status novelty, not by how much you liked the person or how much history you share.** The "safe," comfortable, heavily-talked-to contact is statistically the *lower*-value target, even though it feels like the obvious one to message first.
- **An unkept promise of yours** is a strong anchor because it directly demonstrates the "engagement" factor (trustworthy, follow-through) that the study identifies as necessary alongside novelty — it's evidence, not just a claim, that you're worth re-engaging with.
- **Their stated problem** is a strong anchor only if you have genuine new information about it (satisfies "why now"); if you don't, naming their old problem without a new angle repeats the generic "how's the project going" pattern that reads as content-free.
- **A mutual person** is a decent anchor for lower-stakes peer ties, weaker for status-asymmetric ones (namedropping upward can read as leverage-seeking rather than warmth) [speculative, no direct study on the status-asymmetric case].
- **A question they asked you** functions like the unkept-promise anchor — it's evidence you were actually listening, which is exactly the "Remembering" ingredient Rondi/Levin/De Massis found necessary.
- **An event you both attended** is weakest of the concrete-history anchors unless it's genuinely distinctive (generic "great conference" energy is closer to the empty "let's catch up" you're trying to avoid).

The **Reconnection Process** paper adds a second layer directly relevant to anchor *quality*, not just selection: a successful reconnection needs all three of **Remembering** (the anchor itself), **Catching up** (an actual delta), and **Perceiving the tie similarly** (both people agreeing on how close the relationship is/was) — the study documents a real case where a reconnection attempt caused a lasting rupture specifically because the two parties disagreed on how close they'd been ([Rutgers Business School summary of Rondi/Levin/De Massis](https://www.business.rutgers.edu/business-insights/professors-new-research-identifies-three-elements-reconnecting-successfully); [established], though this specific rupture is a single qualitative case, not a statistic). Practical implication: **don't over-claim intimacy in the anchor** — "we talked about X" is safer than "we were really close back then" unless you're confident they'd agree.

### 3. The no-delta case — the internal dispute

Evidence is thin but not empty, and it does not support a flat "silence is strictly better" position:

- **Nonprofit donor reactivation** has real operational data (not controlled experiments, but large-N practitioner consensus) that a *timed* ritual touch works, and specifies a sweet spot: reactivation attempts around 12–15 months after last contact outperform both earlier (feels premature) and later (feels disconnected) windows ([DonorPerfect/Data Axle nonprofit blogs](https://www.donorperfect.com/nonprofit-technology-blog/featured/win-back-lapsed-donors/); [low-authority][vendor-sourced, but this is standard, cross-vendor "moves management" doctrine in the fundraising world]). This gives your team a concrete number to test against your internal rhythm rule.
- **The Ben Franklin effect** gives real (if old and narrow) experimental backing to "asking a specific opinion" as a legitimate no-content-delta touch: the request itself, if social/advice-flavored rather than transactional, measurably increases the *other person's* liking of the asker ([Jecker & Landy, 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin_effect); [established, classic, but a single 1969 study — later replications exist but with refinements, not a large modern meta-analysis]). This is arguably the strongest evidence-based answer to "is there a legitimate ritual touch": **an opinion-request touch is not really content-free — the ask itself functions as the delta.**
- **"Sending a gift of information"** (an unprompted update, no ask) is supported by the same donor-reactivation sequencing logic (lead with value before any ask) and by general reciprocity/self-disclosure research, but I found no study isolating this specific tactic for professional dormant ties [speculative extrapolation].
- **Seasonal/generic "happy new year" touches** have essentially no support as effective *revival* mechanisms in anything I found — they appear across advice columns as low-effort defaults, not as evidence-backed tactics [no source found for effectiveness; widely practiced, rarely measured].
- The strongest case *against* "no-news is fine" comes from the B2B data: "just checking in" language specifically **decreases** meetings booked by 14% even though it can increase raw reply volume ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/avoid-this-tempting-cold-email-mistake-at-all-costs); [established for that specific dataset]) — i.e., a truly empty touch generates noise-replies ("hey! all good here") that don't convert to anything, which supports your canon's instinct.

**Verdict on the live dispute:** the evidence doesn't cleanly settle "rhythm vs. delta" as a binary — it resolves it into a **third category**: a touch that carries no *news* but does carry a genuine, specific *social move* (a real question, a real acknowledgment, a real timed anniversary) is evidenced to work; a touch that carries neither news nor a specific social move ("it's been a while, how are you") is the one thing every source, from Gong to Gartner to nonprofit fundraising, converges on as the failure mode. Your canon's ban should probably be reworded from "no news → don't send" to "no news and no specific ask/acknowledgment → don't send" — asking for a specific opinion should be classified as satisfying the delta requirement, not as an exception to it.

### 4. Status asymmetry

- The **Reconnection Choices** anxiety-avoidance finding is the single most important input here: reconnecting with higher-status dormant ties is *harder* (more anxiety-inducing) precisely because it's *more valuable* (more novelty) ([Levin et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2015.0996); [established]). This reframes "should I bother the VC" from a politeness question into an expected-value question with the evidence pointing toward "yes, and your discomfort is not signal that it's a bad idea."
- **What reads as needy, consistently across sources:** leading with an ask, especially a resource/money/intro ask, before any value-give or genuine content ([multiple career-advice/networking sources, low-authority but convergent](https://markghaida.medium.com/how-to-approach-without-seeming-too-needy-36ca65516f6d)). This matches your canon's cynical-privateer framing but from the *recipient's* side: a senior contact reads "needy" the same way you'd read a low-value inbound ask.
- **What reads as status-lowering:** over-apologizing for the silence, excessive hedging/self-deprecation stacked on top of an ask (mild self-aware humor is fine per the liking-gap logic above; a paragraph of apology is not — [speculative], no direct study, but consistent with general impression-management literature on excessive self-deprecation reading as low-confidence).
- **Practical asymmetry rule with the best evidence backing:** for peers/engineers, lead with the concrete delta or the mutual-person anchor (both are efficient, information-dense, and match the async-native preference documented in devrel/engineering-culture sources). For VCs/senior operators, lead with either the unkept-promise anchor or the opinion-request anchor — both are evidenced (engagement/trustworthiness signal; Ben Franklin effect) and neither requires you to manufacture false intimacy that a status-asymmetric relationship may not support.
- For former customers, the closest evidenced analogy is silver-medalist recruiting practice: **acknowledge the prior relationship explicitly and transparently, then lead with something of value (an update, a case study, a "here's what changed") rather than a bare "want to buy again?"** ([SHRM/Gem recruiting guides](https://www.gem.com/blog/silver-medalists-candidate-rediscovery); [moderate-authority]).

### 5. Age of silence

- No study gives a hard threshold for "reframe as fresh introduction" [no source found — this is a genuine gap]. The best available proxy is **Jeffrey Hall's friendship-formation-hours research**: ~40–60 hours of contact to reach casual-friend status, ~80–100 for "friend," 200+ for "close friend" ([Hall, 2019, *Journal of Social and Personal Relationships*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0265407518761225); [established], though this measures *friendship formation*, not professional dormant ties, so treat the specific hour counts as [speculative] when transplanted). The actionable implication: **the right variable isn't calendar time since last contact, it's how many cumulative real hours of relationship existed before dormancy.** A contact you spent 3 hours with 8 months ago functionally never crossed into "friend" territory and should probably be treated as closer to a fresh, evidence-light introduction regardless of the short gap; a contact you spent 40+ hours with 2 years ago is still solidly a dormant tie worth a full reconnection-style opener, not a cold intro.
- Adjacent evidence on relationship decay supports treating **silence duration as a decay curve, not a cliff**: friendship-maintenance research finds that failing to maintain contact with a close tie measurably erodes felt closeness on a timescale of months, with communication *frequency* mattering more than physical proximity ([Oswald & Clark research summarized via secondary sources](https://researchrepository.wvu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1140&context=etd); the specific "6 months moves a tie down a category" figure I found in a secondary aggregator is **[single-source][low-authority]** and I could not independently verify it against a primary paper — treat as directional, not precise).
- **Practical scaling by silence length, evidence-weighted:**
  - **1 month:** treat as a normal touch, not a "reconnection" — full anchor/delta apparatus is overkill; a light, specific note works.
  - **6 months:** full reconnection-message apparatus (anchor + delta + why-now + easy reply), per your existing playbook — this is squarely the zone the dormant-ties literature studied (the EMBA study's median dormancy was multi-year, but the "still clearly the same relationship" zone).
  - **24 months:** still within the studied range of the dormant-ties literature (Levin's original EMBA study used contacts dormant *3+ years* on average and still found high value — [established]), so don't treat 24 months as "too long" on principle. But cross-check against the Hall hours-logic above: if the *pre-dormancy* relationship was shallow (a handful of interactions), 24 months of silence plus shallow history is a stronger signal to write it as closer to a first introduction (skip claims of closeness, keep the anchor factual and modest) than a signal to skip re-contact altogether.

### 6. Call or not

- **Async-first engineering/technical audiences show a strong, if under-formally-measured, industry-wide preference for written follow-through over synchronous calls** — flow-state/deep-work research and widespread devrel practice both converge on this even though I found no controlled experiment isolating "call request" vs. "async request" reply rates specifically for this population [industry consensus, low-to-moderate authority, established as *practice norm* even though not run as a controlled study].
- **Alternative conversion targets for async-native contacts, evidence-backed by adjacent domains:** a specific, narrow written question (Ben Franklin effect — the ask-for-opinion play, above) beats "let's hop on a call"; a concrete, low-effort artifact exchange (a doc to comment on, a specific technical ask) fits the OSS/devrel norm of contribution-recognition being the primary re-engagement lever, not a meeting ([GitHub ReadME Project](https://github.com/readme/featured/contributor-relations); [moderate-authority]).
- **When a call is appropriate:** status-asymmetric relationship-building (VC, senior operator) where richer bandwidth genuinely helps, and situations where your prior DR's "warm-yes → booked call" findings apply (Gong's specific-slots-beat-open-ended-ask, breakup/close-the-file honesty) — treat those as already-adopted canon, not re-derived here.
- **This is a genuine gap in the wider literature:** I found no study or even a credible practitioner source directly answering "does pushing to a call ever *cost* you a revived relationship" beyond the general Gartner finding that many B2B buyers actively prefer no rep-mediated interaction at all (61% prefer a rep-free buying experience — [Gartner, June 2025 press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience); [established], though this is about *buying*, not professional reconnection specifically — extrapolation to your context is [speculative]).

### 7. Measurement & stop rules

- **Realistic reply-rate ranges, by closest available proxy (none is a perfect match for your exact channel/relationship):**
  - Cold B2B email 2025–2026: 3.4–8.5% typical, 15–25% for top-decile personalized ([multiple cross-corroborated benchmarking sources](https://whali.co.uk/blog/cold-email-response-rates-benchmarks); [emerging])
  - LinkedIn cold DM: ~10.3% average; LinkedIn alumni-network messages: 10–40% depending on relevance ([Belkins/Alsona 2025 benchmarks](https://alsona.com/blog/linkedin-messaging-benchmarks-whats-a-good-reply-rate-in-2025); [emerging])
  - Reactivation email open rates: ~21.4% average 2025, up from 17% in 2023; personalized subject lines lift open rate from ~18% to ~31% ([secondary aggregation of Litmus/Campaign Monitor data](https://growthwayadvertising.com/the-best-re-engagement-email-templates-backed-by-open-rate-data/); [low-authority, secondary source, could not verify against Litmus primary report directly])
  - Personalized video (closest proxy for a warm voice-note-style touch): 10–16% reply, up to 30% ([Vidyard/Loom/BombBomb](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/top-10-video-outreach-platforms-for-sales-2026); [emerging])
  - **For your exact case (warm dormant Telegram/WhatsApp DM), expect the true number to sit above the cold-outreach range and likely above general LinkedIn cold-DM range, closer to or above the alumni-network band (10–40%)**, because "previously warm 1:1" is a stronger prior than "alumni of the same institution." This is a reasoned extrapolation, not a measured number — **[speculative]**, and worth instrumenting yourselves rather than trusting any borrowed benchmark.
- **What counts as a failed revival:** the clearest evidence-backed definition, again borrowed from nonprofit/recruiting practice, is *no reply after your stop-count is reached* — not "no meeting booked." A reply that doesn't convert to a call/deal is not a failed revival under the dormant-ties literature's framing, since the value Levin's research documents (advice, information, novel perspective) doesn't require a call at all.
- **How many touches before stopping:** your existing 3-touch stop rule is well within the range the broader sales literature converges on (3–6 touches, spaced 2–5 days apart for cold sequences; the oft-cited "80% need 5+ touches" statistic traces to an unverifiable, methodologically bare 1942 survey of fewer than 40 people and should not be trusted as evidence — [debunked / low-authority, traced and confirmed via Conciergr's investigation](https://conciergr.com/blog/80-percent-sales-follow-up-myth)). For a warm dormant tie (not cold), 3 non-triggered touches before stopping is, if anything, on the generous side compared to cold-outreach norms, which is appropriate given the relationship equity involved.
- **Reputation cost of over-touching in a small community:** well-corroborated in general terms (>4–5 touches with no engagement risks being perceived as spam even in paid, permission-based channels — [multiple cross-corroborated sales sources](https://ditlead.com/blog/how-often-should-you-reach-out-to-your-prospects); [emerging]) but **I found no study specifically quantifying reputation cost within a small, socially-connected community** (where multiple recipients might compare notes) [no source found] — this is a real gap; your 3-touch-then-stop rule is a reasonable risk-managed default given the absence of direct data, precisely because reputational damage in a small community compounds in ways the large-N cold-outreach literature can't see (it's optimized for anonymous total-addressable-market outreach, not a dense social graph where people talk to each other).

### 8. AI-drafted touches

This is the best-evidenced answer in the entire brief, from a single strong, very recent, directly-on-point source:

- **Molnar & Zhu (2026), *Computers in Human Behavior*, n=1,300+ US adults 18–84**: unaided, people essentially cannot detect that a personal message (including apology-type messages closely analogous to a reconnection touch) was AI-written — impressions of undisclosed-authorship messages matched impressions of disclosed-human messages almost exactly, even among heavy AI users. **But** when told a message was AI-generated, the same recipients rated the sender as "lazy," "insincere," and showing "lack of effort," versus "genuine," "grateful," "thoughtful" for identical text believed to be human-written ([full study writeup via The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/most-people-do-not-realize-when-a-personal-message-they-receive-was-written-by-ai-study-finds-278874); [established] — this is the strongest single citation in this report: peer-reviewed, 2026, large N, directly on the exact question asked).
- **Reply-rate effect of AI authorship on raw sends**: industry data circulating in 2026 suggests a modest ~1.1 percentage-point reply-rate gap between fully AI-generated and human-written messages on comparable lists ([secondary industry-community source](https://firstsales.io/blog/ai-vs-human-cold-email-reply-rates/); [single-source][low-authority] — could not trace to a controlled study). More consistently corroborated: personalization depth matters far more than authorship mechanism — deep personalization (~5–8% reply) beats generic AI or generic human templates (~1–2%) regardless of who/what wrote it ([multiple 2026 AI-outreach benchmarking sources](https://www.sendr.ai/blog/high-reply-rate-cold-email-data-2026); [emerging]).
- **Which part must stay human:** the Molnar & Zhu disclosure-penalty finding, combined with the near-zero unaided-detection finding, together imply the operative risk isn't "will they notice the AI," it's "will disclosure ever happen, and does the message read as generic enough to trigger suspicion regardless of actual authorship." Practically: the anchor (the specific, only-a-human-who-was-actually-there-could-know detail) is the part that must be unmistakably grounded in real, specific memory — because a generic-sounding message is what triggers "did a bot write this?" suspicion even without formal disclosure, and that suspicion functions the same way the disclosure penalty does (reads as low-effort/insincere) even if never confirmed. This directly supports your own operating canon's distinction between Майкрофт-voice content (raised-disclosure, low-stakes, non-warm) and undisclosed Tier-A VIP-lead handling (§4.1 carve-out) — the research suggests the VIP carve-out is evidentially sound *specifically because* those messages need to read as unmistakably specific/human regardless of disclosure policy, since genericness is the real trigger, not the disclosure itself.

## C. Contradictions & Debates

**1. Message length.** Gong's outbound-email data (long emails "15x more effective") directly contradicts Woodpecker's 20M-email dataset (short <80–100 words, ~50% higher engagement) and the broader 2025–2026 benchmarking consensus favoring brevity. **Steelmanning Gong:** their claim may hold specifically for genuinely cold, unfamiliar B2B pitches where the recipient needs enough context to evaluate a stranger's credibility before replying — length there may substitute for the trust a warm relationship already provides. In a **warm, dormant** relationship, that credibility-building function of length is largely unnecessary, because the anchor itself (shared history) does the trust-building work a stranger-cold email needs length for. I could not verify Gong's underlying methodology despite fetching the source page directly, so I weight this contradiction toward the brevity side for your specific use case, consistent with your already-adopted 50–100 word canon — but flag that Gong's claim is not resolved, only out-weighted by better-documented data for a different (colder) context.

**2. The "80% require 5+ touches" statistic vs. modern data.** This widely-repeated stat traces to an unverifiable 1942 survey of fewer than 40 door-to-door and phone salespeople, with no published methodology, no peer review, and an organization ("National Sales Executive Association") that multiple investigators have been unable to confirm ever existed as a distinct entity ([Conciergr's traced investigation](https://conciergr.com/blog/80-percent-sales-follow-up-myth); confirmed independently via [VentureBeat's earlier debunking](https://venturebeat.com/marketing/these-incredible-sales-stats-everyone-cites-are-actually-completely-false)). More reliable modern data (Velocify, 3.5M leads) puts 93% of converted leads reached within *six* attempts, not five, and finds 50% of inbound leads never get a second contact at all — a different problem (under-following-up) than the one the myth warns against. **This is a genuine debunking, not a live disagreement between good sources** — the myth should be retired from your canon's evidence base if it's cited anywhere, even though the general *directional* advice ("persistence matters, most people quit too early") happens to still be roughly right for unrelated reasons.

**3. Rhythm-first vs. delta-first (your internal dispute).** As discussed in B3: the evidence doesn't cleanly back either pure position. It supports a **reclassification** — a specific-ask or specific-acknowledgment touch counts as satisfying "delta" even with zero news, while a generic time-based check-in never does regardless of how well-timed the rhythm is. Steelmanning the rhythm-first position: nonprofit donor data does show a *timing* effect independent of content quality (the 12–15 month reactivation window outperforms both earlier and later attempts) — so rhythm isn't nothing, it interacts with delta rather than substituting for it.

**4. AI-detection risk.** One might assume detectable-AI-writing is the primary risk in scaling reconnection messages. The Molnar & Zhu (2026) finding says the opposite: **detection is essentially a non-issue because people can't detect it unaided** — the real risk is *disclosure* (voluntary or discovered) and, more subtly, *genericness triggering suspicion regardless of actual authorship*. This is a meaningful correction to an intuition your team may be operating under.

## D. Best Practices

1. **Anchor selection: default to novelty over comfort.** When choosing which dormant tie to message first from a queue, deprioritize the contact you talked to most/liked most, and prioritize contacts with real information asymmetry (different domain, higher status, less-overlapping network) — this is the single most evidence-backed, counter-intuitive lever available ([Levin et al., Reconnection Choices](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2015.0996)).
2. **Anchor content: carry all three of Remembering + Catching-up + Perceived-parity**, and be conservative on claimed closeness specifically to avoid the rupture failure mode documented in Rondi/Levin/De Massis.
3. **Classify "no-delta" touches correctly.** A specific opinion-ask is a delta. A specific, real anniversary is a delta. "Just checking in" is never a delta — retire that exact phrase from every draft (Gong's -14% booked finding is one of the cleanest, most replicated findings in this entire research).
4. **Weight status-asymmetric contacts up, not down**, in your outreach queue — the anxiety you (or Antón) feel about messaging a VC/senior operator is evidence the tie is high-novelty/high-value, not evidence it's a bad target.
5. **Match conversion target to audience.** Engineers/async-native contacts: convert to a specific written ask or artifact exchange, not a call. Status-asymmetric/relationship-building contacts: a call is more defensible, following your already-adopted "specific slots beat open scheduling" rule.
6. **For AI-assisted drafting**, invest disproportionate human attention in the anchor sentence specifically (the only-a-real-human-detail part) — genericness, not AI-authorship per se, is what triggers suspicion and the "lazy/insincere" read.
7. **Instrument your own reply-rate data rather than trusting borrowed benchmarks** — no source in this research covers your exact channel+relationship combination, and the honest answer to "what's a realistic reply rate" is closer to "we don't know for this specific case, go measure it" than any of the borrowed proxy numbers above.

## E. Failure Modes

- **"Just checking in" and equivalent empty check-ins** — measurably reduce meeting-booking rate (-14%) even while sometimes raising raw reply counts, because the replies it generates are content-free ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/avoid-this-tempting-cold-email-mistake-at-all-costs)).
- **Pitching in the first re-contact touch** — reduces reply rate by up to 57% in Gong's outbound dataset; directly validates your existing "no links/deck/Calendly in touch #1" rule.
- **Over-claiming closeness/relationship parity** — the documented Rondi/Levin/De Massis rupture case shows this can permanently damage a tie, not just fail to revive it.
- **Referencing specific tracked behavior ("I saw you were active in three chats")** — the exact viral case name couldn't be sourced, but the underlying principle (referencing granular observed activity reads as surveillance, not personalization) is broadly corroborated across sales-psychology practitioner writing; treat this as [moderate-confidence practitioner consensus, not a documented statistic].
- **Platform ban risk, Telegram/WhatsApp specifically:** Meta reportedly banned 6.8M WhatsApp accounts in H1 2025, most not intentional spammers but pattern-crossers; safe operating thresholds cited by messaging-infrastructure vendors are >30% reply rate, <30 outbound messages/hour, and <50 messages/day for a new number, with WhatsApp's report-threshold reportedly tightening ~18% by April 2026 ([whapi.cloud vendor blog](https://whapi.cloud/blog/how-to-avoid-whatsapp-ban-2026); **[single-source][low-authority]** — this is a vendor blog without a traceable Meta primary source; treat the specific numbers as directional risk-awareness, not verified thresholds). Telegram similarly flags immediate mass-messaging from new/VoIP/shared-device accounts as abnormal-behavior triggers ([kookeey vendor blog](https://www.kookeey.net/en/news/datacenter-proxies/2025-telegram-mass-messaging-anti-ban-guide); **[low-authority]**). Given you're operating at thousands-of-contacts scale, this is a real operational constraint worth independently verifying with your own account-health monitoring rather than trusting these vendor figures outright.
- **Disclosed (or suspected) AI authorship** — triggers a measurable, real negative-perception penalty ("lazy," "insincere") even though unaided detection is near-zero ([Molnar & Zhu, 2026](https://theconversation.com/most-people-do-not-realize-when-a-personal-message-they-receive-was-written-by-ai-study-finds-278874)).
- **Over-touching in a small, socially-connected community** — general sales-reputation data supports >4–5 unanswered touches risking a spam/annoyance perception; the specific *compounding* risk in a dense network where recipients compare notes is **[no source found]**, an evidence gap you're managing by policy (3-touch stop) rather than by data.
- **Sending the ritual "no-news" touch too early or too late relative to the last real contact** — nonprofit donor data specifically shows both too-soon (feels premature) and too-late (feels disconnected) windows underperform a ~12–15 month sweet spot; this is cross-domain evidence, not direct, but it's a concrete number worth testing.

## F. Strategic Recommendations

**Option 1 — Adopt the "delta reclassification" and ship it as the settlement to the internal dispute.**
Rewrite the internal rule from "rhythm decides when, delta decides what, never send an empty check-in" to: *"A touch must carry either a concrete delta OR a specific ask/acknowledgment (opinion request, real anniversary, named unkept promise) — never neither."* This is directly evidence-backed (Ben Franklin effect + Gong's empty-touch penalty + donor-reactivation timing data) and requires only a policy edit, not new tooling.
- *Trade-off:* slightly loosens the "never send an empty check-in" absolutism your canon currently holds, which may feel like a regression if misapplied — needs a clear bright line (a specific question ≠ "how are you").
- *Risk:* low. This mostly formalizes what good operators already intuitively do.

**Option 2 — Re-rank the dormant-contact reactivation queue by novelty, not comfort.**
Build (or adjust) the scoring so status-asymmetric and low-overlap contacts get *upweighted*, not deprioritized, contrary to the natural instinct to start with easy/familiar contacts.
- *Trade-off:* higher near-term rejection/no-reply risk on the hardest contacts, but this is exactly the segment the academic literature says is highest-value if it lands.
- *Risk:* moderate — requires discipline against the natural anxiety-avoidance bias the research itself documents as the reason people get this wrong.

**Option 3 — Instrument reply-rate measurement natively, rather than importing any of the benchmark numbers above as targets.**
None of the borrowed benchmarks (cold email, LinkedIn, video) is a verified match for warm Telegram/WhatsApp DM reactivation. Build the counter now so in 60–90 days you have your own real number instead of a proxy.
- *Trade-off:* near-zero cost, this should just be a `pub_ledger`/CRM field, consistent with your existing measurement discipline (§6.4, §5.8).
- *Risk:* none meaningful.

**Recommendation:** adopt **all three** — they're complementary, not exclusive, and Option 3 is what lets you validate or overturn Options 1 and 2 with your own data within a quarter.
**Confidence: [emerging]** — the underlying academic findings (Reconnection Choices, the empty-touch penalty, the AI-disclosure study) are individually [established], but the *composite operational rule* synthesized from them for your specific channel is a reasoned extrapolation, not a tested playbook.
**Main assumption this rests on:** that findings from professional-network reconnection (mostly studied via LinkedIn-adjacent and in-person professional contexts) and from B2B email/LinkedIn outbound generalize to warm Telegram/WhatsApp 1:1 DM in a founder-relationship context. This is the single largest unverified assumption in the whole brief — there is no direct study of that exact cell, and it should be treated as the first thing your own instrumented data (Option 3) either confirms or overturns.
