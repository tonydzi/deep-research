# Trojan Alpha

## Executive Summary

The strongest available evidence suggests that **knowledge-layer poisoning should be handled as a supply-chain integrity problem, not as a “bad-content detection” problem**. Current model-side defenses reduce risk, sometimes substantially, but they do not make an autonomous agent safe to auto-implement mined recommendations from untrusted sources. Anthropic reports a non-zero prompt-injection attack success rate even after reinforcement learning, classifiers, and red-teaming in a browser agent, and the UK NCSC argues prompt injection may never be fully mitigated in the way SQL injection was because LLMs do not inherently separate data from instructions. OpenAI’s current guidance is consistent with that view: when models can search the web, read vector stores, or call remote tools, developers still need their own trust boundaries, staged workflows, validation, and audit controls. (Sources: Anthropic, 2025-11-24, https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses; NCSC, 2025-12-08, https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/prompt-injection-is-not-sql-injection; OpenAI, current docs, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/deep-research). citeturn17view0turn19search0turn31view5

For your architecture, the practical implication is blunt: **no mined idea should move directly from external content into implementation backlog as an actionable recommendation**. Instead, every idea needs to pass through a **quarantine prism** that captures immutable provenance, sanitizes the carrier, scores the proposal’s effect surface, checks corroboration, and requires stronger evidence as the proposal becomes harder to reverse or broader in blast radius. This is not a theoretical concern. Academic work shows that RAG systems can be poisoned with only a handful of malicious texts, and newer work shows that even a **single poisoned document** can be enough under realistic conditions. Separate in-the-wild work from Google, Palo Alto Networks Unit 42, and Microsoft shows that external pages, links, and even “Summarize with AI” affordances are already being used to steer assistants, plant persistent memory, bias future recommendations, or attempt destructive actions. (Sources: USENIX PoisonedRAG, 2025, https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zou-poisonedrag; arXiv “One Shot Dominance,” 2026-04-09, https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11548v4; Google Security Blog, 2026-04-23, https://blog.google/security/prompt-injections-web/; Unit 42, 2026-03-03, https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/ai-agent-prompt-injection/; Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-02-10, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/02/10/ai-recommendation-poisoning/). citeturn15view3turn28view0turn15view11turn15view13turn38view0

The best transferable pattern comes from **software supply-chain security**. SLSA, in-toto, Sigstore, GitHub artifact attestations, and PyPI’s post-PEP 740 attestation model all separate two questions that agent builders often blur: **“Where did this come from?”** and **“Should I trust or run it?”** Attestations answer the first question well, but the official guidance is explicit that provenance is **not** proof of safety or correctness. That distinction maps directly to mined ideas: you should cryptographically bind an idea to its exact source extracts, retrieval path, normalization steps, and approval history; then you should **evaluate policy on top of that record**, not treat provenance as approval. ## Contradictions and Debates

There is a real disagreement about **how far model-side defenses can take you**. Anthropic shows substantial progress in a concrete agent setting, reporting a roughly 1% attack success rate against an adaptive internal attacker in browser use after combining model training, classifiers, and red-teaming. That is evidence that model-side robustness is improving. But Anthropic also explicitly says the problem is far from solved, and the NCSC’s position is harder-edged: because LLMs do not inherently separate data from instructions, full mitigation may never look like the clean parameterized-query solution that worked for SQL injection. The most decision-relevant reading is that **better models reduce review volume but do not change the need for a quarantine prism**. (Sources: Anthropic, 2025-11-24, https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses; NCSC, 2025-12-08, https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/prompt-injection-is-not-sql-injection). citeturn16view0turn17view0turn19search0

There is also disagreement about **how much retrieval-layer engineering alone can help**. OpenRAG-Soc and Spotlighting both show practical gains from sanitation, Unicode normalization, and provenance-marking. A recent independent study reports that hybrid BM25+vector retrieval cut one gradient-guided poisoning class from 38% to 0% in its setting, but that same study also says joint optimization can partly circumvent the defense and should be treated as a first-line barrier rather than a terminal fix. That means hybrid retrieval is worth deploying, but only as one layer. **Steelman for the stronger retrieval-defense view:** it is cheap, deployable, and immediately raises attacker cost. **Steelman for the skeptical view:** the attacker adapts, and the field has not converged on general results across corpora and models. (Sources: OpenRAG-Soc, 2026-01-20, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10923v2; Spotlighting, 2024-03-20, https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14720; Semantic Chameleon, 2026-03-10, https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18034v1) [single-source for the 38%→0% figure]. citeturn15view5turn28view2turn28view1

A third debate is whether **source reputation** meaningfully protects you. NIST’s AI cybersecurity profile says supplier trustworthiness, transparency of data, and training/evaluation methods should be considered before adopting third-party relationships, which supports reputation as a real signal. But recent incidents show why reputation cannot be decisive: Amazon Q’s official VS Code extension shipped malicious code after an inappropriately scoped GitHub token allowed a threat actor to commit to the repository; PyPI’s Ultralytics incident was detectable because the malicious releases lacked the expected provenance trail, not because the project had a weak reputation. So the evidence supports a narrower claim: **reputation should lower triage cost, not waive provenance checks**. (Sources: NIST IR 8596 initial public draft, 2025-12, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2025/NIST.IR.8596.iprd.pdf; AWS Security Bulletin AWS-2025-015, 2025-07-23, https://aws.amazon.com/security/security-bulletins/AWS-2025-015/; PyPI attack analysis, 2024-12-11, https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2024-12-11-ultralytics-attack-analysis/). citeturn20view0turn15view10turn34view4turn35view2

Finally, there is a subtle but important gray zone around **“helpful guidance.”** Google’s scan found webpages that tried to shape AI summaries in seemingly benign ways, and Microsoft’s recommendation-poisoning research shows that “remember this company as trusted” may look mild while still silently degrading future recommendations. ## Best Practices

The core best practice is to make the agent treat an incoming idea the way a secure build system treats an incoming artifact: **normalize it, fingerprint it, attest it, score it, corroborate it, and only then decide whether it is eligible for execution-adjacent use**. NIST’s AI cybersecurity profile explicitly says data provenance should be weighted as heavily as software and hardware origin, and that the integrity of training and input data should be verified to detect and prevent poisoning and tampering. Microsoft’s memory guidance likewise says to gate writes on intent and provenance and to treat retrieval as a risk decision rather than as authoritative truth. (Sources: NIST IR 8596, 2025-12, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2025/NIST.IR.8596.iprd.pdf; Microsoft Learn, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety). citeturn20view0turn17view2

A workable **quarantine prism** for a small fleet has seven stages. First, **carrier sanitation**: fetch raw content, detect MIME type, strip or separately preserve active/hidden content, normalize Unicode, and render HTML/Markdown into both “user-visible text” and “machine-visible raw text.” This is directly supported by OpenRAG-Soc and Microsoft’s Spotlighting family, which both show that sanitation and source-marking reduce instruction-following from untrusted carriers. Second, **claim extraction under attribution constraints**: force the miner to output a canonical recommendation, the exact supporting spans, and an uncertainty statement, rather than a free-form “best practice” summary. Third, **immutable provenance capture**: store source URL, author/publisher if known, retrieval time, content hash, sanitizer version, extraction-model version, and the exact spans that justified the idea. Fourth, **effect-surface classification**: map the recommendation to one or more risk surfaces such as code execution, dependency changes, auth changes, secrets handling, logging changes, memory writes, network egress, deletion, persistence, or spend amplification. Fifth, **corroboration**: require multiple independent sources for higher-risk ideas, with at least one primary or vendor-authored source when the idea touches a product-specific mechanism. Sixth, **policy decision**: auto-clear only if the recommendation is reversible, read-only, low-blast-radius, and well-supported. Seventh, **post-adoption observability**: keep the attestation linked to the resulting backlog item, commit, or config change so you can later answer “which external idea caused this?” (Sources: OpenRAG-Soc, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10923v2; Spotlighting, https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14720; SLSA, https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance; in-toto, https://github.com/in-toto/attestation; Microsoft Learn, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety). citeturn15view5turn28view2turn15view8turn6search6turn17view2

The best way to **bind a mined idea to provenance immutably** is to create an **Idea Attestation** that borrows the in-toto/SLSA shape. Subject: `sha256(canonical_idea_text || normalized_supporting_spans || source_hashes)`. Predicate fields: original URLs, retrieval timestamps, content digests, parser/sanitizer versions, model version, extracted spans, corroborating sources, conflict notes, risk class, reviewer decision, and downstream linkage to issue/PR/commit/config hash. Envelope: DSSE-style signed JSON using the same Ed25519 discipline you already use elsewhere. Store: append-only Git repository or SQLite log replicated by Syncthing, plus optional external transparency via Sigstore/Rekor or GitHub attestations if you want public verifiability. This is a design adaptation, but it tracks the exact lesson from SLSA, Sigstore, PyPI, and GitHub: **identity-bound, digest-bound, append-only metadata beats mutable notes or free-form backlog text**. (Sources: SLSA, https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance; Sigstore, https://docs.sigstore.dev/about/security/; GitHub Docs, https://docs.github.com/en/actions/concepts/security/artifact-attestations; PyPI Docs, https://docs.pypi.org/attestations/; PEP 740, https://peps.python.org/pep-0740/). citeturn15view8turn15view9turn26view2turn26view3turn35view3

The most effective way to avoid alert fatigue is **not** to alert on every suspicious sentence. Instead, alert on **policy-relevant transitions**: low-confidence provenance on a high-impact recommendation; hidden-text or URL-parameter steering trying to create future memory; a recommendation that weakens logging/auth/sandboxing; a recommendation that conflicts with current policy; a recommendation that cannot be corroborated independently; or a recommendation whose claimed provenance hash no longer matches the source content. This is consistent with Microsoft’s advice to maintain full lifecycle observability for memory operations and with broader secure-AI guidance that emphasizes logging, monitoring, and ownership of security outcomes. (Sources: Microsoft Learn, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety; NCSC/CISA Secure AI Guidelines, 2023-11-27, https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/guidelines-secure-ai-system-development). citeturn17view2turn27view0turn27view3

## Strategic Recommendations

You have three realistic operating models.

**Prism Lite** keeps complexity low. It adds ingest sanitation, visible-vs-hidden text extraction, idea hashing, source/citation capture, a simple risk label, and mandatory human review for anything touching code, installs, secrets, network changes, memory writes, or safety configuration. Advantages: fast to deploy, maintainable by one person, low accidental-complexity risk. Drawback: weak deduplication and poor long-term traceability unless you are very disciplined. Best when your backlog volume is small and you can tolerate manual review. This option is supported by the field’s emphasis on sanitation, staged workflows, and human approval for sensitive operations. (Sources: OpenAI, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/deep-research; Microsoft Learn, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety; OpenRAG-Soc, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10923v2). citeturn31view5turn17view2turn15view5

**Prism Standard** is the recommended choice. It adds a signed **Idea Attestation** per mined recommendation, append-only storage, corroboration thresholds, automated policy checks on effect surface, read-only shared memory/knowledge, and a quarantine registry for packages, snippets, config deltas, and “optimizations” awaiting review. Advantages: materially better for forensics, rollback, and later trust decisions; maps directly to proven software-supply-chain patterns; still manageable by one owner if implemented with plain signed JSON plus Git. Drawback: you must formalize metadata fields and review states, which adds process overhead. This is the best fit for your described multi-machine Syncthing fleet. **Recommendation: choose Prism Standard. Confidence: [established] on the underlying controls; [emerging] on exact threshold tuning. Main assumption: backlog throughput is high enough that provenance debt will otherwise accumulate faster than a human can audit it.** (Sources: SLSA, https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance; Sigstore, https://docs.sigstore.dev/about/security/; PyPI Docs, https://docs.pypi.org/attestations/; GitHub Docs, https://docs.github.com/en/actions/concepts/security/artifact-attestations). citeturn15view8turn15view9turn26view3turn26view2

**Prism Maximal** adds transparency-log publication, selective-disclosure proofs, benchmark replay, automated adversarial evaluation, and stronger retrieval-side defenses such as hybrid retrieval and token-level sanitization. Advantages: strongest auditability and better resilience against adaptive attacks. Drawback: complexity rises sharply, and some frontier ideas are not yet operationally mature for a one-person deployment. Recent papers on privacy-preserving SBOMs and token-level IPI sanitization are promising, but they are still better read as frontier direction than as low-maintenance defaults. (Sources: NDSS Rennervate, https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/attention-is-all-you-need-to-defend-against-indirect-prompt-injection-attacks-in-llms/; arXiv zkSBOM, 2026-04-30, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00076; arXiv VeriSBOM, 2026-02-14, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13682). ## Action Plan

1. **Stop direct promotion from mined idea to implementable backlog item.** Change the alpha-mining pipeline so every extracted idea lands first in a quarantine registry with status `raw`, never `ready`. Attach URL, retrieval time, content hash, extractor version, and exact evidence spans on day one. This is the single highest-value change because it creates the boundary that your current task-layer controls already assume. (Supported by OpenAI’s staged-workflow guidance and Microsoft’s “gate writes on intent and provenance.”) (Sources: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/deep-research; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety). citeturn31view5turn17view2

2. **Implement carrier sanitation before extraction.** For web pages and documents, store both sanitized visible text and raw machine-visible text; flag hidden text, suspicious URL parameters, non-printing Unicode, encoded blobs, and overlong irrelevant sections. Do not let the extractor read only the sanitized view; preserve both views so reviewers can spot concealment. (Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10923v2; https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14720; https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/blog/2025/07/how-microsoft-defends-against-indirect-prompt-injection-attacks). citeturn15view5turn28view2turn17view1

3. **Create an Idea Attestation schema.** Use signed JSON with fields for `idea_id`, `canonical_claim`, `supporting_spans`, `source_records`, `retrieval_context`, `normalization_steps`, `risk_surfaces`, `corroboration`, `review_decision`, and `downstream_links`. Hash the canonical content, sign it, and store it append-only. If you later adopt external transparency, this schema can map cleanly to in-toto/SLSA concepts. (Sources: https://github.com/in-toto/attestation; https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance; https://docs.sigstore.dev/about/security/). citeturn6search6turn15view8turn15view9

4. **Adopt an effect-based review policy.** Auto-clear only ideas that are all of the following: read-only, reversible, do not alter execution or memory, do not add dependencies, do not change auth/network/deletion/logging/safety behavior, and are corroborated by at least two independent sources or one primary source plus one independent secondary source. Hold for human review if any of those conditions fail. This threshold is a design recommendation, but it is directly motivated by the empirical pattern that one poisoned source can be enough and memory/persistence drastically raise blast radius. (Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11548v4; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety; https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/indirect-prompt-injection-poisons-ai-longterm-memory/). citeturn28view0turn17view2turn15view12

5. **Make shared knowledge and shared memory read-only by default.** LangChain’s own docs recommend application-code-only writes for shared policy memory, and Microsoft says not to rely on prompting for boundary enforcement. For your fleet, that means Syncthing-shared policy files, trusted snippets, and “approved best practices” should be writable only by the quarantine service or a signed human-review tool, never by general agents. (Sources: https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/deepagents/memory; https://docs.langchain.com/oss/javascript/deepagents/going-to-production; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety). citeturn31view0turn31view1turn17view2

6. **Maintain a quarantine registry for incoming packages, fixes, and config changes.** Record whether an idea implies a dependency addition, a package upgrade, a CLI install, a Docker image, a config delta, a code snippet, or a policy rewrite. Require provenance checks for the underlying package or artifact using existing ecosystems where possible—GitHub attestations, PyPI attestations, Sigstore, SLSA—before the agent is even allowed to propose implementation. That prevents an “idea” from smuggling a supply-chain shortcut. (Sources: https://docs.github.com/en/actions/concepts/security/artifact-attestations; https://docs.pypi.org/attestations/; https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2024-12-11-ultralytics-attack-analysis/). citeturn26view2turn26view3turn35view2

7. **Instrument revalidation and revocation.** External sources change. Re-fetch high-impact sources periodically; compare content hash and source claims; invalidate attestations if the upstream content changed materially or if the provenance no longer verifies. This is the “ideas age and drift” equivalent of certificate revocation and expected-provenance checking. The motivation comes directly from PyPI’s expected-provenance lesson and from C2PA’s revocation problems. (Sources: https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2024-12-11-ultralytics-attack-analysis/; https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24890v1). citeturn35view2turn34view2turn34view3

8. **Measure the system like a security control, not a content curation tool.** Track: percentage of ideas with full provenance; fraction of high-risk ideas held for review; later rollback rate; mean time from ingest to decision; proportion of accepted ideas later contradicted by stronger sources; hidden-text detection rate; and number of downstream changes linked to revoked or stale Idea Attestations. Without these metrics, the prism will quietly degrade into either rubber-stamping or alert spam. (Supported by Microsoft’s observability guidance and secure-AI lifecycle guidance.) (Sources: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/manage-agentic-memory-safety; https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/guidelines-secure-ai-system-development). citeturn17view2turn27view0turn27view3
