### A3. Obstacles, and who handles them

Confirmed.

*   Export controls and blacklists. Zhipu and affiliates, Entity List, 15 January 2025 (Reuters). DeepSeek was approved internally for that list and then not published, as of 17 June 2026 (Reuters), so “DeepSeek is not listed” is a decision that can be reversed. The January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule was rescinded and a replacement is still awaited (Greenberg Traurig, 22 September 2026: https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/9/navigating-gpu-export-controls-and-ai-use-restrictions-in-data-center-operations).
*   Procurement and device bans. NDAA FY2026 §1532, in force 17 January 2026, DeepSeek off DoD and IC devices and their contractors (Digital Policy Alert; Wikipedia’s DeepSeek page states the same scope). Texas (January 2025) and New York (February 2025) banned it on state devices (Mashable, 17 June 2026). A secondary compilation lists more states (https://deepseekai.guide/news/deepseek-us-restrictions/, 23 April 2026). I did not open each state order. There is no federal ban on private use.
*   Distillation as the current brand event. Bloomberg, 8 September 2026: a joint NSA, FBI and CISA statement accused DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI of industrial-scale distillation since at least 2024. I did not open the agency PDF. The Next Web, 22 September 2026, citing The Information: China’s CAC summoned seven labs and focused on DeepSeek and Moonshot. Reported volumes, which I did not see in an Anthropic primary document: Alibaba more than 151 million exchanges, Moonshot about 23 million, DeepSeek more than 12.1 million in 14 days, Zhipu 3.4 million, Xiaomi just over 400,000.
*   Congress is already investigating US companies for using the models, not only for working at the labs. Airbnb and Anysphere, April 2026 (The Hill). DoorDash, July 2026.
*   Trust and data residency are being productized by intermediaries, not by the labs. OpenRouter’s pitch on 9 September 2026 is explicit: use the Chinese weights, keep plaintext in the US, the lab is not involved.
*   Recruiting. Epoch’s 23 June 2026 scrape of 1,604 postings at DeepSeek, MiniMax, Moonshot, Z.ai, ByteDance and Alibaba: 93% of located postings are in Beijing, Hangzhou or Shanghai; Beijing alone is 63%. US frontier-lab postings in their comparison were about 85% San Francisco. Chinese technical reqs asked for 1.6 years of experience on average versus 5.5 in the US sample. DeepSeek’s June 2026 hiring notice denied a “no foreigners” rule and said the bar is Chinese, the way US labs require English (ycwb, 26 June 2026).
*   Payment and contracting. Z.ai’s non-China developer terms are a Singapore company, Singapore law, SIAC arbitration, and they forbid re-export to restricted parties without approvals (terms, 14 April 2026). That is the cleanest structure I found, and it is also the Entity Listed group.

Who handles it best. MiniMax, on the numbers: majority revenue already outside mainland China, English consumer products, a named DevRel head, SF reqs as of June 2026, and no Entity List hit found. Alibaba, on the org chart: a standing US cloud subsidiary, a named North America GM, and open Sunnyvale reqs with California-style pay disclosure. Z.ai handles government sales outside the US well and handles the US badly: Entity List plus a non-US office map. DeepSeek handles distribution by publishing MIT weights and letting Azure, Fireworks and OpenRouter carry them, and handles the US labor market by not entering it. Moonshot handles product adoption and is handling the politics worst.
