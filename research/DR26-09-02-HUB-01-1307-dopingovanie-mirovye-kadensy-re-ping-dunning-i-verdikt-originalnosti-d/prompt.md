DR26-09-02-HUB-01-1307

DEEP RESEARCH REQUEST: Systematic re-pinging of people who ignore commitments ("doping"/tapping/dunning) — prior art, data, risks, and cadence design.

CONTEXT (who is asking). We are a small AI-automation lab. We built a mechanism we internally call "допингование" (doping/tapping): when a person AGREED to something on a call or in writing, we did our part, and they went silent across all channels, our automation re-delivers the ORIGINAL agreed message instead of writing "hey, remember me?". In messengers (Telegram/WhatsApp) it DELETES our old message and re-sends the identical text, so it becomes unread again and jumps to the top of their chat list. In email it simply re-sends the same email. Schedule: days 1-7 daily, then days 14/21/28 weekly, hard stop at day 31 ("doping died"). Any reply = immediate stop. Confirmed ban in a channel = stop that channel. The founder invented this trick for his crypto-CRM around 2022 (internal name "tapping", ~30 taps/24h cap by keyword) and wants to know if he is an original ideologue or if the world already does this.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS (answer ALL, with sources and numbers):

1. PRIOR ART IN SALES ENGAGEMENT. How do Outreach.io, Salesloft, Apollo.io, Reply.io, lemlist, Instantly, Woodpecker, Klenty and similar platforms design follow-up sequences/cadences for prospects who went silent AFTER a verbal agreement (not cold outreach)? Typical touch counts, spacing curves (daily? exponential backoff?), channel mixing, give-up thresholds. Cite published data: reply-rate by touch number (Woodpecker, Backlinko, Belkins, Gong, InsideSales/XANT studies), the famous "80% of sales require 5 follow-ups" claim and its critique.

2. COLLECTIONS / DUNNING. The closest formal discipline to "collecting on commitments" is dunning (debt collection cadences, SaaS failed-payment recovery: Churnbuster, Baremetrics Recover, Stripe Smart Retries, Chargebee dunning). What schedules do they use (day offsets, number of attempts, escalation ladders), what recovery rates do they publish, and what does regulation (FDCPA contact-frequency caps in the US, similar EU rules) say about maximum ping frequency? Map their schedule logic onto our 7-daily + 3-weekly + stop-31 design: is ours aggressive, average, or soft?

3. THE DELETE-AND-RESEND TRICK. Does ANYONE publicly teach or use "delete your own message and re-send the same text so it becomes unread again and jumps up" in Telegram/WhatsApp/other messengers? Search growth-hacking forums, sales-engagement blogs, Telegram-marketing communities (RU and EN), GitHub tools. Is there a name for it? Known risks: does the counterparty get any trace of the deletion (Telegram DM behavior for delete-for-both, WhatsApp "message deleted" placeholder — IMPORTANT: WhatsApp SHOWS a placeholder, does that kill the trick there?), unread-counter behavior, notification behavior (does re-send re-notify on muted chats?), Telegram anti-spam (PEER_FLOOD) thresholds for repeated identical texts to the same peer, account-ban case studies.

4. ORIGINALITY VERDICT. Given 1-3: is the founder an original ideologue of messenger delete-and-resend commitment-collection, or is it documented prior art? Give a clear verdict: (a) the cadence idea, (b) the "same text, no new words" principle, (c) the delete-and-resend mechanic — for each: known/rare/novel, with best evidence found.

5. PSYCHOLOGY & ETIQUETTE EVIDENCE. What does research say about persistence vs. annoyance: mere-exposure effect, Zeigarnik effect for unfinished commitments, reciprocity/consistency (Cialdini commitment-consistency) applied to follow-ups; data on when persistent pinging FLIPS to negative (unsubscribes, blocks, brand damage). Any studies on "re-sending the same message verbatim" vs "adding new value each touch" — which wins on reply rate?

6. PRIORITIZATION UNDER CAPACITY. When a system can only send N touches/day (anti-ban limits: ~5/day per messenger account), how do engagement platforms prioritize WHO gets touched first? Lead scoring + cadence engines (Outreach/Salesloft prioritization, Apollo task queues, CRM "next best action" systems). Best practices for: staggering enrollment (max new enrollments/day), tier-based preemption (high-value lead bumps low-value out of today's window), and what they do with missed touches (shift the whole schedule vs skip).
