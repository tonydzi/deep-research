# Insight (DR DR26-08-29-MACANTON-02-0742): Deep Research scoping report: no topic specified — ranking of 5 candidate longevity/health topics by evidence strength

> Since the original DR request had no concrete topic, the report instead scopes and ranks five plausible longevity/health research topics (CRF/grip strength, metformin, CGM in non-diabetics, NAD precursors, SIBO/microbiome) by evidence maturity and decision value.

## Ключевые выводы
- Cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) and grip strength are the most evidence-mature longevity markers: 1-MET higher fitness ≈13% lower all-cause mortality and 15% lower CHD/CVD risk (2009 JAMA meta-analysis, 33 studies, 102,980 participants); a 122,007-person 2018 cohort found mortality lowest in 'elite' fitness group (HR 0.20 vs low fitness), no upper ceiling of benefit.
- Grip strength (PURE cohort, 17 countries, 139,691 people): each 5kg lower grip strength = 16% higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.16), 17% higher CV mortality, and outperformed systolic blood pressure as a mortality predictor.
- Metformin has strong diabetes-prevention evidence (DPPOS: ~18% lower diabetes incidence at ~10yr vs placebo, lifestyle intervention 34% lower) but NOT proven healthspan/geroprotection benefit in healthy adults — the flagship TAME trial (>3,000 adults 65-79, 14 sites, 6 years) is still described by AFAR as pre-launch/fundraising, not completed.
- Randomized studies show metformin can blunt exercise adaptations in older adults — impairs insulin sensitivity, CRF gains, and mitochondrial/muscle adaptation from aerobic and resistance training (a 2025 Lancet Healthy Longevity study confirmed blunted strength AND endurance gains in healthy older people).
- CGM in non-diabetic adults is a promising phenotyping tool but not outcome-validated: a 2026 Communications Medicine study of 8,025 non-diabetic adults found 3 latent features (mean, variance, autocorrelation) explain >80% of interindividual CGM variation and correlate with carotid intima-media thickness/hepatic steatosis, but clinical interpretation guidance for non-diabetics remains unestablished (2025 expert study).
- NAD precursors (NR/NMN) reliably raise blood NAD metabolites (NR-SAFE phase I: up to 5-fold increase, supports testing 3,000mg/day) but functional/clinical benefit is inconsistent — a 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis concluded evidence does NOT support NMN/NR for preserving muscle mass/function in adults over 60; a 2024 review of 8 RCTs (342 people) found no benefit on glucose/insulin/HbA1c/lipids.
- SIBO/microbiome interventions show narrow, guideline-anchored support: ACG 2020 guideline backs breath testing and antibiotics for symptomatic SIBO but mostly on 'very low'/'low' certainty evidence; rifaximin meta-analysis (32 trials, 1,331 patients) shows 70.8% ITT success but high heterogeneity, only 1 placebo-controlled trial.
- AGA 2024 guideline supports fecal microbiota-based therapy (FMT) only for selected recurrent C. difficile cases, and explicitly recommends AGAINST conventional FMT outside clinical trials for ulcerative colitis, Crohn's, pouchitis, and IBS — much narrower than online/marketing enthusiasm.
- Cross-topic pattern: official/society guidance is consistently narrower and more conservative than commercial/marketing narratives around each topic (AHA on CRF as vital sign vs. lab geroprotector hype; AGA on FMT vs. internet enthusiasm; ADA on CGM in diabetes only vs. blanket healthy-adult rollout).

## Рекомендации / решения
- Do not commission a generic 'anti-aging' deep research — pick ONE of the five scoped topics and lock a precise PICOS-style question (population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, study design) before the next DR pass.
- For highest-confidence, actionable healthspan guidance: prioritize CRF/VO2max and grip strength as the next full report — cleanest measurement logic, lowest intervention ambiguity.
- For deciding whether metformin is worth using as a geroprotector: run a focused review specifically on metformin-in-active-non-diabetic-adults, explicitly modeling exercise status as an effect modifier (since exercise-interference data may flip the risk-benefit calculus).
- For N-of-1 metabolic self-experimentation: use CGM-in-non-diabetics review to separate validated signal (mean/variance/autocorrelation) from consumer overinterpretation.
- For supplement-claim evaluation: commission NR/NMN functional-outcomes review, splitting PK/PD (biomarker) outcomes from actual functional/disease-modifying endpoints.
- For GI/microbiome decisions: anchor on ACG/AGA guidelines first, keep recurrent C. difficile, IBS, IBD, and SIBO analytically separate — guideline support is not interchangeable across them.
