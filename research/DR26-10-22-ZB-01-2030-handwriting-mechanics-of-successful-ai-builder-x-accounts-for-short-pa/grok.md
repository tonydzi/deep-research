E. What separates a performing pain post from a flat one

From the posts, not from a slogan:

Specificity. Post 1 has "27% cache miss rate" and "50%". Post 8 has "18gb" and "4 simulators". Post 6 has "$10k MRR" and "3 weeks". Post 16 has "5.5 miles", "14.8mph", "45 mins". Post 23 has "$0.14" against "$2.21". Post 7 has "90 days" and "double the amount". The flat retrieved proverb has none of that.

Stakes the reader can lose too. Money (cache bill, dispute fee, churn), a lockout (post 17), a thing that will ship broken, a security flaw (post 9). @geoffreylitt's screen-recording bug is specific and honest and got 15 likes. Specificity without an audience, or without a stake anyone else shares, stays small.

Admission, said once. "it's my mistake", "welp", "lmao", "Skill issue", "yes i know my churn is a very big problem". The admission is the incident, not a paragraph about growth.

A time, when it is the fact. "twice this week" on the rejected PSA, "for a few weeks", "after 45 mins", "tomorrow will be nerfed". A timestamp is useful when it is part of the break. It is not a dateline.

No moral. The performing short posts stop. They do not tell the reader what kind of person succeeds.

Studies, with their limits:

LigoSocial, 26 Sep 2026, 1,209 posts from 15 build-in-public accounts, 2024 through Sep 2026, each post scored against that author's own median. The sample is their top-liked posts, so rates are "among better posts", not the odds for a random post. 43 of 1,209 (3.6%) hit 10x likes. 110 of 1,209 (9.1%) hit 5x. The two biggest multiples were personal mishaps (a tax seizure; "I accidentally deleted a browser"), not product posts. On 1,188 posts with at least 1,000 views, text-only median engagement rate was 1.60%, image 1.39%, video 1.28%. Video had the highest bookmarks per view, 0.39%. By engagement rate, 140–280 characters was best at 1.54%, then 60–140 at 1.38%, under 60 at 1.24%, over 280 at 1.16%. Over 280 had the highest median multiple versus the author's own baseline, 1.36x. 10x posts opened in lowercase 12% of the time, against 27% for the rest. A dollar sign was 26% against 21%. A bare number as the opener was 0% of outliers against 4% of the rest. Save-lists: 74 posts, 1.66x median multiple, bookmark rate 1.84% of views. https://ligosocial.com/research/x-build-in-public-outliers

ClimbX, 3 Jun 2026, 9,556 posts from 185 creators, a hit defined as that author's own top 25%. Everyday hit rates: 12 words or fewer 21.3%; 26–50 words 29.1%; 51–100 words 30.4% with a 17.9% flop rate. Goal-share hit rate 50% (about 1% of posts). Building in public 41.5%. Hot take 34.1%. Story 29% on 1,774 posts. Plain one-liner 18.2% hit, 34% flop. A specific number 1.21x. An @mention 1.26x. A URL 0.91x. Hashtags 1.21x on everyday posts. On 372 breakout posts, 13–25 words were over-represented, bullets 1.42x, and only 3 of 372 had a hashtag. Questions were the only breakout format whose replies outnumbered likes (reply-to-like 1.06). These are correlations. Goal-share is a small cell. https://climbx.so/blog/what-works-on-twitter-9556-tweets

The two studies disagree on hashtags and on images, and they agree on one thing that matches this corpus: a concrete detail beats a one-liner, and the freak hit is often a personal mess rather than a product announcement. Ligo's length result is the one that matches the brief. ClimbX's "13–25 words for a breakout" is shorter than 150–250 characters. Both can be true: short for a spike, 140–280 for a steady rate.

The public scorer, as a reason replies matter more than likes. Evan Long, published 15 Oct 2026, line-cited xai-org/x-algorithm commit 17c96bf (committed 14 Oct 2026): a like weight of 0.66, a reply weight of 5.0, a copy-link share weight of 20.0, a share weight of 2.0, a DM-share weight of 5.0, a report weight of −234, a mute weight of −58.8. Weights multiply predicted probabilities, not raw counts. The 2023 twitter/the-algorithm-ml weights he cites were reply 13.5 and author-engaged reply 75; the 2026 file has no separate author-reply weight, and `focal_author_reply` is still a model feature. Age filter drops posts older than 48 hours. Cold-start parameters he cites: impression threshold 200, follower cap 50,000, max post age 7,200 seconds. The README, as he quotes it, says experiments can override the defaults and a cron sets those defaults to the primary production values. Bookmarks do not appear in the weights he lists. This pass did not render `param.rs` itself; the GitHub blob failed in the earlier part of the run, and this part used his line-cited article. https://evanlong.me/en/articles/x-algorithm-open-source and https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/17c96bf346df3d62b90f1bb01830d432dba74485/home-mixer/params/param.rs

That lines up with the corpus. Post 9 has huge likes and comparatively few replies. Post 1, post 7, post 14, and post 33 have the reply counts a 5.0 reply weight would rather see. Post 27's 2,776 bookmarks are a creator-side signal the public weight list does not name.

F. Anti-patterns that read as corporate or as AI in this niche

Taken from posts retrieved this pass, not from a style guide:

The symmetric proverb. @dannypostma, 27 Jan 2025, 162 characters, likes 210, replies 26, bookmarks 44, views 19,947: "The difference between successful and failed entrepreneurs is that the successful one puts their personal opinions aside and listens to what customers truly want." https://x.com/dannypostma/status/1883675914774675681 Parallel nouns, a moral, no incident, no number.

The triple cadence. @asmartbear, 12 Sep 2026, likes 33: three sentences each starting "It's", each restating "admit a mistake". https://x.com/asmartbear/status/2098777152246186422 That shape is what a model writes when asked for wisdom.

The labeled lesson. Post 13 opens "Agentic AI mistake to avoid:" and still works because the next sentence is a mechanism. The label alone is the tell. "Lessons learned", numbered takeaways, and "here is the framework" were the long posts, not the short ones. @tibo_maker's pricing thread (22 Aug 2026) is eight numbered points and a "so do this today". It got 92 replies and 615 bookmarks, so a list can perform; it is not the handwriting the brief asked for, and it is far outside 280 characters. https://x.com/tibo_maker/status/2091095568005116164

The bolted closer. "subscribe below" on his failed-relaunch post. https://x.com/tibo_maker/status/2087526893348061477 Post 5 grows a reply that is an auto-plug of five products. The pain post itself has no closer; the plug is a second post.

A question is not automatically the tell. ClimbX measured questions as the reply format. @tibo_maker's fried-brain post ends "anyone else feeling this?" and has 1,722 replies. The failure mode is a moral question ("what kind of founder will you be?"), not a bare "anyone else".

Polish. Straight apostrophes and a final period are normal in the engineering hand (posts 1, 17, 19, 28). What reads as generated is every sentence the same length, a colon list inside 200 characters, emoji used as bullets, and a hashtag. This corpus has zero hashtags. ClimbX found hashtags a mild everyday positive and almost absent among breakouts, so leaving them off matches the posts that travel, not a 2026 law.

G. Ten-line recipe, usable as a system prompt

Write one X post from a voice note about something that broke. 150–250 characters. One incident, one real number, first person, then stop. Open on the broken fact or mid-thought; lowercase is fine; do not open with "Lessons learned", "So,", or a bare number. Name the thing that failed in the operator's words. If they said welp, my mistake, or skill issue, keep that once. Do not add a moral, a framework, or a closer. End on the complaint, the number, or a shrug. One blank line or none. No hashtags, no bullet list, no bold, no symmetric pair of sentences. One emoji only if the note had one, at the end. If a link exists, it goes in the first reply, not in the post. If the note is a proverb about success, do not post it.
