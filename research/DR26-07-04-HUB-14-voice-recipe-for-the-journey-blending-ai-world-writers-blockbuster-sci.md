# Insight (DR DR26-07-04-HUB-14): Voice recipe for 'The Journey': blending AI-world writers + blockbuster sci-fi authors for Tony/Mike duet

> How should the book 'The Journey' blend reputable AI-world writers (Karpathy, Graham, Willison, Swyx, patio11, Altman/Amodei) and blockbuster sci-fi authors (Ghost in the Shell, Matrix, Blade Runner, Chiang, Adams, Scalzi, Weir, Stephenson, Gibson) into a credible, funny, page-turning two-narrator voice (Tony/Mike)?

## Ключевые выводы
- Best version of the book is 'blockbuster narrative nonfiction wearing a sci-fi exoskeleton', not a tech memoir: Tony carries embodied scene momentum, Mike supplies calm analytical reframes.
- Six AI-world voices to steal, each with a distinct transferable move: Karpathy (rename a phenomenon into one clean first-principles analogy — 'Software 2.0'), Paul Graham (compressed plain-spoken aphorism, functions as house-style discipline not a character), Simon Willison (enthusiasm about capabilities balanced by a bright line around what breaks), Swyx (energetic category-creation naming, launch energy), Patrick McKenzie/patio11 (reframe absurd institutional behavior as legible tradeoffs with one funny line), Sam Altman/Dario Amodei (calm civilizational zoom-out, used sparingly at chapter turns only).
- Nine sci-fi/pop-culture DNA sources to borrow devices from without becoming fan fiction: Ghost in the Shell (cool precision on unstable identity), The Matrix (ontological reframe that retroactively recolors prior chapters), Blade Runner/Philip K. Dick (noir mood in service of empathy), Ted Chiang (moral clarity about second-order effects), Douglas Adams (engineered, not random, deadpan absurdity), John Scalzi (dialogue-driven speed, 'start late, get to the point'), Andy Weir (technical problem-solving as scene pressure), Neal Stephenson (big-concept riffing only in controlled doses), William Gibson (compressed atmospheric metaphor).
- Concrete mixing formula given: Tony = 30% Scalzi + 20% Weir + 15% Adams + 15% Blade Runner/Gibson + 10% Matrix + 10% Chiang; Mike = 25% Karpathy + 20% Willison + 20% patio11 + 15% Paul Graham + 10% Altman/Amodei + 10% Ghost in the Shell.
- House-style operating rules: default narrative ratio ~65% Tony / 35% Mike per chapter; repeating paragraph pattern (concrete resistance → one explanatory frame → one joke/image → stakes ratchet → changed state); one metaphor per explanatory unit; never explain before the scene earns it.
- Eight-technique humor engine (deadpan understatement, comic precision via one over-specific detail, absurd-but-logical AI literalism, competent narrator vs absurd situation, self-deprecation that preserves competence, running gags/callbacks, status flips, explanatory punchline) — jokes must target confusion/ego/jargon/institutional absurdity, never grief or unmetabolized humiliation.
- Credibility markers for 'reputable AI writer' tone: name the mechanism instead of gesturing at magic, state reversible claims (what it can/can't do), admit uncertainty, ground grand claims in a concrete noun/operational detail/cost.
- Cross-vendor convergence: 3 independent reports (ChatGPT + 2 in a Grok session) collected the same day landed on essentially the same voice roster, humor-engine structure, and Tony/Mike mixing approach — treated as corroboration.
- Unresolved identity puzzle: the 'Шилд/Shild' reference is ambiguous between John Scalzi (ChatGPT's inference, based on accessible/funny/commercial SF) and Greg Egan's 'Schild's Ladder' (Grok's first-pass phonetic guess, hard-SF identity precision) — both folded into VOICE-RECIPE.md pending Anton's one-word confirmation.
