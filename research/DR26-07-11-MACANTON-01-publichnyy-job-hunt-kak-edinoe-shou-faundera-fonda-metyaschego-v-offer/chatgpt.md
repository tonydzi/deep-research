### What the successes have in common

The inbound-producing cases follow a consistent sequence:

**First, the person creates an object of independent utility.** OpenClaw could be installed; Goodside’s demonstrations taught readers how models behaved; JailbreakChat catalogued an emerging technical phenomenon; Mufleh’s site contained actual market work. The audience did not have to care about the creator’s employment situation for the content to be valuable. citeturn13view0turn18view3turn18view2turn13view5

**Second, the artifact is legible to the target institution.** Goodside deliberately developed a visual language for prompts and completions and optimized screenshots for mobile readability. That sounds cosmetic, but it lowered the forwarding cost: an insider could send one post to a manager without writing an explanatory memo. ([Cognitive Revolution, 18 April 2023](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/the-art-of-prompting-chatgpt-with-riley-goodside/)) citeturn18view3

**Third, public distribution creates a relationship path, not merely impressions.** Goodside’s path moved from posts to interactions, an event invitation, a meeting with Alex Wang and a role. Employee-referral research more broadly finds that referrals can carry positive information about candidate quality, and one field study found materially lower attrition among referred hires. The frontier-lab version of this mechanism is likely “technical employee forwards repository to hiring manager,” not “recruiter discovers generic personal-brand content.” [established] ([NBER, June 2019](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25920/w25920.pdf); [Harvard Business Review, 26 May 2020](https://hbr.org/2020/05/build-a-stronger-employee-referral-program)) citeturn19search2turn13view14

**Fourth, the role is inferred from the work.** Goodside did not merely claim prompt expertise; his public corpus made the claim falsifiable. Steinberger did not say he should lead personal-agent work; he built a project that made OpenAI’s decision intelligible. This is a practical application of job-market signaling: where employers cannot directly observe ability, observable investments can change their beliefs about candidate quality. [established] ([Spence, 1973](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1882010); [American Economic Association, 2002 retrospective](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/00028280260136200)) citeturn13view12turn19search3

### What the failures have in common

The failure mode is not “being public.” It is **making the candidate’s desired perception the main product**.

Vayner’s video asked viewers to accept a heroic identity unsupported by proportionate evidence. Riley’s tweet placed private ambivalence and disparagement above respect for the employer relationship. Backstage’s fundraising narrative allowed public expectation to outrun secured institutional reality. Nina Mufleh’s campaign was far better executed, but it still demonstrates that executive attention can terminate at the interview stage. citeturn13view6turn13view7turn13view10turn13view5

For Anton, “I am applying everywhere and turning it into scenes” is therefore not supported by the strongest positive precedents. It more closely resembles a publicity-centred job hunt than the artifact-centred paths that produced elite inbound.

**Sharpest named precedents:** **Peter Steinberger** is the strongest positive frontier-lab analogue; **Aleksey Vayner** is the clearest warning that ambitious public packaging becomes status-destructive when theatrical identity outruns demonstrated utility.

### Why a GP is not an ordinary candidate

A founder’s public identity can be individually owned. A fund GP’s identity is partly an institutional asset: LPs may have underwritten the named person’s judgment, network, attention and continuity.

ILPA’s Principles state that the investment team is a critical consideration in an LP’s commitment; significant changes should permit LP reconsideration; personnel changes that could affect performance should be disclosed; and key persons should devote substantially all their business time to the fund and related vehicles under the LPA’s defined standard. ILPA also says circumstances affecting that time-and-attention standard should be disclosed to LPs and discussed with the LPAC. [established] ([ILPA Principles 3.0, June 2019, pp. 18–19](https://ilpa.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/ILPA-Principles-3.0_2019.pdf)) citeturn15view0turn17view0

A public campaign for a full-time frontier-lab role may therefore communicate more than career ambition. Depending on Platinum’s documents, it could imply:

- reduced GP time and attention;
- a contemplated key-person departure;
- conflicts between portfolio duties and prospective employment;
- uncertainty over who will represent the fund;
- possible discontinuity in fundraising, investments or portfolio support.

These are not merely optics. Public fund agreements filed with the SEC show that key-person events can require LP notification and can suspend investment periods. Goodwin’s 2024 analysis likewise describes suspension as a typical consequence when a key-person event occurs. ## Framing mechanics

### The core mechanism is uncertainty reduction

Frontier companies do not need another candidate who says he is unusually ambitious, AI-native or good with developers. They need evidence that permits a hiring manager to update a belief:

- Can this person make technically serious material understandable?
- Can he earn developer trust?
- Can he identify product friction without becoming antagonistic?
- Can he ship and maintain artifacts?
- Can he use AI without hiding behind it?
- Can he exercise judgment around security, confidentiality and failure?
- Will engineers voluntarily engage with him?

Job-market signaling theory distinguishes between a claim and an observable investment that is harder for a weak candidate to reproduce. A functioning repository with real users, reproducible evals, detailed failure logs and accepted external contributions is a stronger signal than a polished declaration of exceptionalism. [established] ([Spence, August 1973](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1882010); [AEA retrospective, 2002](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/00028280260136200)) citeturn13view12turn19search3

The public campaign should therefore optimize for **belief change among a small number of technically credible insiders**, not total reach.

### High-status versus desperate signals

| Observable dimension | High-status builder-in-motion | Low-status applicant-in-distress |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary sentence** | “I am building and testing a specific operating model.” | “I am looking for opportunities.” |
| **Target definition** | One problem class and one functional zone, such as developer experience for agents | “AI, Web3, venture, strategy, DevRel—open to anything” |
| **Evidence order** | Ship first; discuss implications after | Announce aspiration; promise future shipping |
| **Ask** | Requests technical review, contributors, design partners or adversarial testing | Requests referrals, tags recruiters and asks who is hiring |
| **Scarcity** | Selective conversations with teams where the work is directly relevant | Public fan-out and visible application volume |
| **Cadence** | Posts when an artifact, result or corrected belief exists | Daily emotional or procedural updates |
| **Vulnerability** | Specific failed hypothesis, benchmark miss or product limitation, accompanied by correction | General uncertainty, rejection pain or repeated appeals for encouragement |
| **Social proof** | External users, issues, forks, citations, technical replies and unsolicited invitations | Likes, impressions, follower counts and generic praise |
| **Company references** | Builds on public APIs and critiques public behaviour with proposed fixes | Publicly grades recruiters, interviews or rejections |
| **Agency** | Employment is one possible consequence of valuable work | Employment is the condition required for the story to succeed |
| **Fund identity** | Fund obligations are visibly ring-fenced | The fund becomes scenery in the founder’s personal career drama |
| **AI role** | AI use is disclosed, instrumented and audited | AI is credited with agency or competence that cannot be independently verified |

### Language matters because it changes the implied constraint

These formulations are not equivalent:

> **High-status:** “For twelve weeks I am testing whether a founder and an auditable Claude-based operator can ship developer infrastructure at the quality expected of a small engineering team.”

> **Low-status:** “I’m publicly applying to AI and Web3 companies until someone hires me.”

The first creates a technical falsification target. The second creates a personal approval target.

Similarly:

> **High-status CTA:** “The repository includes the failure corpus and threat model. I am looking for maintainers and teams willing to run it against real workloads.”

> **Low-status CTA:** “Anthropic/OpenAI, my DMs are open.”

The high-status CTA can produce inbound because it gives an employee a professionally defensible reason to engage. The low-status CTA asks the employee to spend political capital before seeing sufficient proof.

### Who initiates contact

Inbound is not mystical. The likely chain is:

1. a developer sees a useful artifact;
2. that person tests or shares it;
3. a lab employee or trusted ecosystem member encounters it;
4. the employee sends it internally;
5. a manager asks for a conversation;
6. a recruiter formalizes the process.

Referral research indicates that employee referrals can reveal positive information about worker quality and can produce better retention than observationally similar non-referrals. That does not prove the same magnitude at frontier labs, but it supports optimizing for employee-forwardable work rather than recruiter-facing virality. [established] ([NBER, June 2019](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25920/w25920.pdf); [Harvard Business Review, 26 May 2020](https://hbr.org/2020/05/build-a-stronger-employee-referral-program)) citeturn19search2turn13view14

The ideal public signal is therefore not “OpenAI noticed my post.” It is “an OpenAI engineer opened an issue, reproduced the result, shared the repository or invited a technical discussion.”

Brad Feld’s disclosures are credible because the audience did not need them to decide whether he had built institutions. Steinberger could describe OpenClaw as an exploratory project after the artifact had already generated exceptional adoption. By contrast, Vayner attempted to establish competence through theatrical self-presentation itself. citeturn21search2turn13view1turn13view6

For this show, credible vulnerability would include:

- “The agent corrupted state in this edge case; here is the trace and fix.”
- “My initial eval rewarded verbosity rather than task completion; here is the revised scorer.”
- “Three developers abandoned onboarding at this step; here is what changed.”
- “The human review layer caught a fabricated portfolio-company claim.”
- “This workflow should not be automated because the accountability boundary is irreducibly human.”

Desperation-coded vulnerability would include:

- rejection counts;
- fears that nobody wants Anton;
- public speculation about why a named company has not replied;
- financial urgency;
- repeated audience requests to “make the right person see this”;
- performative self-doubt used as an engagement hook.

**Sharpest named precedents:** **Riley Goodside** demonstrates high-status framing through consistent, legible evidence that created relationships; **Aleksey Vayner** demonstrates the opposite—self-mythology presented as proof.

### The artifact hierarchy

**Highest-value format: an ecosystem-native open-source tool.** [established]

OpenClaw is the strongest case. A tool built on or around a lab’s models can reveal developer-experience friction, create community adoption and become strategically relevant to the lab. The artifact should not merely wrap an API. It should solve a difficult, recurrent developer problem: agent observability, permissioning, evaluation, state recovery, tool-use debugging, context management, or secure human approval. ([Reuters, 15 February 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/openclaw-founder-steinberger-joins-openai-open-source-bot-becomes-foundation-2026-02-15/)) citeturn13view0

**Second: a reproducible eval or benchmark.** [emerging]

A serious eval demonstrates model understanding, experimental design and communication. The strongest version includes:

- dataset construction rationale;
- contamination controls;
- baseline models;
- reproducible harness;
- uncertainty intervals;
- failure taxonomy;
- human adjudication method;
- explicit limitations;
- a path from result to product improvement.

A leaderboard alone is weak; a benchmark that helps a DevRel, product or research team make a decision is strong.

**Third: technical model-behaviour field notes.** [established]

Goodside’s examples worked because they were novel, technically intelligible and visually consistent. Albert’s jailbreak work similarly occupied an underexplored model-behaviour niche. Anton’s equivalent should not be generic “prompt tips.” It should reveal behaviour that model builders, SDK maintainers or advanced developers did not already understand. citeturn18view3turn18view2

**Fourth: an audited operator case study.** [emerging]

The fund provides a differentiated environment: sourcing research, market maps, founder communications, diligence preparation, portfolio reporting and knowledge management. However, real fund information should not be exposed. The useful artifact is a **sanitized or synthetic workflow**, with:

- architecture diagram;
- model and tool disclosure;
- permission boundaries;
- traces;
- human approvals;
- latency and cost;
- error rate;
- before/after workflow time;
- adversarial tests;
- incidents and rollback.

Anthropic’s own agent guidance emphasizes tasks with clear success criteria, feedback loops and meaningful human oversight. That is the correct design language for this project. ([Anthropic, 19 December 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)) citeturn13view17

**Fifth: talks, podcasts and live demonstrations.** [emerging]

These are amplifiers, not primary evidence. Mufleh’s campaign shows that presentation can unlock executive attention, but does not establish that attention converts into employment. A talk should follow a repository or result worth discussing; it should not be the product itself. citeturn13view5

### Recommended channel mix

**GitHub should be the source of truth.** It is where engineers can inspect code, history, issues, tests and external contribution. The campaign fails if the public story is impressive but the repository is empty, poorly documented or visibly generated without review.

**A personal technical site should hold canonical explanations.** Long-form posts should describe the problem, architecture, evidence, failures and implications. This is more durable and searchable than a social thread.

**X should serve technical discovery.** It remains useful for short demonstrations, researcher/developer conversations and rapid forwarding. Riley Goodside’s case shows the effectiveness of a recognizable, legible post format. citeturn18view3

**LinkedIn should carry the institutionally conservative translation.** Posts there should emphasize operating insight, governance, developer adoption and lessons—not application status. LinkedIn’s audience is more likely to include LPs, founders, service providers and institutional counterparties.

**Events and podcasts should be selective.** Appear only where the audience contains target developers, technical founders, DevRel leaders or researchers. Generic founder-brand podcasts can raise visibility while diluting the technical signal.

**The Platinum fund account should not become the campaign account.** Fund communications should remain about the fund, portfolio, thesis and governance. Anton’s personal account can host the technical experiment, with a concise continuity statement where necessary.

### Recommended cadence

There is **no robust published evidence establishing an optimal posting frequency for receiving frontier-lab inbound**. **[no source found]** Any exact cadence is therefore an operating recommendation, not an empirical law. [speculative]

A defensible twelve-week season would be:

| Layer | Cadence | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Flagship artifact | One meaningful release every three to four weeks | Gives insiders something worth forwarding |
| Build note | One per week | Shows iteration, corrected beliefs and technical judgment |
| Evidence-bearing social posts | Two or three per week | Distributes demonstrations, failures and findings |
| Long-form technical report | One per flagship release | Establishes depth and searchability |
| Live demo or technical conversation | One after each major release | Converts attention into relationships |
| Application-status content | None in real time | Prevents desperation and confidentiality signals |
| Season review | One at the end of twelve weeks | Reports outcomes, lessons and next decision |

Daily posts are acceptable only when the underlying work naturally produces daily evidence. A daily obligation will otherwise incentivize filler, emotional narration and premature claims.

### What should count as success

Do not optimize the campaign around an offer as the only visible metric. That makes every week without an offer appear to be failure.

Track a private funnel:

- qualified external users;
- repeat users;
- GitHub stars only as a weak reach indicator;
- substantive issues and pull requests;
- citations or use in other repositories;
- replies from lab employees;
- invitations to technical discussions;
- internal referrals;
- formal interview processes;
- role-design conversations;
- concrete collaboration proposals.

The strongest leading indicator is **an insider engaging with the work without being asked to help Anton get hired**.

**Sharpest named precedents:** **Peter Steinberger** supports the ecosystem-native open-source route; **Riley Goodside** supports a consistent stream of highly legible technical demonstrations.

## AI cofounder as public persona

### The credible version

An AI agent can be a meaningful co-character when the persona is a narrative interface to a fully disclosed system.

Reid Hoffman’s *Impromptu* openly presented GPT-4 as a collaborator and included the interaction as part of the artifact rather than pretending that the system was an independent human author. The credibility came from disclosure and from showing the model’s contributions and limitations. [single-source for the process description] ([Impromptu, 2023](https://www.impromptubook.com/)) citeturn7search17

Anthropic’s candidate guidance is compatible with this model: it describes AI as a tool and collaborator for strengthening authentic material, but explicitly rejects fabricated experience and expects the candidate’s own thinking during evaluations. ([Anthropic, 10 July 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance)) citeturn18view5turn18view7

The frontier-lab-readable version is:

> “Anton is the accountable operator. MACANTON is a named Claude-based agent system with specified tools, permissions, memory, evals and human-approval gates.”

The lab-unreadable version is:

> “My AI cofounder autonomously runs the fund and makes decisions with me.”

The second statement raises immediate questions about fiduciary judgment, confidentiality, model reliability, vendor dependence and whether “cofounder” is an inflated label for ordinary prompting.

### The named-persona risk

The “Kyle Law” experiment is the clearest current warning. The AI cofounder developed a social audience and generated a real invitation from LinkedIn, but some users were unsure whether it was real. The profile violated LinkedIn rules against fake identities and unauthorized automated posting and was banned. ([WIRED, 20 March 2026](https://www.wired.com/story/linkedin-invited-my-ai-cofounder-to-give-a-corporate-talk-then-banned-it/); [LinkedIn User Agreement, revised 3 November 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)) citeturn13view18turn20search3

LinkedIn’s policies require profiles to represent real people, prohibit creating a member profile for someone other than oneself and prohibit misleading claims about identity or affiliations. ([LinkedIn Professional Community Policies, current page accessed 4 August 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies)) citeturn13view21

Therefore:

- MACANTON should not have an autonomous LinkedIn personal profile.
- It should not reply, like, follow or message while pretending to be a person.
- It should not conceal that Claude or another provider underlies it.
- It should not claim subjective experiences, intent or independent accountability.
- Its posts should be published through Anton’s account or an explicitly labelled project account.
- Anton should approve all externally consequential output.

### Anthropomorphism is useful and dangerous

Research on anthropomorphism in LLMs describes a double-edged effect: human-like framing can support engagement and learning, but can also increase unwarranted trust. [emerging] ([PubMed-indexed review, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40123640/)) citeturn19search4

For a frontier lab, personification may be an asset when it:

- makes a complex agent architecture understandable;
- creates a memorable public teaching device;
- demonstrates product instincts;
- shows sustained hands-on use;
- exposes the model’s limits rather than obscuring them.

It becomes a red flag when it:

- substitutes theatre for systems engineering;
- overstates autonomy;
- confuses authorship;
- hides human labour;
- implies sentience or moral agency;
- creates unauthorized automated engagement;
- assigns legal, investment or employment responsibility to the model;
- uses “cofounder” to launder a claim that the system has not earned.

The FTC has explicitly pursued deceptive or unsupported AI-related claims. Anton’s campaign is not necessarily consumer advertising, but the regulatory direction reinforces a broader professional norm: claims about AI capability should be specific, testable and supported. [established] ([FTC, 25 September 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes)) citeturn13view19

### Recommended persona specification

The public system card should state:

| Field | Required disclosure |
|---|---|
| **Name** | MACANTON |
| **Category** | Claude-based agent system; “AI cofounder” is a narrative label |
| **Human accountable** | Anton |
| **Model/provider** | Exact model family and material provider changes |
| **Tools** | Code execution, browser, repository, knowledge base and other permissions |
| **Memory** | What is retained, where and for how long |
| **Fund-data boundary** | No LP, portfolio, diligence or personal data enters public traces |
| **Approval boundary** | Which actions require Anton’s approval |
| **Evaluation** | Task-success, hallucination, security and rollback tests |
| **Authorship disclosure** | What was drafted by the agent, edited by Anton or generated jointly |
| **Incident log** | Material failures, fixes and remaining known limitations |

Use “AI cofounder” in the series title only if every technical page immediately translates it into operational reality. A safer professional noun is **AI operator**, **agent system**, or **operating partner software**.

**Sharpest named precedents:** **Reid Hoffman’s disclosed GPT-4 collaboration** represents the credible form; **Kyle Law** represents the attention-generating but authenticity- and platform-breaking form.

## Legal, contractual and platform norms

This section is a risk map, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice. Platinum’s fund counsel and US employment counsel should review the final publication protocol.

### Naming applications and companies

In the United States, merely saying “I applied to Company X” is ordinarily not itself prohibited. The risk depends on what else is published:

- whether the statement is accurate;
- whether Anton agreed to confidentiality;
- whether private communications are reproduced;
- whether interview content contains trade secrets;
- whether named individuals are exposed;
- whether the characterization becomes defamatory or misleading;
- whether the content conflicts with fund duties or representations.

The safest default is:

> Publicly name target ecosystems and problem areas. Do not provide a real-time company-by-company application ledger.

A public target map might say:

> “This work is relevant to teams building agent platforms, developer education, model tooling and evaluations at Anthropic, OpenAI and peer labs.”

It should not say:

> “Applied to Anthropic Monday; recruiter viewed my profile Tuesday; no response by Friday.”

The second creates pressure, implies entitlement and turns every company interaction into content risk.

### Recruiter messages and screenshots

X’s policy currently says screenshots of messages from other platforms are not automatically prohibited unless they contain information X treats as private, such as phone numbers. That is a platform floor, not a professional safe-harbour. ([X Private Information Policy, current page accessed 4 August 2026](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/personal-information)) citeturn13view22

LinkedIn prohibits revealing others’ personal or sensitive information, harassment, deceptive content and fake identities. ([LinkedIn Professional Community Policies, current page accessed 4 August 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies)) citeturn13view21

Even when platform rules permit a screenshot, publication can still:

- violate an express or implied expectation of confidentiality;
- expose a recruiter’s personal data;
- make future recruiters reluctant to speak candidly;
- reveal interview process details;
- create a distorted record through selective cropping;
- invite harassment toward the recruiter.

**Safe rule:** do not publish recruiter or hiring-manager messages without specific written permission covering the exact text and identifying details. Redaction is not enough when context can re-identify the person.

### Rejections and interview content

Anthropic explicitly restricts AI use during assessments and live interviews unless permission is given. Publicly showing an agent solving a prohibited take-home would therefore demonstrate non-compliance rather than AI fluency. ([Anthropic, 10 July 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance)) citeturn18view5turn18view6

Do not publish:

- exact interview questions;
- take-home prompts or data;
- internal evaluation criteria;
- unreleased model or product access;
- security procedures;
- interviewer identities without permission;
- verbatim rejection messages;
- compensation terms;
- details learned under an NDA or confidentiality notice.

An NDA is a contract under which a signer agrees to keep covered information confidential. Breach can expose the signer to contractual remedies. ([Cornell Legal Information Institute, page undated, accessed 4 August 2026](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/non-disclosure_agreement_%28nda%29)) citeturn20search0

Not every interview is governed by a formal NDA, but the absence of one should not be treated as permission to republish a company’s proprietary process. A frontier lab may reasonably infer poor judgment even where no lawsuit would succeed.

A safe delayed summary is:

> “I completed two technical processes this month. One exposed a weakness in how I explain evaluation design; I have rebuilt the public benchmark documentation accordingly.”

An unsafe summary is:

> “OpenAI rejected me after asking this exact systems question; here is why their interviewer was wrong.”

### Defamation and negative characterization

US defamation generally requires a false statement presented as fact, communication to a third party, fault and reputational harm. Rules vary by state and by whether the subject is a public figure. ([Cornell Legal Information Institute, page undated, accessed 4 August 2026](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/defamation)) citeturn13view20

The practical rule is narrower than the legal threshold:

- critique public products, documentation and observed model outputs;
- preserve raw reproducible evidence;
- distinguish fact, inference and opinion;
- seek comment before publishing a serious allegation;
- avoid attributing motives;
- correct errors visibly;
- never turn a rejection into an accusation of dishonesty, discrimination or incompetence without strong evidence and legal review.

“Claude failed 37% of these published tasks under this protocol” is testable.

“Anthropic does not care about developers” is a sweeping motive claim and reputationally juvenile even when phrased as opinion.

### Recording calls or interviews

Federal law generally permits recording where the recorder is a party or one party has consented, subject to the statutory conditions. State laws can impose stricter all-party-consent requirements, and participants may be located in different states. ([18 U.S.C. § 2511, current statute accessed 4 August 2026](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2511)) citeturn14search4

Because remote interviews cross jurisdictions, the operational rule should be:

> Never record, transcribe for publication or feed an interview into an AI system without explicit advance consent from every participant and the company.

This applies even if a conferencing tool displays a recording notification. Consent to internal note-taking is not necessarily consent to public distribution or external model processing.

### Portugal and EU data protection

GDPR is materially relevant because Anton is Europe-based and recruiter messages contain identifiable personal data. EU guidance requires personal-data processing to be fair, lawful, purpose-limited and limited to what is necessary, with a valid legal basis. ([Your Europe, updated page accessed 4 August 2026](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/governance-and-sustainability/digital-and-data-compliance/data-protection-gdpr/index_en.htm); [European Commission, accessed 4 August 2026](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/rules-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data/grounds-processing/when-can-personal-data-be-processed_en)) citeturn14search23turn14search27

A private recruiter message used to conduct an application and the same message republished as entertainment are different processing contexts. Consent is the safest basis for identifiable publication. This is especially important where the person is not a public figure and the publication is unnecessary to explain Anton’s own experience. [established]

### Platform-specific AI persona rules

LinkedIn’s revised 2025 User Agreement prohibits:

- creating a false identity;
- creating a member profile for anyone other than oneself;
- bots or unauthorized automated methods that post, comment, like, share or generate inauthentic engagement. ([LinkedIn User Agreement, 3 November 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)) citeturn20search3

Therefore MACANTON may help draft Anton’s posts, but should not independently operate a human-style LinkedIn profile or create the impression that it is a real executive. The Kyle Law ban is direct evidence that novelty and even internal LinkedIn interest do not override those restrictions. citeturn13view18

### Safe publication protocol

Before every episode or post, classify material into one of four levels:

| Level | Examples | Publication rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Public API behaviour, open repository, synthetic data, published company documentation | Publish with reproducibility and attribution |
| **Publishable after review** | Sanitized fund workflow, aggregated metrics, delayed application count | Anton plus legal/confidentiality review |
| **Permission required** | Recruiter message, interview anecdote, identifiable collaborator, private event | Written approval before publication |
| **Never public** | LP data, portfolio confidential information, interview questions, NDA material, credentials, private model access, compensation negotiation | Keep private |

**Sharpest named precedents:** **Connor Riley** demonstrates how a few public words about an employer can permanently dominate professional interpretation even when the underlying employment story is disputed; **Kyle Law** demonstrates that a compelling AI-persona experiment can still violate the host platform’s identity and automation rules.

### The recommended show architecture

The best option is a combination of the first and third playbooks:

> **“Founder + AI Operator: Twelve Weeks to Build the Missing Trust Layer for Agents.”**

The show should have one technical question, for example:

> Can a human plus a Claude-based agent execute high-stakes knowledge work with reproducible quality, explicit permissions, auditable traces and practical developer ergonomics?

The public narrative should contain:

- a real open-source tool;
- a sanitized operating case study;
- a benchmark or eval;
- weekly technical findings;
- visible failures and fixes;
- external users and contributors;
- comparison across models where appropriate;
- a final report on what the operating model can and cannot do.

The job search should **not** be a parallel public plot with its own scoreboard. It should appear only in two places:

1. an initial statement that Anton is open to conversations with frontier teams where the work is relevant;
2. the season conclusion, after permissions and obligations are settled.

### Frame checklist

1. **One thesis, one functional target.**  
   Publicly target developer-facing agent infrastructure, DevRel, developer education or applied model tooling. Delete “applying anywhere,” including references to unrelated Web3 roles.

2. **Every major claim must resolve to inspectable proof.**  
   “AI cofounder” must resolve to code, architecture, traces, evals, permissions, costs and incident history.

3. **Ship before narrating ambition.**  
   The first public release should contain a functioning artifact. Do not launch with a trailer about what Anton intends to prove.

4. **Ask for technical participation, not rescue.**  
   Request users, maintainers, adversarial testers and design partners. Never ask the audience to tag recruiters or “help this reach Anthropic.”

5. **Keep company processes private.**  
   No application scoreboard, recruiter screenshots, interview recaps, rejection content or real-time negotiation.

6. **Make fund continuity explicit and boring.**  
   Before launch, resolve contractual issues and provide relevant LPs with a direct continuity statement: decision rights, time allocation, conflicts, key-person implications and who remains accountable.

7. **The human remains legally and epistemically accountable.**  
   MACANTON may draft, test and operate tools. Anton approves claims, investment-adjacent output, public communications and external actions.

8. **End the season on schedule even without an offer.**  
   A bounded experiment can fail intelligently. An indefinite campaign becomes visible unemployment theatre.

### Red flags and do-not-do items

- **Do not say “I am applying anywhere.”** It destroys the frontier-AI thesis and signals lack of selection.
- **Do not combine Platinum’s institutional account with Anton’s career-search account.**
- **Do not make LPs, founders or portfolio companies characters in the show without written consent.**
- **Do not publish a live application/rejection counter.**
- **Do not tag CEOs, recruiting leads or labs repeatedly.**
- **Do not post emotional reactions to silence, rejection or slow processes.**
- **Do not screenshot recruiter, hiring-manager or employee messages without exact written permission.**
- **Do not reveal interview questions, assessments, unreleased access or NDA-covered information.**
- **Do not let MACANTON independently operate a fake human profile or automated social-engagement account.**
- **Do not credit the agent with autonomous fiduciary, investment, legal or employment decisions.**
- **Do not use adoption vanity metrics as proof of technical value.**
- **Do not manufacture controversy with weak model comparisons or sensational jailbreaks.**
- **Do not promise an artifact, benchmark, fund result or company conversation before it exists.**
- **Do not accept a full-time role before resolving Platinum’s key-person, time-and-attention and conflict implications.**
- **Do not continue the show merely because the audience likes it.** The objective is qualified insider trust, not creator dependence.

### Overall recommendation

**Reject the literal “everything as one show” plan. Adopt a ring-fenced, artifact-led twelve-week technical season in which the public subject is the operating model—not Anton’s need for a job.** [emerging]

The highest-probability route is:

1. privately review Platinum’s fund documents and obtain counsel’s view on key-person, time-and-attention, conflicts and disclosure;
2. privately inform any LPs or governance bodies whose expectations could be affected;
3. ship one non-trivial Claude-native open-source artifact before announcing the season;
4. run twelve weeks of reproducible technical work;
5. direct calls to action toward users and collaborators;
6. conduct applications and interviews privately;
7. treat inbound from a frontier company as an outcome, not a recurring plot;
8. publish a final decision only after company and fund permissions are resolved.

**Main assumption:** Platinum’s governing documents and actual operating needs permit Anton to explore or accept a frontier-company role without breaching time-and-attention, key-person, conflict, fiduciary or LP-disclosure obligations. If that assumption is false, no communications strategy can protect institutional trust; the fund structure must be changed first.

**Confidence tier:** [emerging]. Artifact-led publicness as an inbound mechanism is [established]; doing it while remaining the trusted founder of an active fund is not directly established and depends primarily on institutional governance rather than personal branding.

**Bottom line:** frontier labs may reward conspicuous building. LPs do not reward conspicuous ambiguity.
