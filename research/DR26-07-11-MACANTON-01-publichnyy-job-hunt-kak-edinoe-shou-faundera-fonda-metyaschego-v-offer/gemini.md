You cannot be a "candidate applying for a job"; you must be a "researcher publicly benchmarking a novel system."

By positioning your Claude-based AI cofounder as the entity being tested against the world's most rigorous technical hiring loops, you transform a vulnerable job search into a high-status engineering experiment. This protects Platinum’s credibility because the activity reads as extreme R&D, not employment desperation. The objective is to force an inbound DevRel or Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) offer by proving your multi-agent architecture in public. To execute this, you must reject traditional "performance-in-public" (vanity metrics, networking pleas) and adopt strict "workflow-in-public" (shipping technical artifacts, pull requests, and evaluation logs). Q3 — High-Status vs. Desperate: The Mechanics of Framing

Status signaling theory dictates that desperation is telegraphed by asking for attention, while high status is telegraphed by distributing value. The "Build in Public" meta has forked in 2026 into two distinct playbooks [6].

Low-Status (Desperate): "Performance-in-public." This includes tagging hiring managers, posting MRR screenshots, sharing generic rejection letters to complain about "ghosting," and using the vocabulary of a job seeker ("hire me," "open to work," "networking"). This signals that the market does not value the builder.

High-Status (Confident): "Workflow-in-public." This relies on sharing raw technical exhaust: terminal transcripts, evaluation metrics, and architectural decisions. The vocabulary is that of a peer researcher ("benchmarking," "evaluating," "hard forks").

The Tells (Disagreement Noted): While some brand strategists argue for total emotional vulnerability (sharing the pain of rejection), doing so as a VC founder violates the gravitas required for your primary role. The consensus among technical recruiters is that transparently sharing the technical failure of a take-home assignment is high-status, while sharing the emotional failure of a rejection is low-status [7]. Q4 — Formats & Cadence for LLM Inbound

To trigger an inbound offer for a DevRel or Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role in 2026, the artifact must be immediately useful to the lab's own developers.

Artifact Types: The highest ROI artifacts are Pull Requests to the labs' official repositories, multi-agent evaluation frameworks, and implementations of their published papers. For example, narrating the exact architectural decisions behind a submitted pull request (e.g., PR #784 to the official Anthropic Claude Cookbooks) is a near-perfect DevRel signal. It proves you already do the job.

Platform Mix: GitHub for the code. X (Twitter) for the micro-updates. Technical deep-dives published on developer-heavy platforms (Habr, VC.ru, or Substack) to capture long-form search intent.

Cadence: The 2026 workflow playbook requires a daily 30-minute window for "ship posts" (raw, lowercase, specific commits) and a weekly demo [6].

How Scouting Works Today: DevRel hiring managers (like Logan Kilpatrick's former teams) monitor GitHub forks of their own repositories and the #buildinpublic hashtag for developers who are pushing their API limits [2][7]. [established]

Q5 — AI Cofounder as a Public Persona

Presenting a custom AI agent as a cofounder is a high-risk, high-reward framing.

The Asset: It acts as a live demonstration of your systems-engineering capability. Integrating a 168,000-file personal knowledge vault (like your Obsidian graph) into the agent's memory banks proves a mastery of RAG and local-first data architecture that most candidates lack.

The Red Flag (Cringe Factor): Anthropomorphizing the tool too heavily (giving it human emotions, fake stock-photo headshots, or pretending it has independent agency) is widely mocked in frontier AI circles. The labs know exactly how the models work; they view extreme anthropomorphism as a misunderstanding of the technology. [established]

Precedent: Yohei Nakajima succeeded by keeping BabyAGI and his "GPT VC Associate" strictly framed as tools and automations, not sentient peers [4].

Q6 — Legal, ToS, and Norms of Documenting Applications

Documenting real hiring loops carries severe legal and reputational risks.

NDAs and Take-Home Tests: Frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind) heavily utilize proprietary take-home tests and internal research papers during the interview loop. You will be required to sign an NDA before receiving them [8]. Publishing the specific prompts, questions, or proprietary datasets provided by the lab is a breach of contract. [established]

Reputational Exposure: Sharing a company's confidential interview questions publicly will instantly blacklist you from the industry. AI labs prioritize alignment and security; leaking their hiring IP proves you are a security risk [9].

The Safe Path: You may legally state you are interviewing at a specific company (unless the NDA explicitly forbids it). You can share your own architecture and the tools you built to prepare for the interview, provided they do not reveal the lab's proprietary prompts.

Precedent: Interview NDAs are standard in tech. Breaching them via a public post on LinkedIn, even if not sued, results in permanent industry ostracization [8][9]. [established]
