B. The two metrics, kept apart

Metric 1, rumours. A niche is “full of fresh rich” because a forum, a broker, a conference floor, or a leaked deck says so. Legitimate as a lead. Confidence here is scored 1–5 on whether a named speaker, a filing, or a regulator stands behind it. In this run the following stayed rumours because no purchase ledger was opened: iGaming affiliates filling Richard Mille waitlists; OnlyFans agencies as a distinct luxury-buyer class; crypto market-makers as a counted Dubai villa cohort; dropshipping, nutra, MLM, forex/CFD shops, and prop-trading payouts as measured supercar demand. Confidence 2. They are leads, not facts.

Metric 2, purchase facts and semi-facts. A dated count or a named transaction, plus the industry only when the source actually states it. The cleanest fact series in this run is Dubai $10 million-plus home sales, 2010–2025, Knight Frank using REIDIN. The cleanest semi-facts are one brokerage’s nationality ranks (Betterhomes) and one brokerage’s crypto-settlement anecdotes (fam Properties). Neither is a market-wide industry share.

Hard numbers, third layer, are profit pools: GGR, export value, filings, VC rounds. A profit pool is not proof that the money was spent on a yacht.

H. Low-profile verticals: who they are, and the detection path that does not depend on a press profile

These businesses can throw off cash to a few hundred people without ever creating a billionaire UBS will name. That is why they are absent from the billionaire report and present in rumours. Legal status is a jurisdiction fact, not a moral one.

iGaming operators and affiliates. Legal and large in licensed US states, the UK, Italy, and now Brazil. Restricted or banned for real-money play in India from 2025–2026. Offshore books remain large: AGA’s $53.9 billion US illegal estimate, and Brazil’s own enforcement against offshore sites. The people are licence-holders, media buyers, affiliate managers, payment brokers, and odds traders. They do not need fame. Detection: gaming-licence registers (MGA, UK Gambling Commission, AGCO, SPA Brazil, state US lists), company registers in Malta, Cyprus, Curaçao, and the Isle of Man, and sponsor walls at ICE and SiGMA. Match director names to those registers, then to visa filings. False positive: a Cyprus company is often a shell.

Affiliate marketing and traffic arbitrage outside gaming. Same skills, different offer: nutra, lead-gen, consumer finance, app install. Detection is worse because there is often no licence. Card-scheme descriptors, ad-account bans, and Affiliate World speaker lists are the soft layer. Confidence that this group buys luxury: rumour, 2. Confidence that the cash-flow model exists: high, because the gaming affiliate leg is an observed part of a licensed industry.

OnlyFans agencies. The platform filing shows a tiny payroll and a huge creator payout. Agencies sit between the two and are not broken out. Detection: UK Companies House for agency vehicles, creator-management contracts that surface in disputes, and the lifetime count of 5,076 seven-figure creators as a ceiling on how many principals can exist. Most “agencies” will not be UHNW.

Crypto market-makers and OTC desks. Durable version of the ICO boom. They show up as liquidity providers, not as consumer brands. Detection: regulatory registrations where they exist, exchange equity caps tables, and on-chain market-maker labels. Henley’s 172,300 crypto millionaires mix these people with passive holders. The passive holder is not a vertical.

Prop trading and forex/CFD. Retail-broker revenue is a licensed pool in some countries and a banned promotion in others. This run did not open a current global CFD revenue figure reliable enough to print. Detection: CySEC, FCA, ASIC registers. Do not infer Dubai villas from a licence list.

Telegram and TON mini-apps. A TON Foundation Dune board shows 2025–2026 in-app gift trading and GameFi flows in the tens to hundreds of millions of dollars by month, which is real activity and not the same thing as retained profit. https://www.dune.com/ton_foundation/verticals The 2024 clicker-game wave minted some token wealth and a lot of zero. Treat as episodic. Detection: on-chain deployer and treasury wallets, not conference selfies.

Gulf contractors and sanctioned-trade intermediaries. One documented pattern is the energy trader who is already rich and becomes richer in a dislocation, plus the intermediary whose corporate person is in Cyprus or Dubai and whose boat is in the Mediterranean. The Subbotin yacht is a case study method: corporate record, citizenship, vessel flag, port call. It does not generalise to a share of the fleet.

Indian real-money gaming, now a closed pool onshore. The investigative question is where the displaced liquidity went: offshore apps, equity left in Dream Sports and peers, or consumption already completed in 2021–2024. The luxury tell, if it existed, would be in 2022–2024 purchases by founders whose companies then lost the India market in 2025. That sequence is a document request, not a number this run can invent.

I. Leading-indicator playbook for 2026 onward

Each line is a signal, what it actually measures, lag, cost, and the false positive that will waste a reporting cycle.

Dubai $10 million count, Knight Frank / REIDIN, quarterly. Measures arrival of money into one city’s housing, with a one-quarter lag. Cost is low once the release is on the site. False positive is high for industry: visas, the dirham peg, tax, and project launches move the same series. Use it as a where, then open a who-study.

Betterhomes or equivalent broker nationality board, quarterly. Measures one firm’s book. Lag one quarter. Cost is a relationship. False positive: that firm’s marketing focus. Never quote it as DLD.

DLD micro tape, price, off-plan share, community. Free, short lag. Measures speculation versus use. Does not measure profession. https://firswoodproperties.ae/insights/dld-transactions.html

Henley private-wealth migration, annual, model-based. Lag is the forecast year itself. Cost is the report. False positive: old money moving for tax. The 2025 UK outflow and UAE inflow are the template. Re-read the method note before quoting a headcount as fact. https://www.henleyglobal.com/publications/henley-private-wealth-migration-report-2025

UBS Billionaire Ambitions, annual, around December. Lag about one quarter after Q3 cutoff. Free press release. Measures the top of the top, so it will miss every laptop millionaire and catch industrial, software, and inheritance. A grey niche that never produces a billionaire can still produce a hundred people at $10–30 million. https://www.ubs.com/global/en/media/display-page-ndp/en-20251204-billionaire-ambitions-report-2025.html

Altrata World Ultra Wealth Report, annual, paid. Best current industry mix of the $30 million population. If financial services is still first, the “new vertical has taken over UHNW” claim is false at that tier. https://altrata.com/articles/key-findings-world-ultra-wealth-report-2026

Capgemini World Wealth Report, annual. Best headcount of new millionaires, and the most contaminated by stock-market beta. An AI rally will mint “millionaires” who are doctors with an index fund. Subtract the equity effect before calling it a vertical. https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/06_04_Capgemini-Press-release_World-Wealth-Report-2026.pdf

FHS Swiss watch exports, monthly, free. Lag weeks. Measures wholesale demand and stocking, including tariff front-running, as 2025 in the US showed. Not the end buyer. Pair with WatchCharts or the Morgan Stanley quarterly so retail and secondary are not confused. https://www.fhs.swiss/documents/statistics/eng/world-watchmaking-2025.pdf

WatchCharts overall index, continuous, paid for depth. Lag is short. In 2021–2022 it coincided with crypto and liquidity, then fell more than 35% by October 2023 while the UHNW headcount later rose. That history means a rising watch index is a risk-appetite tell. A falling index during a rich year falsifies “luxury prices equal new money.” https://thedialreport.com/watch-market-after-the-bubble/

Ferrari, Lamborghini, Rolls-Royce deliveries and personalisation, quarterly. Free in filings and the trade press. Lag one quarter. Measures capacity-constrained demand. Rolls-Royce’s age statistic is the only regular demographic. Ask the investor-relations line for region and for the share of first-time buyers. They rarely give industry. A waiting list that lengthens while deliveries fall is allocation, not a boom.

GAMA shipment report, annual, February, free. Lag about seven weeks. Measures factories, not owners. 854 jets in 2025 is the baseline. https://gama.aero/news-and-events/press-releases/gama-releases-2025-aircraft-shipment-and-billing-report/

VistaJet or WingX route notes, as echoed by Knight Frank. Annual in the Wealth Report, higher frequency if bought from the data firm. Measures where rich people fly, including charter. The under-45 first-time share is the early-age tell. False positive: a new route can be a subsidy or a sports season.

BOAT Global Order Book and SuperYacht Times nationality file, annual. Lag to the December publication. Partial paywall. Measures intent to build and passport of owner, not the business that made the money. Unit down, length up, is the current pattern and should be tracked as two series, not one. https://pressmare.it/en/media/press-mare/2026-10-23/yachtings-future-buyers-through-milanovics-lens-93686

Regulator GGR: AGA monthly commercial tracker, UK Gambling Commission, SPA Brazil, state US dashboards. Lag one to three months. This is the profit-pool early warning for gaming. It will light up a year or two before any honest luxury join, because operators get cash before they get a boat.

Company registers and licence lists. Cost is analyst time. Lag is the filing lag, often months. Method: new gaming licences, new Cyprus or Malta directors, then a negative-news and vessel search on those names. This is the actual early detector for low-profile money. Conferences are the weak version of the same list.

PitchBook or an equivalent for AI, quarterly, paid. Lag weeks. Measures capital, which leads consumption for founders only when a secondary or a tender happens. Track tender offers and employee share sales, not headline round size, if the question is luxury.

Golden Visa and CBI statistics where governments publish them. UAE applications are mostly not public at profession level. Portugal’s property route is closed, so a Lisbon purchase after October 2023 is not a visa trade. https://www.amirismail.com/portugal-golden-visa-after-real-estate-the-routes-that-are-still-open/ False positive: a visa buyer is often realising old wealth.

The practical stack, in order, each quarter. Read GGR dashboards and PitchBook tenders for new cash. Read FHS and WatchCharts for whether risk appetite is being spent on watches. Read the Dubai $10 million count for whether a city is absorbing it. Read broker nationality only as a hypothesis. Test that hypothesis in a register, not in a quote. Once a year, check whether Altrata’s industry order has changed. If financial services is still first, do not write “the new rich have taken over.”

J. Where the money is in 2025–2026, ranked by evidence

1. US and Asian public equity, with technology and semiconductors as the earnings engine. Evidence: Capgemini, +736,000 US millionaires, +436,000 in Japan, HNWI wealth +8.7% to $98.3 trillion. Recipients: existing shareholders, employees with listed stock, fund holders. Barrier: capital already in the market. This is the volume leader and the easiest to misread as a new niche.

2. Frontier AI labs and the pick-and-shovel layer around them. Evidence: PitchBook, $254.4 billion in 2025 and $255.5 billion in Q1 2026, three rounds of $122 billion, $30 billion, and $20 billion; UBS tech billionaire wealth +23.8%, six US names +$171 billion. Recipients: founders and early investors on paper, a thin set of employees if a tender clears, Nvidia-class suppliers’ shareholders. Barrier: extreme. Not a laptop vertical in 2026. The laptop layer is application startups, where PitchBook says deal counts are down and cheque sizes are up, so fewer winners.

3. Industrial, infrastructure, and LNG developers. Evidence: UBS, industrial wealth +27.1% to $1.7 trillion, fastest sector, with named new billionaires in infrastructure and LNG. Recipients: sponsors, pre-IPO holders, project equity. Barrier: balance sheet and permits.

4. Licensed sports betting and US iGaming. Evidence: AGA, $16.89 billion and $10.73 billion of GGR, both growing above 20%. Recipients: operator equity, senior trading and marketing staff, affiliates with state deals, media rights holders. Barrier: licence, tax, and ad rules. The illegal parallel pool at an estimated $53.9 billion still pays people who will not appear in a licence list.

5. Brazil’s newly licensed betting market. Evidence: about $7 billion GGR, 79 firms, year one. Recipients: licence-holders who paid BRL 30 million each, their affiliates, and a large illegal remainder the regulator is still cutting off. Barrier: falling, relative to a closed market, but tax is rising toward 15% and enforcement is active. This is the clearest 2025 “new legal cash pool” in the set.

6. Inheritance. Evidence: $297.8 billion to 91 heirs in one year. Recipients: children and spouses of existing billionaires. Barrier: birth. They buy the same objects as new money and will contaminate every luxury series.

7. The UAE as a landing zone, not a producer. Evidence: Henley forecast of 9,800 net millionaire arrivals and about $63 billion of investable wealth in 2025; Dubai’s 500 sales over $10 million. Recipients: developers, brokers, anyone already rich enough to move. The money was usually made somewhere else.

8. Global online gambling outside the US and Brazil. Evidence: H2, online at 41% of $712 billion in 2024 and still taking share. Recipients: the same operator and affiliate roles, in more jurisdictions. Mature, not new, still cash-heavy.

9. OnlyFans and agencies. Evidence: $7.8 billion of fan payments, $715 million pre-tax profit, 5,076 lifetime creator millionaires, growth about 10%. Recipients: the owner, a few dozen staff, the far tail of creators, and an unmeasured agency layer. Not where acceleration is.

10. Crypto holders and a smaller set of infrastructure people. Evidence: 2024 millionaire count; UBS noting a 2025 crypto rebound inside financial-services billionaire wealth; Justin Sun named. Recipients: large holders, exchange and protocol equity, market-makers. Memecoin launchpads remain episodic. Barrier to the durable seats is capital and a regulatory perimeter; barrier to the lottery seats is none, and the expected value is poor.

11. GLP-1 manufacturers. Evidence: Novo’s kroner revenue lines. Recipients: shareholders. Telehealth partners are a real channel as of April 2025 and an unproven millionaire factory.

12. Superyacht and jet consumption itself. Evidence says the top end spent in 2025 and that first-time jet users are younger. Evidence does not say a new industry filled the order book. Unit orders are the warning light.

Falling or shut, which is also a location of money leaving. India onshore real-money gaming after May 2026. Swiss watch volumes for two years. Rolls-Royce unit sales since the 2023 peak. Russian share of the large-yacht fleet since 2022. UK as a residence for millionaires, on Henley’s forecast. These are exits. Exits still produce one last round of purchases in the destination city. Dubai 2022–2023 is the case.

L. Strongest counter-argument, and what would falsify the thesis

The thesis says new operating niches reveal themselves in conspicuous purchases before they reveal themselves in statistics. The strongest counter-evidence from this run is that the purchase series and the wealth series have already diverged in ways a pure new-money story cannot absorb.

Ultra-wealthy headcount rose through 2024 and 2025 on both Knight Frank’s and Altrata’s models, and billionaire wealth hit a record, while the watch secondary market stayed well below its March 2022 peak, Swiss watch exports fell for two years, Rolls-Royce deliveries fell after 2023, and the superyacht order book fell in units. If fresh fortunes automatically bought the objects, those series would not fall together while the headcount rose. Liquidity, interest rates, and taste did the moving. The 2022 watch peak lined up with crypto and with easy money, then broke, which is a regime fact.

Where purchases did surge, the documented causes are often policy. Dubai’s $10 million count rose from 30 to 500 between 2020 and 2025 while the broker tape rotated from a COVID-era European and Indian mix, to Russians in 2022, back toward Indians and then, on a weaker 2025 brokerage composite, Chinese, Saudi, and British tax movers. Knight Frank now describes end-users and residency, not flippers. Henley describes tax, geopolitics, and a Golden Visa. A visa market will absorb old money, frightened money, and new money and issue the same title deed.

At the only tier where industry is actually counted, the new fortunes of 2025 are industrial, infrastructure, LNG, software, genetics, and inheritance. Altrata says financial services is still the main UHNW industry in every major region. The laptop niches can be rich and still be invisible at that tier. Invisibility is not the same as dominance.

The thesis would be in trouble, and should be dropped for a given niche, if a proper join showed that niche’s share of verified $10 million buyers, new yacht contracts, and new jet owners was no higher than its share of the existing UHNW population, after controlling for nationality and for the visa rule in force that year. It would be confirmed for that niche if the same join showed a stable over-index, for example several times the base-rate share, in more than one city and more than one asset, with the over-index appearing before the niche’s revenues were common knowledge. One broker’s anecdote, one spring of watch premia, or one record penthouse with an unpublished buyer does not meet that test.

A second falsifier is already half-visible. ICO funds raised exploded in 2017–2018 while Dubai $10 million sales fell to 15. Either the winners bought elsewhere, or they bought later, or most of the “raised” money was not spendable wealth. Any method that treats a fundraising boom as a same-year luxury boom will false-positive. The right lag has to be measured. It is not yet measured, because the buyer file does not exist in public.
